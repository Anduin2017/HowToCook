# Making Caramel Color

Principle: sugar melts at high temperature, and takes on different properties depending on how long it is heated.

Example: making 200ml of caramel color

## Water Method

1. Use a pan **with a curved bottom**, or one whose **flat bottom takes up less than 1/3 of its area**
2. Add 8g of rock sugar, granulated sugar or soft white sugar to the pan. Rock sugar is best. If you want the caramel color to taste noticeably sweeter, add about 2g more
3. Add 50ml of 70&deg;C hot water to the pan
4. Put the pan on the stove
5. If using a gas stove, turn the heat from high down to the lowest setting so that the flame doesn't extend beyond the area of the water surface. If using an induction cooker, set it to the lowest power and be ready to switch it off at any time
6. Keep stirring the sugar-water mixture with a spatula. If using rock sugar, you can tap it gently with the back or edge of the spatula to crush it and speed up melting. If during this process the sugar starts to change color before it has fully melted, add more hot water to bring it back up to 50ml
7. Once all the sugar has melted, the mixture becomes thick and its color is clear to whitish, like mica. Keep stirring at this point
8. Keep stirring; the mixture gradually turns white
9. Keep stirring; the mixture starts to show a light brown color
10. Keep stirring; the brown color becomes very obvious
11. Keep stirring; the mixture is brown and fine, dense bubbles appear
12. Keep stirring; the mixture is brown with fine, dense bubbles, and larger bubbles appear among the small ones
13. Add 150ml of 70&deg;C hot water to the pan. The liquid in the pan is now a deep reddish brown, clear and transparent, with a light caramel aroma
14. Pour into a bowl. You now have 200ml of caramel color

> [!Note]
> Note on step 13:  
>Do not use cold water instead of 70&deg;C hot water, because the low temperature will make the sugar solidify and stick to the pan

## Oil Method (Recommended)

1. Same as the water method
2. Same as the water method
3. Add 10ml of room-temperature cooking oil to the pan
4. Same as the water method, except the sugar-water mixture becomes a sugar-oil mixture, and at the end add 190ml of hot water. The rest is the same

## Syrup Stages and Their Uses

|Stage|How to make|Use (example)|
|:--:|:--:|:--:|
|Syrup not fully melted|Water (oil) method step 8|Frosted hawthorn|
|Fully melted syrup|Water (oil) method step 9|Candied hawthorn skewers (tanghulu)|
|Caramel|Water (oil) method step 11, then let cool naturally|Caramel|
|Caramel color|Water (oil) method step 13|Caramel color|

* When using caramel color to cook small pieces of food such as red-braised pork, you can add the ingredients directly before step 13 and stir-fry quickly until there is no obvious liquid pooled on the bottom of the pan, then add water. This colors the food faster
