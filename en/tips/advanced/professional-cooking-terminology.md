# Professional Cooking Terminology

Cooking is like learning to program: first you need to know the technical terms, just like learning basic syntax. Only by combining these terms can you complete a dish.

Each term below gives the Chinese character, its pinyin, and an English explanation.

【炒 (chǎo) Stir-frying】 Anciently written "煼 (chǎo)", it is one of the most basic cooking methods today: cutting food into small pieces, tossing it rapidly in an iron wok (huò) of very hot oil until cooked, then adding seasonings.

【炝 (qiàng) Flash-dressing】 After the food is cut, it is "blanched" or "soaked" in boiling water or hot oil, then tossed in a hot wok with dried chilies and Sichuan pepper oil.

【炊 (chuī) Cooking by steaming or boiling】 Cooking food through steaming, boiling and similar methods. Often seen in the names of Chaozhou (Teochew) dishes.

【煮 (zhǔ) Boiling】 One of the simplest cooking methods: cooking food in a wok with an appropriate amount of boiling water or broth plus seasonings.

【煎 (jiān) Pan-frying】 Heat an iron wok, add a little raw oil, then lay the food flat against the wok and use low heat and hot oil to turn its surface golden and cook it through.

【爆 (bào) Quick-frying】 Using a hot wok and hot oil, splash in a prepared sauce or broth so that small pieces of food cook quickly and take on aroma.

【炸 (zhá) Deep-frying】 Anciently written "煠 (zhá)", one of the most common cooking methods: putting food into a large amount of hot oil until it is cooked and crisp.

【烚 (xiá) Simmering tough food】 Anciently written "煠": using a large amount of boiling water over the fire to cook tough-textured food until soft and done.

【滚 (gǔn) Rolling boil】 Using the churning of a large amount of boiling water to draw off off-flavors (yǔ) from food.

【汆 (cuān) Quick-boiling】 A northern Chinese cooking term, anciently "川"; similar to "渌 (lù)" in Cantonese cooking: food shaped into balls or slices is cooked in boiling water, scooped into a bowl, then topped with boiling broth.

【灼 (zhuó) Scalding】 Written "焯 (chāo)" in the north: food cut into thin slices is cooked quickly in boiling water, then dipped (zhàn) in sauce and eaten.

【炟 (dá) Soft-boiling greens】 Cooking vegetables thoroughly over low heat in boiling water with lye water (jiǎn) or raw oil added, so they come out soft yet stay bright green.

【涮 (shuàn) Rinse-cooking】 A northern Chinese cooking term: putting thinly sliced food into a spicy broth until cooked, then dipping it in sauce to eat.

【煀 (kuò) Aromatic smothering】 Anciently written "爩 (yù)": putting food directly into a wok or clay pot, adding lots of aromatics like ginger and scallion, covering, and using the aromatics to perfume and cook the food.

【焗 (jú) Salt-baking】 Cooking food sealed in foil or paper using hot coarse salt, in a closed environment.

【焖 (mèn) Covered braising】 A northern cooking method: putting tough food into a wok, adding an appropriate amount of broth, covering, and cooking over gentle heat until soft and done.

【炆 (wén) Southern braising】 Similar to the northern method "烧", hence the saying "southern 炆, northern 烧": putting tough food into a wok with an appropriate amount of broth and cooking over gentle heat until soft and done.

【烩 (huì) Mixed stewing】 Cooking various meats and vegetables together in an appropriate amount of broth.

【蒸 (zhēng) Steaming】 Cooking food with the heat of steam.

【炖 (dùn) Double-boiling / stewing】 Food with water or broth is placed in a covered container, covered, then cooked with the heat of steam to produce a broth. In northern cuisines it refers to cooking food until soft with lots of broth and gentle heat.

【扣 (kòu) Bowl-steaming and inverting】 After seasoning and pre-processing, food is neatly arranged in a bowl and steamed, then the main ingredient is turned out onto a plate and topped with a glossy sauce thickened from its own juices.

【煲 (bāo) Slow-simmering】 Putting food in a large amount of water and simmering slowly over the fire until cooked, producing a broth.

【熬 (áo) Reducing】 Using low heat over a long time to dissolve the savory flavor of meat into a broth and concentrate it.

【靠 (kào) Flavor-infusing】 Using strongly flavored ingredients and a rich broth, over gentle heat and a long time, to infuse savory flavor into another bland main ingredient.

【煨 (wēi) Simmering / infusing】 Anciently, cooking by burying in hot ashes. Today it means using ginger, scallion and broth to season food and remove its own off-flavors. In northern cuisines it also means sealing food and broth in a clay jar and cooking it over gentle heat.

【焐 (wù) Clay-baking】 Takes over the ancient meaning of "煨": food is marinated, wrapped in lotus leaves or similar, sealed in wet clay or dough, and cooked in charcoal.

【烘 (hōng) Baking】 Cooking seasoned or prepared pastries or food in an oven.

【煸 (biān) Dry-frying】 Same as "煏 (bì)", formerly miswritten as "鞭" or "火便", close to "熯 (rǎn)": putting food into a hot wok and stir-frying constantly to dry out some of its moisture and remove off-flavors; or the dish eaten after concentrating its flavor this way.

【溜 (liū) Saucing】 A northern cooking term, close to "打芡 (qiàn)" in Cantonese cooking: a sweet and sour sauce thickened with starch coats crispy fried food, making it smooth and tasty.

【羹 (gēng) Thick soup】 One of the oldest cooking methods: diced food is cooked in boiling broth, then starch slurry is added to thicken the soup into a paste.

【攒 (zǎn) Splashing】 Formerly written "溅" or "灒 (zàn)", divided into "splashing oil" and "splashing wine": the former pours sizzling hot oil over steamed food to remove fishiness and add smoothness; the latter splashes Shaoxing wine onto food as it cooks to give it more "wok hei (镬气 huò qì)".

【烫 (tàng) Scalding】 Using boiling water to tighten the skin of meat. In the north it commonly means cooking sliced or cut ingredients in boiling or spicy broth.

【烧 (shāo) Roasting / braising】 The ancient "炙 (zhì)". In Cantonese cooking it means cooking food over charcoal or an open flame. In northern cuisines today, it means cooking food over low heat while reducing the sauce somewhat.

【烤 (kǎo) Roasting】 Used in northern cuisines in place of the old meaning of "烧", hence the saying "southern 烧, northern 烤": cooking food over an open flame.

【卤 (lǔ) Master-stock braising】 Cooking or flavoring food with a "lu stock" made from light soy sauce, spices and medicinal herbs.

【酱 (jiàng) Sauce-braising】 Flavoring or cooking food with a large amount of sauce or light soy sauce.

【浸 (jìn) Poaching】 Cooking food over a set time in a large amount of boiling water or broth kept at a gentle "chrysanthemum heart" simmer. Similar to the northern "氽 (tǔn)": the ingredient is cooked and then ladled (yǎo) into enough broth to cover it.

【风 (fēng) Wind-drying】 Hanging marinated food in a ventilated place year-round to let it air dry naturally.

【腊 (là) Winter curing】 Hanging marinated food in a ventilated place around the twelfth lunar month to let it air dry naturally.

【烟 (yān) Smoking】 Burning tea or spices and herbs in a sealed environment to infuse food with their fragrant smoke.

【熏 (xūn) Fumigating】 Formerly also written "熏", divided into "dry smoking" and "wet smoking". "Dry smoking" is similar to "烟"; "wet smoking" infuses food with fragrance using fresh flowers, Shaoxing wine and so on.

【糟 (zāo) Lees-marinating】 Putting food into fermented wine lees to flavor or cook it.

【醉 (zuì) Drunken marinating】 Flavoring or cooking food with a large amount of distilled liquor.

【甑 (zèng) Pot-steaming】 The ancient "蒸": food is chopped, seasoned, placed in a clay bowl (bō), then cooked with strong steam.

【冻 (dòng) Aspic】 Also called "crystal": well-cooked food is cooked again into a thick soup with agar or pork skin, then refrigerated until it sets and eaten cold.

【飞水 Parboiling】 Putting food into boiling water just long enough to half cook it, then quickly removing it, to give a good foundation for further cooking.

【冰浸 Ice-bathing】 Shredded food is quickly plunged into ice water to make it crisp. This method originated in Japan.

【拨丝 Spun sugar】 Food is coated in batter and fried, then tossed in melted syrup so that when picked up, it pulls out fine threads of sugar.

【挂霜 Sugar-frosting】 Fried food is tossed in melted syrup and stirred apart, or dusted directly with powdered sugar.

【椒盐 Salt and pepper】 Fried food that is cooked and dry is tossed in a pre-mixed "pepper salt" made from ground peppercorns and fine salt.

【油泡 Oil-poaching】 Cooking food rapidly in a large amount of hot oil.

【走油 Passing through oil】 Also called "拖油" or "跑油": prepared ingredients are passed quickly through very hot oil, giving a foundation for further cooking.

【火焰 Flaming】 Live fresh seafood is placed in a glass vessel and cooked with the heat from setting high-proof liquor alight.

【啫啫 (zé zé) Sizzling clay pot】 Food with ginger and scallion is put into a clay pot heated until extremely hot, making it sizzle with a "zé zé" sound and release its aroma.

【串烧 Skewer cooking】 Meat is sliced and marinated, threaded onto bamboo skewers and "poached" in hot oil; or sliced meat is threaded onto iron skewers, roasted over charcoal and sprinkled with cumin (zī rán) and other seasonings.

【铁板 Sizzling iron plate】 Originally a Western cooking method: food that has been "passed through oil", together with aromatics (mainly onion) and sauce, is placed on an extremely hot iron plate to finish cooking and release its aroma.

【桑拿 "Sauna"】 Also called "stone cooking": food is passed through oil, then placed on scorching hot stones (usually rain-flower pebbles), and a prepared sauce or broth is splashed on so the steam cooks the food or releases its aroma.

【煎封 Pan-fry and seal】 Called "煎烹" in the north, usually for fish: fish is marinated with seasonings, pan-fried thoroughly in hot oil over low heat, then sealed with an aromatic thickened sauce (qiàn) to let the flavor soak in.

【窝贴 Pot-sticking】 A "semi pan-fry/deep-fry method": marinated meat is coated with "pot-sticker batter" and pressed onto a piece of fatty pork, then cooked with a "hot wok, little oil" technique so that one side is crispy and the other soft and tender.

【窝塌 (tā) Pan-fry then braise】 Marinated food is coated in "egg-flour batter", pan-fried and then fried until cooked, then simmered through in a seasoned broth.

【软煎 Soft pan-frying】 A "semi pan-fry/deep-fry method": marinated meat is coated in "egg-flour batter", pan-fried and then fried until cooked, then cut into pieces and drizzled with sauce.

【蛋煎 Egg-frying】 Meat is pre-cooked by "parboiling" or "oil-poaching", mixed into seasoned beaten egg, then pan-fried over gentle heat until the egg base is golden.

【吉列 Cutlet】 A transliteration of the English word CUTLET: food is coated in egg, then breadcrumbs, then deep-fried in hot oil. This method comes from Western cooking.

【酥炸 Crispy deep-frying】 Food is marinated with seasonings, coated first in a wet batter and then dry starch, deep-fried, and then tossed in sauce.

【火锅 Hot pot】 Also called "rinse pot", and "打边炉" in Guangdong: fresh meat is "sliced" or "cut" into thin slices, or pounded into balls or fillings, and brought to the table with vegetables for diners to cook themselves in boiling water or broth.

【汽锅 Steam pot】 Marinated meat and medicinal herbs are placed in a special "steam pot" with boiling seasoned broth and slowly simmered before eating.

【凉拌 Cold dressing】 Cooked food or fruits and vegetables are cut, then mixed evenly with seasonings.

【鱼生 Raw fish】 Fresh live seafood is bled, cut into thin slices, mixed with shredded ginger, shredded scallion, crispy crackers, shredded lemon and so on, then dipped in light soy sauce and eaten.

【刺身 Sashimi】 Originally a Japanese preparation meaning sliced raw meat. As borrowed by Chinese cooking, it means scaling and bleeding live seafood, slicing it thinly, adding a few drops of lemon juice, and eating it dipped in Japanese mustard (jiè, wasabi).

【竹筒 Bamboo tube】 Anciently called "熷 (zēng)": using a bamboo tube as the vessel and cooking the food inside by "roasting", "grilling", "steaming", "stewing (dùn)" and so on.

【蜜汁 Honey glazing】 Sugar, honey, maltose and so on are dissolved into a thick syrup, the prepared ingredient is added, and through "reducing", "steaming" and similar methods it becomes soft and glutinous (nuò), with the sweetness soaked all the way through.

【焯 (chāo) 水 Blanching】 Also called "出水": a method of partially cooking ingredients in a pot of boiling or cold water.

【过油 Oil pre-cooking】 Partially cooking ingredients using oil as the heat medium. Passing small ingredients through warm oil is called "滑油 (oil-velveting)"; passing large ingredients through very hot oil is called "走油".

【挂糊 Battering】 Evenly coating the ingredients with a layer of batter before cooking.

【上浆 Velveting】 Mixing ingredients with starch, egg, salt and so on so they are coated in a thin layer of slurry.

【上劲 Working the paste】 Adding fine salt, water, starch and other ingredients to minced (róng ní mò) animal ingredients and stirring repeatedly until the paste is glossy and tender, doesn't sink in water, and doesn't fall apart.

【勾 (gōu) 芡 (qiàn) Thickening】 Adding a starch-water solution to the pot during cooking to give the sauce a certain thickness. Also called "着腻", "着芡" and "拢 (lǒng) 芡 (qiàn)".

【温油 Warm oil】 Commonly called 3/10 to 4/10 heat, usually 70℃~100℃.

【热油 Hot oil】 Commonly called 5/10 to 6/10 heat, usually 110℃~170℃.

【旺油 Very hot oil】 Commonly called 7/10 to 8/10 heat, usually 180℃~220℃.

【滑锅 Seasoning the wok】 Heat the wok, pour in a little oil and swirl it all over the wok, then pour the oil out.

【炝锅 Blooming aromatics】 Also called "炸锅": putting ginger, scallion, minced chili or other fragrant seasonings into hot base oil, stir-frying until fragrant, then promptly adding the main ingredients.

【高汤 Superior stock】 Also called "clear soup", "上汤" or "顶汤": a rough stock simmered from pork bones, chicken bones, duck carcasses, meat trimmings and so on, then clarified with an unsalted paste of minced chicken or meat until it is as clear as water, rich and savory.

【奶汤 Milky stock】 Also called "white soup": a milky white stock made by simmering ingredients in water.
