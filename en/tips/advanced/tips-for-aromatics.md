# Tips for Aromatics

* The basic order for adding aromatics is: ginger first, then scallion and garlic, chili peppers, then dry spices (star anise / Sichuan peppercorns / numbing peppercorns), then dried chilies. Depending on the dish, you can drop any aromatics you don't need from this queue.
* Ginger has the highest water content, which means we need more time to fry the juice out of it.
* If you are using scallion segments (preferably smashed twice with the flat of a cleaver), I recommend adding the scallion segments first and then the minced garlic. If you are using chopped green onion, both can go into the pan together. Note: it is harder to fry the juice out of scallion segments.
* If you are making a stir-fry, I recommend adding the dry spices before the ginger. This makes the oil more flavorful, so the stir-fried dish is more fragrant. Stews and braises don't do this because you may need to stir-fry many aromatics, and stir-frying them for so long would turn the dry spices black and bitter.
* Dried chilies go in last because they turn black easily from the heat of the pan. A few seconds of stir-frying is enough.
* Note: whatever cuisine you like to cook, frying these aromatics until golden over low heat makes the whole dish more fragrant, which seasonings alone cannot do.

## When to Add Salt and How Much

* For quick stir-fries, add salt first. Amount of salt = total weight of ingredients x 0.9%;
* For meat dishes, add salt when the meat is about 80% cooked. Amount of salt = (total weight of ingredients + 30mL of liquid) x (1-1.2%);
* For soups, add salt at the very end. Amount of salt = initial amount of water x 0.8%.
* Total daily salt intake should not exceed 5g. See [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/salt-reduction)
