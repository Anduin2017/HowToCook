# Pressure Cooking

Steaming (rice) and stewing with a rice cooker, stovetop pressure cooker or electric pressure cooker.

## What a Pressure Cooker Is

A pressure cooker is really an ordinary pot with a lockable, semi-sealed lid that has a valve for controlling the pressure inside.

### How It Works

A pressure cooker lets steam build up inside the pot, raising the internal pressure. As the pressure rises, so does the boiling point of water, so water-containing food can cook at temperatures above 100 ℃.

### Advantages

* Because the actual cooking temperature is higher, cooking times can be greatly shortened.
* The high temperature inside promotes browning and caramelization, producing distinctive flavors.

## Process

* Put the ingredients and water in the inner pot, close the lid, **make sure the pot is sealed**, and heat.
* For tough ingredients such as tendons, a pressure cooker can make them tender fairly easily, giving a better texture.
* Pressure cookers usually have a self-locking valve (float valve). As pressure builds during cooking, the valve rises and locks, sealing the inside from the outside so pressure can build. Once it rises, it also locks the lid so it can't be forced open, as a safety measure. Make sure nothing blocks the self-locking valve during cooking, so the cooker works properly.
* After it switches to keep-warm, **release all the steam through the pressure release valve before opening the lid**.

### Notes

* **Steam is very hot—don't put your face near the release valve.**
* When cooking **liquid foods**, **don't release the pressure manually**—watch out for spraying (you can put the food in a sealed jar or vacuum bag before pressure cooking).
* For some dishes (such as soups), manual pressure release **may affect the flavor and texture**.
* Before opening the lid, make sure the steam has been released. Don't open it all at once, and especially **don't open it facing anyone**, to avoid steam burns.
* After cooking, once the pressure inside drops to match the outside, the self-locking valve releases. You can use this as the sign that the lid can be opened.
* The seal depends on the rubber gasket in the lid; for older pressure cookers, check that the gasket still works.
* Make sure the gasket is completely clean; any particle stuck in it can break the seal.
* Many pressure cookers have a max-fill line that ingredients and liquid shouldn't exceed; too much food and liquid can make steam surge and block the release valve, or spray out a lot of steam that's hard to clean up.
* For pressure cookers without a max-fill line, it's best not to fill above 2/3 of the pot either.
* **Don't use a pressure cooker for foods that foam easily, such as oats or dried noodles**. The foam can block the steam valve and pressure release pipe.
* If, during cooking, the pressure valve rises and releases steam or vapor, the cooker is over-pressurized and the valve is releasing the excess for safety. The escaping steam may smell wonderful, but the food loses flavor, and over-pressurization can bend the locking grooves on some types of pressure cooker. So when you see steam escaping, turn down the heat.
* Tip: opening the lid from the side is a good choice.
