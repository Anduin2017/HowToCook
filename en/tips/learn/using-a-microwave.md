# Using a Microwave

## What a Microwave Is

The microwave oven was invented in 1945 by [Percy Lebaron Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Spencer).

While working as a radar systems engineer, he noticed that the chocolate bar in his pocket started melting whenever the radar was on, which gave him the idea that led to the invention.

### How It Works

A microwave uses electromagnetic waves at a frequency of 2.45 billion hertz produced by a magnetron; this frequency makes water and oil molecules vibrate and heat up.

## Process

Microwaves do quite a good job at many cooking tasks.

High power is good for:

* [Cooking] Cooking vegetables
* [Cooking] Softening hard, high-moisture vegetables (such as potatoes, onions and artichokes)
* [Puffing] Popping snacks, such as cream puffs, Indian papadums and popcorn

Medium power is good for:

* [Cooking] Seafood (e.g. [Microwave Black Cod with Ginger and Green Onion](../../dishes/aquatic/microwave-scallion-and-ginger-black-cod.md))
* [Cooking] Tenderizing meat
* [Dehydrating] Drying fruit and vegetable peels
* [Dehydrating] Making jerky
* [Frying] Crisping herbs
* [Frying] Wilting leafy greens
* [Heating] Reheating leftovers

Low power is used for:

* [Defrosting] Thawing food
* [Defrosting] Melting butter and chocolate

## Notes

* A microwave shouldn't be used to heat plain water, as it may superheat and boil violently, which can be dangerous to you
* Never heat eggs in a microwave! Unless it's a **shelled**, **raw** egg with the yolk pierced. Heating makes the gas inside the egg expand, causing it to explode.
* Whatever the reason, even out of curiosity, you shouldn't heat whole fruit (such as grapes, blueberries or cherry tomatoes) in a microwave—it may make them explode
* Because the microwave wavelength is 12.2 cm, microwaves heat small objects more slowly than large ones. So if the ingredients are very small, it's best to group them together for heating.
* Microwaves only penetrate a few centimeters into food, so sometimes the outside is very hot while the inside is still cold. The solution is to put the ingredients with a little liquid into a sealed bag, or into a bowl covered with plastic wrap, so enough steam forms inside the container to make up for the microwave's uneven cooking.
    * Tip: when opening the sealed bag, watch out for escaping steam
* Microwaves only heat things containing liquid molecules such as water and oil, so plastic wrap and sealed bags aren't heated by the microwaves.
* Metal reflects microwaves rather than heating up; avoid containers with gold rims or metal patterns, to avoid uneven heating that cracks the container. Always use porcelain or glass containers or microwave-specific bakeware.
