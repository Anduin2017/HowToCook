# Cold-Dressed Dishes

## What Cold Dressing Is

Cold dressing (liangban) is a way of making a dish by tossing the main ingredients together with supporting ingredients

### Forms of Cold Dressing

A cold-dressed dish can have the ingredients and supporting ingredients mixed together
Or it can keep the ingredients and supporting ingredients separate, in which case the supporting ingredients are called a dipping sauce

### Why Cold Dressing

* Some cold-dressed dishes need no heat source
* Some cold-dressed dishes reduce washing up (no washing, or just a rinse)
* Cold dressing preserves the state of the ingredients, which shows especially with vegetables and raw meats

### Purpose of Cold Dressing

* The purpose of cold dressing is to add flavor to bland or tasteless ingredients, such as chicken ribs

### What Can Go In

Including but not limited to:

* Main ingredients
* Supporting ingredients
* Marinades
* Seasonings

### Notes

* Pay attention to food safety: if you're not sure an ingredient is safe, don't serve it cold-dressed, and if you know it's unsafe, definitely don't
* Maximize the surface area of the main ingredients, so scoring, slicing, smashing or even mincing is recommended
* Cold-dressed dishes make basic demands on your digestive system; only eat them if you're confident you won't get explosive diarrhea, or if you have time to deal with it
* This was written during the COVID-19 pandemic; for peace of mind, it was suggested to heat all ingredients above 100 degrees Celsius for more than 15 seconds, and if you want to be safe, avoid cold-dressed dishes as much as possible

## Equipment

Any container will do, from a porcelain crock to a food-grade plastic bag

### Notes

* To keep ingredients from spilling while you toss them, a rigid container at least twice the volume of all the ingredients is best
* For food safety, after marinating in a plastic bag or plastic bowl, move the ingredients to a porcelain or metal container as soon as possible
* For food safety, handle raw ingredients and supporting ingredients on a clean cutting board

## Process

### Preparing Leafy Vegetable Main Ingredients (optional) (choose one or more options)

Examples: cabbage, lettuce, napa cabbage hearts, onions, etc.

* Tear the ingredient into 4cm * 4cm pieces
* Cut the ingredient into 0.5cm-long strips
* Trim off the unused parts and use the leaves whole
* Blanch the prepared ingredient

#### Notes

For some ingredients you don't need to separate the leaves: cut along the axis, then cut crosswise into sections. If you're confident in your knife skills, just cut crosswise into sections directly

### Preparing Chunky Vegetable Main Ingredients (optional) (choose one or more options)

Examples: potatoes, water chestnuts, cucumbers, etc.

* Cut the ingredient into long strips with a 0.5cm * 0.5cm cross-section
* Cut the ingredient into 4cm * 4cm slices less than 0.5cm thick
* Smash or crush the ingredient with the flat of the knife (especially for cucumbers)
* Use the ingredient as-is (especially for ingredients that are already small)
* Blanch the prepared ingredient

#### Notes

Smashing an ingredient may make it fly everywhere; you can wrap it in a food-grade plastic bag first

### Preparing Mushroom and Fungus Main Ingredients (optional) (choose one or more options)

Examples: all kinds of mushrooms, all kinds of wood ear fungus, etc.

* Rehydrate the ingredient
* Cut the ingredient into long strips with a 0.5cm * 0.5cm cross-section
* Cut the ingredient into 4cm * 4cm slices less than 0.5cm thick
* Use the ingredient as-is (especially for ingredients that are already small)
* Blanch the prepared ingredient

#### Notes

All mushrooms and fungi must be strictly confirmed safe—otherwise you might end up lying on a slab with everything gone

### Preparing Meat Main Ingredients (optional) (choose one or more options)

Examples: fish, jellyfish heads, cooked pork, cooked poultry, etc.

* Cook the ingredient by steaming, boiling, roasting, frying, etc.
* Soak the ingredient in cold water for a while (especially for seafood)
* Tear the ingredient into strips
* Slice the ingredient thinly (especially for boiled pork)
* Cut the ingredient into long strips with a 0.8cm * 0.8cm cross-section
* Tear or cut the ingredient into large pieces by part (especially for whole cooked poultry)

#### Notes

* No exceptions for pork and poultry: they must be well done, fully cooked, with no visible blood
* Some beef, fish and seafood can be eaten raw once confirmed safe

### Preparing "I Reckon This'll Work Too" Ingredients (avoid this process if possible) (all options required)

Examples: noodles, rice, fruit, tender tree leaves, etc.

* Confirm the ingredient is safe
* Prepare the ingredient into an edible state
* Prepare the ingredient into a state that can be tossed

#### Notes

* Confirm the ingredient is safe: you can mince a small amount and smear it on your upper arm for 30 minutes to test for allergic reactions; if anything abnormal happens, discard it
* Most tree leaves and grasses contain substances that cause discomfort, even after cooking; confirm they're safe
* Most grains are hard to digest and absorb before cooking and may upset your stomach

### Preparing Supporting Ingredients (optional) (choose one or more options)

Examples: facing-heaven chilies, garlic cloves, ginger, dried chilies, etc.

* Remove the stems from the facing-heaven chilies, wash, cut off 0.5cm at the stem end, then mince or cut into sections
* Smash the garlic cloves and peel them, cut off 0.5cm at the root end, then mince or cut into sections
* Peel the ginger, then mince, shred or slice
* Crush the dried chilies
* Put the prepared ingredients into a small bowl or the container you'll toss in
* Add to the container: various powders, seasonings or marinades, and mix well

#### Notes

* Supporting ingredients, preparation and methods vary enormously—don't limit your thinking, but verify carefully, use moderate amounts, and remember safety

### Combining the Ingredients (optional) (choose one or more options)

* Squeeze the water out of high-moisture meat ingredients and drain
* Put high-moisture vegetable ingredients in a container, add salt at about 200g : 5g, toss, let sit for 5 minutes, and drain
* Add the ingredients and supporting ingredients to the tossing container
* Toss with chopsticks, a spoon, or clean hands
* Seal the container and shake evenly
* Tilt the container to an angle where nothing spills, and rotate it around its axis

#### Notes

* High-moisture ingredients added directly may release too much water and dilute the seasoning
* If there isn't enough liquid or it won't mix evenly while tossing, add a little cooled boiled water; if unsure of the amount, 15mL at a time is best
* Some highly absorbent ingredients shouldn't be tossed, as they may end up too strongly flavored after marinating

### Eating (required)

* Eat the tossed ingredients directly
* Dip the untossed main ingredients in the dipping sauce and eat
* Add the ingredients and dipping sauce to a staple food and eat
