# Stir-frying and Pan-frying

## Equipment

You can use an ordinary metal (iron/stainless steel/aluminum) wok or frying pan, or a nonstick pan.

Aluminum containers are not recommended; see the food safety section for why

### Notes

* How to stir-fry without sticking in an ordinary pan:

#### Fry-an-Egg-First Method

* Before stir-frying anything, scramble an egg; don't wash the pan afterward, and the next dish won't stick.

#### Hot Wok, Cold Oil Method

* Remember: it must be a hot wok with cold oil, so heat the wok first
  * Dry-heat the clean, empty wok so it heats evenly until hot
  * Add cold oil and swirl the wok so the oil coats the whole surface (swirl back and forth so it heats evenly)
  * When you see vapor rising from the wok, the oil is hot
  * Pour the oil out, and don't wash the wok afterward
  * You can repeat the above steps 2-3 times for a better nonstick effect
  * Note: on a gas stove, flames may flare up—be careful

#### Hot Wok, Two Oils Method

* First heat the wok
  * Dry-heat the clean, empty wok so it heats evenly until hot
  * Add "a little cold oil" and swirl the wok so the oil coats the whole surface (swirl back and forth so it heats evenly)
  * When you see vapor rising from the wok, the oil is hot
  * Then add more cold oil and start stir-frying
  * Note: on a gas stove, flames may flare up—be careful.

Additional notes:

* The goal is to coat the bottom of the wok with oil; this works for every household wok on the market, instantly turning it nonstick.
* Food won't stick in a nonstick pan. The nonstick effect comes from the coating on the inside. **Metal spatulas scratch the coating. Use wooden or silicone spatulas with nonstick pans to avoid damaging it.**

### Process

Turn on the heat — set the wok flat on the flame and heat it — pour oil into the wok and heat it — add the food and stir-fry — remember to add seasonings before serving

### Notes

* To judge whether the wok/oil is hot, hold your hand flat above the wok and feel the heat; only add ingredients once the oil is hot.
* Before pouring oil into the wok, make sure there's no water left inside. **Water makes hot oil splatter, which is dangerous.**
* Following on from that, drain ingredients before they go into hot oil (beaten egg is fine); likewise, never put frozen ingredients into hot oil, as the melting ice is dangerous.
* **If the oil catches fire, never pour water on it.** That makes the fire spread. When a fire starts, quickly turn off the heat and cover the wok with its lid.
