# Removing Off-Odors

Removing off-odors (quxing) is a step in cooking.

It means using methods including, but not limited to, adding seasonings and blanching to remove fishy or gamey smells from meat, seafood and other foods.

**Fishy and gamey notes are part of the flavor of some foods; removing too much may leave the food without its character.**

There are many ways to remove off-odors; choose flexibly while cooking.

## Methods

### Adding Seasonings

Adding seasonings to the ingredients is the simplest method. For example, for most dishes that use beaten egg ([Steamed Egg Custard](../../dishes/vegetable_dish/steamed-egg-custard.md), [Tomato and Egg Stir-fry](../../dishes/vegetable_dish/tomato-and-egg-stir-fry.md)), you can add salt, cooking wine, vinegar and other seasonings while beating the eggs to remove the eggy smell.

When cooking certain meats, you can add spices such as Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, bay leaves, cinnamon, fennel seeds and chili peppers to the broth.

Ready-made spicy hotpot base has an extremely strong aroma; adding a suitable amount while cooking is enough to cover the gamey smell of most meats.

### Dipping Sauces

Some foods still smell strongly after cooking. You can mix a dipping sauce to mask the smell while eating.

Common dipping sauce ingredients include: vinegar, soy sauce, sesame oil, doubanjiang, sweet bean sauce, sesame paste, peanut butter, fermented bean curd, salt, garlic, ginger, etc.

Dipping sauce combinations are a matter of taste, so no examples are given here.

### Aromatics in Hot Oil (Qiangguo)

When stir-frying, you can fry aromatics such as green onion, ginger, garlic and dried chilies in hot oil. The high heat releases their aromatic compounds, which cover off-odors to some extent and add flavor to the dish.

### Cold-Water Blanching

Some animal ingredients retain blood, such as chicken, pig's trotters and pork ribs. If not removed, the residual blood gives the dish an off-odor.

When the ingredients go into cold water, the residual blood disperses into the water; as the temperature rises, the proteins in the blood coagulate, and the blood dispersed in the water forms scum that floats to the surface. Simply skim off the scum with a spoon to finish removing the odor, and the remaining clear broth can be used as a base for braising or stewing.

### Notes

- When blanching, seasonings such as Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, cooking wine and scallion are often added to the pot to further strengthen the effect
- Star anise has a strong aroma; add it in moderation
- Sichuan peppercorns and green Sichuan peppercorns are small and used in large quantities, so they may remain in the pot or even in the finished dish; you can wrap them in a cheesecloth spice bag or use a food-grade stainless steel spice infuser so they're easy to remove before serving
