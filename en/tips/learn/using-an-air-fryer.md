# Using an Air Fryer

## What an Air Fryer Is

An air fryer is an electric cooking appliance that uses hot air instead of hot oil to cook food, giving results close to deep-frying without oil.

### How It Works

An air fryer's heater at the top produces hot air, which circulates around the food, quickly heating the food's own fat and drawing out moisture to produce a fried effect and a crispness similar to deep-fried food.

### Advantages

* Since no cooking oil needs to be added, you can **greatly reduce** your intake of high-fat, high-calorie cooking oil.
* Fast-circulating hot air dehydrates the food, making the surface golden and crispy—crisp outside and tender inside.
* Simple to operate and beginner-friendly.

## Process

* Place the air fryer on a stable, flat, level, heat-resistant surface.
* Remove the pan, put the ingredients in the basket, and slide the pan back into the unit.
* Change the preset temperature and turn the knob to set the cooking time.
* Once the time is set, the appliance starts cooking; it's done when the timer rings.
* Tip all the food from the basket into a bowl or plate. Always take the basket with the food out of the pan, because the bottom of the pan **may contain hot oil or fat**.

## Notes

* When using an air fryer, don't set the temperature too high (ideally within 120℃, preferably no higher than 168℃) or the time too long (about 10 minutes), to avoid producing too much harmful [acrylamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrylamide).
* Cooking fewer starchy foods in the air fryer, such as potatoes, bread and youtiao, reduces [acrylamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrylamide) intake accordingly. Comparatively, air fryers are better suited to foods with more fat or moisture, such as meat and vegetables.
* Don't block the air inlet on top or the outlet on the back during use. Blocking them with your hand could result in **burns from the hot air**.
* Temperatures can vary by ±10℃ between brands; for the first try, reduce the time by 10% and adjust gradually

## Cooking Suggestions

### Common Foods

| Food    | Temperature (℃)   | Time (minutes) | Steps                                                               |
|---------|---------|--------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **French fries**  | 200     | 15-20  | 1. No need to thaw frozen fries; spray lightly with oil; - 2. Spread in the basket in one layer (no overlapping), shake every 5 minutes; - 3. For the last 2 minutes you can raise it to 210℃ for color.    |
| **Chicken wings**  | 180     | 18-22  | 1. Score the wings and marinate with light soy sauce, cooking wine, oyster sauce and minced garlic for 1 hour; - 2. Spread in the basket and brush with honey water; - 3. Roast for 10 minutes, then flip and continue.       |
| **Fish**  | 180-190 | 12-15  | 1. Score both sides of the fish and marinate with ginger slices, green onion, salt and cooking wine for 20 minutes; - 2. Brush the fish with oil and line with foil to prevent sticking; - 3. Flip once halfway.          |
| **Steak**  | 200     | 8-12   | 1. Bring the steak to room temperature, season both sides with salt, black pepper and olive oil; - 2. Preheat the air fryer for 5 minutes, then cook according to thickness (4-6 minutes per side).        |
| **Beef cubes** | 180     | 15-18  | 1. Cut the beef into 2cm cubes and marinate with light soy sauce, starch and black pepper for 30 minutes; - 2. Spread in the basket, roast for 10 minutes, then turn once; - 3. You can add onion and bell peppers to roast together. |
| **Pork chops** | 175-185 | 16-20  | 1. Pound the pork chops with the back of a knife, marinate with light soy sauce, garlic powder and five-spice powder for 40 minutes; - 2. Spray with oil and line with baking paper; - 3. Flip halfway and brush with the marinade.          |
| **Egg tarts**  | 170-180 | 12-15  | 1. Thaw the tart shells and pour in homemade custard (milk + cream + sugar + egg yolk); - 2. No need to preheat; bake until the shells are golden and the centers are lightly charred.            |
| **Cake**  | 160     | 25-30  | 1. Line a 6-inch pan with baking paper and pour in the batter (70% full); - 2. Bake slowly at low temperature; done when a toothpick comes out clean; - 3. Invert to cool so it doesn't collapse.            |
| **Pizza**  | 180-190 | 8-12   | 1. No need to thaw frozen pizza; you can add extra cheese; - 2. Line with foil to prevent leaks, bake until the cheese has browned spots; - 3. For homemade pizza, prebake the crust for 5 minutes before adding toppings.        |
| **Peanuts** | 160     | 10-12  | 1. Soak raw peanuts for 5 minutes and drain; - 2. Toss with a little oil and salt; - 3. Spread in a single layer and shake every 3 minutes.                  |

### Key Points

1. **Preparation is key**
    - Meat must be fully thawed and patted dry (bring steaks/pork chops to room temperature)
    - Frozen foods (fries/pizza) can be cooked directly, but shake/flip more often

2. **Preventing sticking**
    - For sticky foods like fish and cake, line with baking paper or foil
    - You can line the bottom of the basket with onion or lemon slices for flavor and to catch juices

3. **Controlling color**
    - Raising the temperature by 10-20℃ for the last 2-3 minutes makes the surface crispier (for fries/chicken wings)
    - Covering egg tarts/cakes with foil prevents over-browning

4. **Checking doneness**
    - Meat: press with chopsticks—firm means well done, soft and springy means medium
    - Cake: done when a toothpick inserted into the center comes out without batter
