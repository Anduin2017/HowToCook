# How to Make Millet Congee

Millet congee is a common home-style congee in northern China: smooth and soft with a rich grain aroma, and the "rice oil" that forms on top is especially soothing for the stomach. It's rich in protein, fat, carbohydrates and carotene, and its vitamin B1 content is among the highest of all grains; it's easy to digest. It's very simple; beginners can master it easily, and it takes about 30-35 minutes from boiling the water to serving.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 362 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Millet
- Water (spring water is best)

## Calculation

- Millet 100 grams
- Water (spring water is best) 2000 grams

Use the above to calculate the proportions of ingredients you plan to use.

## Steps

1. Put 100 grams of millet in a bowl and rinse it lightly once (stir with your hand and pour off the water; this only removes surface dust—don't scrub it!!!)
2. Bring the water to a boil—it must be boiling!!!
3. When the water is at a rolling boil, pour the millet into the pot. (A very important step that's easily overlooked)
4. Stir so the millet doesn't stick to the bottom, and keep cooking on high heat for 6-10 minutes, stirring a few times in between.
5. Turn to medium, then low heat, and simmer for 15-20 minutes with the lid slightly ajar—don't let the millet oil escape. Keep stirring a few times so it doesn't burn on the bottom

## Additional Notes

* This is made in an ordinary pot (only 30-35 minutes) and tastes best. A pressure cooker or rice cooker is more convenient but gives a worse result; use a bit less water, usually 100 grams of millet + 1800 grams of water
* Millet only needs rinsing to remove surface dust; don't over-wash it, or you'll lose the millet oil
* Remember: the millet must go in when the water is boiling
* I don't like adding baking soda; I prefer the natural aroma of millet
