# How to Make Corn and Pork Rib Soup

Corn and pork rib soup is a fresh, sweet home-style soup: tender ribs, with corn and carrot bringing natural sweetness. It's rich in protein, vitamins and dietary fiber for balanced nutrition. It's simple and beginner-friendly: just blanch the ribs, sear them until fragrant, and simmer slowly for about an hour. It takes about an hour and a half from prep to serving.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 1724 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Pork ribs
* Corn
* Carrot
* Ginger
* Scallion (large, Chinese leek-type)
* Cooking oil
* Vinegar
* Cooking wine
* Ground black pepper
* Salt
* Clay pot (an iron pot works if you don't have one)

## Calculation

Per portion:

* Pork ribs 500-800g
* Corn 1 ear (add another if you love corn)
* Carrot 1 (add another if you love carrots)
* Scallion a small half stalk
* Green onion 1 stalk
* Cooking oil 10 ml
* Ground black pepper 4g
* Cooking wine 10ml
* Vinegar 10ml
* Salt 10-15g (depending on how much soup is left at the end)
* Boiling water 1000 ml

## Steps

### Prep

1. Cut the scallion into large 3-4cm segments and smash them with the back of the knife
2. Chop the corn into small pieces
3. Roll-cut the carrot into chunks
4. Peel the ginger and cut into large slices
5. Chop the fresh ribs into small pieces

### Cooking

1. Put the ribs in cold water with the scallion, ginger and cooking wine to blanch. Bring to a boil over high heat, skim off the foam, then take out the ribs and drain
2. Heat the pot, add cold oil, and stir-fry the large ginger slices with the ribs. When the ribs are lightly browned on the surface, add the vinegar (it helps the meat get tender faster) and keep stir-frying for a minute
3. Pour in the boiling water—all of it at once, and don't add more later—and bring to a boil over high heat
4. Add the corn first, add the pepper, cover and simmer over low heat for twenty minutes, then add the carrot, cover and keep simmering over low heat for forty minutes
5. Seasoning is easy: three minutes before serving, add nothing but salt, and finally sprinkle with a handful of chopped green onion
