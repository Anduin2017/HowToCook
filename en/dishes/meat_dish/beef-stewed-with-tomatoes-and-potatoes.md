# How to Make Beef Stewed with Tomatoes and Potatoes

![Beef stewed with tomatoes and potatoes - preview image 1](../../../dishes/meat_dish/西红柿土豆炖牛肉/abaaba_1.png)

A home-style stew that's sweet, tangy and appetizing, with a rich sauce, tender beef and creamy potatoes. Beef provides high-quality protein, tomatoes are rich in lycopene, and with potatoes and onion it's nutritionally balanced. It's simple with no complicated techniques, so beginners can make it easily. It takes about 1 to 1.5 hours from prep to plate.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 827 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Beef
- Aromatics
- Green onion
- Ginger
- Cooking wine
- Sichuan peppercorns
- Star anise
- Bay leaves
- White sugar or rock sugar
- Soy sauce (optional)
- Dark soy sauce
- Ground black pepper (or ground white pepper)
- Potatoes
- Tomatoes
- Onion

## Calculation

- Beef 500-700g
- Aromatics
  - Green onion 1 stalk, ginger 4 slices, cooking wine
  - Sichuan peppercorns 3g
  - Star anise one (or half)
  - Bay leaves 2
  - Oil 15ml (if using beef brisket, you can reduce to 10ml to taste)
- Seasonings
  - White sugar or rock sugar
  - Soy sauce (Qianhe naturally brewed light soy sauce, no additives), dark soy sauce
  - Ground black pepper (white is fine too) 2g
- Potatoes 2-3 (to taste, as many as fit in the pot)
- Tomatoes, 2-3 medium, about fist-sized
- Onion 1, a bit bigger than a fist

## Steps

1. Prep:

  - Peel the potatoes and cut into large 5cm chunks. Set aside
  - Score a cross on the tomatoes, blanch in boiling water and peel, remove the core, and cut into 3cm pieces. Set aside
  - Chop 4g of the green onion, and break the rest into 5-8cm segments
  - Soak the beef in cold water for half an hour to draw out the blood, or put it in cold water and boil until the surface turns white, then take it out, skimming off the foam along the way
  - Dice the onion into 0.5-1cm pieces

2. Cooking

  - Use medium heat throughout
  - Put the beef in the pressure cooker with cold water to cover, add the green onion segments, ginger slices and 20g of cooking wine, and cook for 20 minutes once it reaches pressure
  - Take out the beef and cut into large 5cm chunks. Pick out the ginger, and pour the broth into a large bowl for braising later
  - Add the oil, and at 4/10 to 5/10 heat, add the Sichuan peppercorns, star anise and bay leaves. Once fragrant and before they burn, pick them out and discard
  - Add the beef, green onion and ginger and stir-fry until fragrant. It doesn't matter how long; if it looks like it's about to burn, add 10ml of broth (same below)
  - Once fragrant, add 15ml of light soy sauce, 15ml of cooking wine, the pepper, and 5-10g of tomato paste, ketchup or a can of tomatoes, then add the onion and stir-fry until translucent
  - (Optional) Add a tiny bit of salt so the onion releases water and doesn't burn easily; don't add too much, you'll season later
  - Add the tomatoes and stir-fry until they break down and soften, then pour in the rest of the broth

3. Braising

  - Bring to a boil over medium heat, then use low heat the rest of the way
  - Add the potatoes and season 30-40 minutes before the end
  - Add sugar and salt to taste, tasting as you go, until it's right
  - When a chopstick pokes through the beef easily, it's done!

## Additional Notes

- Make sure the broth is warm when you add it during braising
- Don't cover the pot while braising, or the potatoes will fall apart
- Stir occasionally while braising to keep the bottom from burning
- It tastes even better braised in a clay pot. You can stir-fry in the wok and then transfer to a braising pot; if using a clay pot, don't use high heat before it comes to a boil, to avoid cracking it
- Don't use too much soy sauce in total; the sauce gets saltier as it reduces. It's fine if it's a little bland while still thin, and you can add more soy sauce during braising if needed
- Don't use chicken bouillon or MSG; they mask the natural savoriness of the beef
