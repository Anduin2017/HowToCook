# How to Make Ants Climbing a Tree

Ants climbing a tree (mayi shang shu) is a classic Sichuan home-style dish: savory and slightly spicy, with soft, slippery glass noodles that soak up the sauce and tender, fragrant minced meat. Made mainly from sweet potato glass noodles and minced meat, it's rich in carbohydrates and protein for a quick energy boost. The difficulty is medium and the steps are clear, so it's fairly beginner-friendly. It takes about 20 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 791 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Sweet potato glass noodles
- Minced pork (or minced beef)
- Pixian doubanjiang (chili bean paste)
- Light soy sauce
- Dark soy sauce
- Cooking oil
- Minced garlic, minced ginger
- Green onion (optional)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2 people.

Per portion:

- Sweet potato glass noodles 80g (dry weight)
- Minced pork 150g
- Pixian doubanjiang 15g
- Light soy sauce 10ml
- Dark soy sauce 5ml
- Cooking oil 10ml
- Minced garlic 10g
- Minced ginger 5g
- Water 300ml (for cooking the noodles)

## Steps

1. Soak the sweet potato glass noodles in advance for 20 minutes until soft. Set aside
2. Mince the garlic and ginger separately. Set aside
3. Heat the wok, add 10ml of cooking oil, and fry the minced garlic and ginger until fragrant
4. Add the minced pork and stir-fry until **the meat turns white and releases a little fat**
5. Add the Pixian doubanjiang and stir-fry until **the oil turns red**
6. Add the light soy sauce and dark soy sauce and stir-fry evenly
7. Pour in 300ml of water and bring to a boil
8. Add the soaked, drained noodles, gently moving them with chopsticks to keep them from sticking together
9. Cook over medium-low heat for about 5 minutes, until the noodles have **completely absorbed the liquid** and look slightly dry
10. Sprinkle with chopped green onion to taste, turn off the heat, and plate

## Additional Notes

- Don't cook the noodles too long; they break easily and the texture suffers. If cooking longer, use less liquid
- Pixian doubanjiang is quite salty, so you can reduce the light soy sauce to taste
- Adding 0.5g of ground white pepper improves the flavor
