# How to Make Steamed Spare Ribs with Black Bean Sauce

This is a popular steamed dim sum dish from Cantonese teahouses, with a rich fermented black bean aroma and tender ribs that slip off the bone. Pork ribs provide high-quality protein and calcium, and fermented black beans add flavor and aid absorption. It requires soaking and rinsing, marinating and controlled steaming, which is a little test for beginners, but the steps are clear and easy to follow. It takes only about 1 hour from prep to plate. The leftover juices are especially delicious mixed with rice or noodles.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1513 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Pork spare ribs
- Yangjiang fermented black beans (douchi)
- Minced garlic
- Minced ginger
- Light soy sauce
- Dark soy sauce
- Oyster sauce
- Granulated sugar
- Potato starch
- Cooking oil
- Water
- Chopped green onion (optional)
- White sesame seeds (optional)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2-4 people.

Per portion:

- Pork spare ribs 500 g
- Yangjiang fermented black beans 15 g
- Minced garlic 10 g
- Minced ginger 5 g
- Light soy sauce 15 ml
- Dark soy sauce 3 ml
- Oyster sauce 10 g
- Granulated sugar 5 g
- Potato starch 8 g
- Cooking oil 20 ml (10 ml if you won't pour hot oil over at the end)
- Water 30 ml

## Steps

1. Soak the ribs in cold water for 10 minutes to draw out the blood, change the water and repeat 2 times, then pat dry with paper towels
2. Soak the black beans in water for 5 minutes
3. Mix all the ingredients together (using 10 ml of the oil) and marinate for 8-30 minutes
4. Once the water is boiling, steam for 18 minutes
5. Leave covered for another 2 minutes
6. Heat 10 ml of oil in a pan to 180-200 ℃
7. Sprinkle the chopped green onion and white sesame seeds evenly on top and pour the hot oil over them

## Additional Notes

- Chopping the soaked black beans a little gives more flavor
- Adjust the steaming time to the size of the ribs; they're done when a chopstick easily pokes into the cartilage
- Traditional black bean spare ribs don't have the toppings or hot oil
- The leftover juices can be mixed with rice or noodles
