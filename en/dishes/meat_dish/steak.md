# How to Make Steak

![Finished steak](../../../dishes/meat_dish/牛排/牛排.jpg)

This is a classic Western pan-seared steak: a caramelized crust wrapped around a tender, juicy center, with butter, garlic and herbs for rich flavor. It's rich in high-quality protein, fat and minerals such as iron and zinc. The cooking process is fairly beginner-friendly, but mastering the heat still takes some practice. It takes 15-30 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1605 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Frying pan (cast iron recommended if you have one)
- Aluminum foil (optional)
- Paper towels (optional)
- Tablespoon
- Steak; see [How to choose different cuts of steak](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/72352426)
- Olive oil (extra virgin recommended)
- Butter
- Salt (coarse sea salt recommended)
- Ground black pepper (coarse, freshly ground recommended)
- Garlic
- Herbs (optional; rosemary or thyme recommended, preferably fresh sprigs rather than ground herbs)
- Ready-made steak sauce (optional)
- Sides (optional, as you like; asparagus, white button mushrooms, cherry tomatoes and baby potatoes are recommended—choose 1-2)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2 people.

Per portion:

- Steak 450-500g (two steaks)
- Ground black pepper 2g
- Salt 5g
- Garlic 1 bulb (about 25-30g; about 5-10g actually used)
- Olive oil 10-15ml
- Butter 20-25g
- White button mushrooms 5-10
- Baby potatoes 5-10 (about 20g each)
- Cherry tomatoes 5-10 (about 15g each)
- Thyme 2g (if using fresh thyme sprigs, take 3-6 sprigs about 10cm long)

## Steps

### Prep

1. Take the steak out of the refrigerator to thaw.

  - If the steak is frozen, move it to the refrigerator the night before, and leave it at room temperature for half an hour before cooking.
  - **Do not** thaw the steak **in water or by heating it**.

2. Wash the cherry tomatoes, baby potatoes and mushrooms, cut them in half, and set aside.
3. Peel the garlic, keep 5-8 of the larger cloves, and **crush** them with the back of a knife. Set aside.
4. Preparing the steak

  - For ribeye (see [How to choose different cuts of steak](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/72352426)), insert kitchen scissors vertically into the connective tissue in the center of the ribeye and make 1-2 snips. This is because the "eye" of the ribeye (this piece of connective tissue) contracts sharply at high temperatures, deforming the steak so it can't heat evenly.
  - For sirloin, if you don't like the fat along the edge, you can slowly trim it off with a knife.
  - Then wrap the steak in paper towels to absorb all the moisture on it, until lifting the steak off the board leaves no obvious wet mark.

### Cooking

 Note: this process is for 2-3cm thick steaks cooked medium to medium-well. If you prefer a different doneness, consider increasing or decreasing the searing time in the first half by 20-30%.

1. Turn the stove on high (see the appendix for an explanation of heat levels), add olive oil to the pan, and heat for 15-30 seconds.
2. Season the steak: sprinkle sea salt and black pepper evenly over the entire surface and rub them in by hand so the salt and pepper grains are pressed into the meat as much as possible. Note that the steak **must go into the pan immediately after seasoning; seasoning in advance is not recommended**. This is because salt on the surface draws moisture out of the beef, which affects cooking and loses flavor.
3. Heat the pan until the oil is at 6/10 to 8/10 heat (see [Oil Temperature Guide](../../tips/advanced/oil-temperature-guide-and-conversion-table.md)), and slowly lay the steaks in the pan, moving away from you.
4. If you're using potatoes as a side, put them in a bowl and microwave for 10 minutes, or boil them for 3-5 minutes.
5. Sear one side for 1.5 minutes (this time is for steaks about 2-3cm thick. If your steak is thinner, consider cutting this to 1 minute. If it's thicker, consider increasing it to 2-2.5 minutes.)
6. Flip and sear for another 1.5 minutes.

  - For sirloin and filet mignon, stand the steak up on its edge with chopsticks or a spatula and sear the sides for about 30 seconds.

7. Switch the stove to medium heat, then **quickly add** the butter, garlic and thyme in that order. Use the spatula to push the butter around the pan until it has completely melted. Press the garlic under the steak to add flavor.
8. **Tilt the pan on the stove** so all the fat runs to one side.
9. Place the herbs and garlic on top of the steak, and use the tablespoon to scoop up the fat from the pan and keep pouring it over the steak. Continue for 30 seconds, then flip the steak and repeat for another 30 seconds.

  - You can judge the current heat by watching how the fat behaves when poured over the steak. If it bubbles when it hits the steak and the fat hasn't turned dark brown, the temperature is right.
  - If your steak wasn't patted dry, was thawed in a way not recommended, or was salted in advance, a lot of water will appear in the pan at this point, and the steak ends up being "boiled", losing flavor.
  - If lots of fat bubbles appear on the steak's surface and the fat turns dark brown, the temperature is too high; take the pan off the stove, wait a moment, then continue basting.

10. Transfer the steak to the board, wrap it in aluminum foil, and wait 5-10 minutes. This step (called resting) lets the steak's own heat keep cooking it and locks in the juices.
11. Put the sides (potatoes, tomatoes, mushrooms) in the pan and fry over medium heat for 5 minutes.
12. Take out the steak and cut it into 1.5cm wide strips; now you can see the doneness of the beef.
13. Take out the sides, plate, and drizzle with the remaining fat from the pan or steak sauce (optional)

## Additional Notes

### Heat Control

Searing steak is the art of controlling heat. Space is limited, so here are just a few simple ways to quantify heat and the heat-related terms used in this guide. For more, see [Oil Temperature Guide](../../tips/advanced/oil-temperature-guide-and-conversion-table.md).

- For induction cooktops:
  - High heat: 1.8kW - 2.2kW.
  - Medium heat: 800W - 1.4kW.  
  - Low heat: 200W - 600W.
- For gas stoves:
  - High heat: turn the valve to 70%-90% of maximum flow.
  - Medium heat: turn the valve to 40%-60% of maximum flow.
  - Low heat: turn the valve to 10%-30% of maximum flow.

### About Seasoning

Steak seasoning doesn't have to follow this guide exactly. Generally, garlic, black pepper and butter are essential, but if you don't like the flavor of rosemary, thyme or other mint-family herbs, feel free to try other steak seasonings. There are also many barbecue seasoning salts on the market. I once came across a Chinese-Western fusion approach: frying sliced bird's eye chilies and garlic slices in olive oil and pouring it over the steak at the end as seasoning, which also has a unique flavor.

If you follow this guide and find problems or possible improvements to the process, please open an Issue or Pull request.
