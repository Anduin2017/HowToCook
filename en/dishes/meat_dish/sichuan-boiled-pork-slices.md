# How to Make Sichuan Boiled Pork Slices

Sichuan boiled pork slices (shuizhu roupian) is a classic Sichuan dish that's numbing, spicy and fragrant, with silky pork slices and crisp vegetables—excellent with rice. Pork tenderloin provides high-quality protein, and with bean sprouts and other vegetables it's nutritionally balanced. The method is somewhat laborious, especially the patient marinating of the pork and making the "knife-cut chili"; beginners need about 1 to 2 hours.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 361 kcal

## Calculation

- Pork tenderloin 300g
- Green onion 2 stalks
- Ginger 10g
- Garlic 20g
- Pickled red chilies 20g (0-40 g depending on spice tolerance)
- Garlic sprouts 2 stalks
- Celery 3 stalks
- Red oil doubanjiang 5ml
- Chicken bouillon powder 1.5g
- Light soy sauce 5g
- Salt 5g
- Ground pepper 2g
- Cooking wine 3g
- Egg white 1
- Potato starch 7g
- Vegetable oil 280g (depending on the situation; add another 100g if you want it oilier)
- Rapeseed oil 200g (depending on the situation; add another 100g if you want it oilier)
- Mung bean sprouts 100g
- Romaine lettuce 1 head
- Granulated sugar 1g
- Dried bird's eye chilies 20g (0-40g depending on spice tolerance)
- Green Sichuan peppercorns 5g (add another 5g if you like it more numbing)

## Steps

1. Cut the tenderloin into small blocks, then into 2 mm thin slices (adjust to your preferred texture). Put them in a bowl and rinse twice with water to remove blood and impurities, then squeeze dry and set aside.
2. Add 1.5g of salt, 1g of ground pepper, 5g of light soy sauce and 3g of cooking wine to the bowl, then stir in one direction for 2 minutes to let the flavors soak in.
3. In another bowl, add one egg white and 7g of potato starch, stir evenly in one direction, and pour it over the pork

  - Stir evenly in one direction again (this forms a smooth coating on the pork, making it more tender)
  - Finally add 30g of vegetable oil and stir gently (to keep the slices from sticking together).

4. Take 100g of mung bean sprouts, 1 head of romaine lettuce (cut into strips), 3 stalks of celery cut into short segments, and 2 garlic sprouts smashed and cut into segments.
5. Mince 20g of garlic, a small piece of ginger and 20g of pickled red chilies.
6. Take 15g of dried bird's eye chilies and 3g of green Sichuan peppercorns. Coat the wok with oil, and once the oil is slightly hot, pour out the excess, leaving 50g. Add the dried chilies and peppercorns and fry over low heat until fragrant, being careful not to burn them (stop when they're about to turn dark), then turn them out onto the board and chop finely.
7. Heat the wok, add 100g of vegetable oil and heat to 6/10 heat, add 2g of green Sichuan peppercorns and dried chilies and fry until fragrant. Add the vegetables and 1g of salt, stir-fry until just cooked, and put them in a bowl as the base.
8. Wash the wok, add 150g of vegetable oil and heat to 6/10 heat, add the prepared ginger, garlic and pickled chilies, fry until fragrant, then add 10g of doubanjiang and fry over low heat until fragrant and the oil turns red.
9. Add 800 ml of water (adjust as needed), bring to a boil on high heat, then turn to low heat and season: add 2.5g of salt, 1.5g of chicken bouillon, 1g of sugar for savoriness, 1g of ground pepper, and 5g of starch slurry (adjust as needed) to thicken the broth a little.
10. When the broth boils, turn to low heat and add the marinated pork slices one by one, then turn to medium heat to cook them. Gently push with the spatula to keep them from sticking. When the broth boils again and the pork is cooked, take the pork out and put it on top of the vegetables in the bowl, then pour the broth over (not above the level of the food).
11. Sprinkle the knife-cut chili, minced garlic and chopped green onion evenly over the bowl.
12. Wash the wok, add 200g of rapeseed oil and heat to 7/10 heat, then pour it all at once evenly over the pork in the bowl (be careful). Delicious!

## Additional Notes

- Choose the base vegetables to your taste (mushrooms, napa cabbage, Indian lettuce, etc.).
- If making knife-cut chili is too much trouble, use dried chili segments and green Sichuan peppercorns directly instead.
- Pay special attention to marinating the pork (stir in one direction, remove the blood thoroughly) to keep it tender (mix the egg white and starch evenly).
- Sichuan boiled beef can also be made this way.
