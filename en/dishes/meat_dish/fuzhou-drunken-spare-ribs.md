# How to Make Fuzhou Drunken Spare Ribs

Drunken spare ribs (zui paigu) are a famous traditional Fuzhou dish: crispy outside and tender inside, sweet, tangy and appetizing, with the distinctive savory aroma of fish sauce. Pork ribs are rich in protein and calcium, nutritious and satisfying. This dish requires mastering battering and double-frying, so it's better suited to people with some cooking experience. It takes about 40 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 934 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Pork spare ribs
* Granulated sugar
* Cooking oil
* Fish sauce
* Sesame seeds
* Ketchup
* Zhenjiang black vinegar
* Garlic
* Green onion
* Sweet potato starch
* Egg yolk

## Calculation

Per portion:

* Pork spare ribs 200 g
* Granulated sugar 10 g
* Cooking oil 500 ml
* Fish sauce 5 ml
* Sesame seeds 5 g
* Ketchup 5 g
* Black vinegar 5 ml
* Garlic 2 cloves
* Green onion 1 stalk
* Sweet potato starch 30 g
* Egg yolk 1

## Steps

1. Add 5 g of sweet potato starch and water to the ribs and mix, rinse 2-3 times, then put them in a large bowl
2. Add the fish sauce, sweet potato starch and egg yolk to the ribs and mix thoroughly
3. Add the ribs to the hot oil one at a time (never dump in the whole bowl), making sure they don't stick together
4. Once all are in, flip the pieces back and forth with a spoon every 30 s until the ribs are golden on the surface
5. Take out the ribs, and after one minute put them back in the oil and keep frying until the surface is golden brown, then remove to a large bowl
6. In a small bowl, mix the minced garlic, black vinegar, sugar, fish sauce, ketchup, chopped green onion and sesame seeds evenly, then pour in 5 ml of hot oil
7. Pour the sauce over the ribs, mix thoroughly, and transfer to a plate

## Additional Notes

* Fish sauce, also called fish soy sauce, is a common seasoning in Guangdong, Fujian and elsewhere. It works similarly to light soy sauce
* This time, because of the pandemic, there was no fish sauce, so dark soy sauce was used instead, which is why the ribs look dark; they're normally golden
