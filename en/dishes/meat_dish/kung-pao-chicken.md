# How to Make Kung Pao Chicken

This famous Sichuan dish is known for its "scorched chili lychee" flavor: slightly sweet and sour, with the aroma of dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns, tender diced chicken and crunchy peanuts for layered textures. It's rich in high-quality protein and unsaturated fatty acids, appetizing and great with rice. There's a simple version and an advanced version; it's fairly beginner-friendly as long as you plan the marinating time ahead. It takes about 1.5 hours from prep to plate.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1790 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Pistol-cut chicken leg (or chicken breast)
- Scallion (large, Chinese leek-type)
- Dried chili peppers (or erjingtiao chilies)
- Roasted peanuts
- Light soy sauce
- Zhenjiang black vinegar
- White sugar
- Cooking wine
- Salt
- Chicken bouillon powder
- Starch
- Vegetable oil
- Sesame oil

### Optional Ingredients

- Shaanxi chili oil (youpo lazi)
- Celtuce (stem lettuce)
- Raw peanuts
- Doubanjiang (chili bean paste)

## Calculation

Note: this dish is a one-person version by default, though it's enough for two; in theory, for more people you just multiply.

- Required ingredients
  - Pistol-cut chicken leg (or chicken breast) = 1 (about 350g)
  - Scallion = 1 stalk (about 180g)
  - Roasted peanuts = 150g
  - Ginger slices = 10g
  - Dried chili peppers (or erjingtiao) = 10g (if using erjingtiao, about 4)
  - Light soy sauce = 10g
  - White sugar = 2g
  - Salt = 2g
  - Vegetable oil = 20g
  - Starch = 15g
  - Cooking wine = 15g
- Advanced ingredients
  - Dark soy sauce = 5g
  - Sichuan peppercorns = 5g
  - Black vinegar = 5g
  - Chicken bouillon powder = 2g
  - Sesame oil = 10g
  - Starch (for thickening) = 10g
  - Doubanjiang = 10g
- Optional ingredients
  - Celtuce = about 250g
  - Shaanxi chili oil = 5g

Use the above to calculate the proportions of ingredients you plan to use, adjusting to taste.

## Steps

### Simple Version

1. Debone the chicken leg with scissors, pound the meat side once with the back of a knife, cut into strips and then into 1.5cm cubes; soak in water for 10 minutes, drain and set aside (if using chicken breast, you can go straight to dicing and the following steps)
2. Put the green part of the scallion and 5g of ginger slices in a bowl and pour in 50g of boiling water to make scallion-ginger water; cut the white part of the scallion into 1.5cm rounds; microwave the peanuts on high for 5 minutes to dry them out. Set all aside
3. Add 2g of salt, 5g of dark soy sauce, 15g of cooking wine and 15g of starch to the chicken and mix until slightly dry; slowly add some of the scallion-ginger water, stirring the chicken until it's sticky; seal with plastic wrap and marinate in the refrigerator for 1 hour
4. Cut the dried chilies into segments; heat the wok on high, then turn to low; toast the dried chilies until slightly scorched, then remove; toast the Sichuan peppercorns until fragrant, then remove
5. Turn to high heat, add 20g of vegetable oil, and at 7/10 heat (bubbles form around a bamboo chopstick) add the chicken. Fry until the top starts turning white, flip with the spatula, fry for 30s, then stir-fry evenly
6. Add the scallion rounds and stir-fry, add the remaining scallion-ginger water, topping it up with a little water (must be hot) to make 100g; cover and braise over medium-low heat for 2 minutes;
7. Turn to high heat, add the roasted peanuts, dried chilies and Sichuan peppercorns; add 2g of chicken bouillon, 5g of black vinegar and 2g of white sugar, and stir-fry evenly;
8. Mix 10g of starch with 50g of water to make a slurry, add it to the wok, stir-fry evenly, and reduce to your preferred thickness
9. Turn off the heat, drizzle with 10g of sesame oil, and serve

### A Slightly More Complex but Even More Mouthwatering Version

Compared to the simple version, erjingtiao chilies and raw peanuts are recommended here, and celtuce is added to complement the crunch of the peanuts and the tenderness of the chicken

1. Peel the celtuce and cut into 1cm cubes, set aside;
2. Cut the erjingtiao chilies into 1cm segments;
3. Debone the chicken leg with scissors, pound the meat side once with the back of a knife, cut into strips and then into 1.5cm cubes; soak in water for 10 minutes, drain and set aside (if using chicken breast, you can go straight to dicing and the following steps);
4. Put the green part of the scallion and 5g of ginger slices in a bowl and pour in 50g of boiling water; cut the white part of the scallion into 1.5cm rounds and set aside
5. Add 2g of salt, 5g of dark soy sauce, 15g of cooking wine and 15g of starch to the chicken and mix until slightly dry; slowly add some of the scallion-ginger water, stirring the chicken until it's sticky; seal with plastic wrap and marinate in the refrigerator for 1 hour
6. Over medium heat, add 20g of vegetable oil and the raw peanuts and stir-fry until slightly scorched on the surface. Remove the peanuts but leave the oil in the wok;
7. Keep heating, and at 7/10 heat (bubbles form around a bamboo chopstick) add the chicken and the doubanjiang and stir-fry for about 1 minute;
8. Add the prepared celtuce and keep stir-frying for 1 minute;
9. Add the scallion rounds and stir-fry, add the remaining scallion-ginger water, topping it up with a little water (must be hot) to make 100g; add the erjingtiao chili segments; cover and braise over medium-low heat for 2 minutes;
10. Turn to high heat, add the peanuts you set aside and the Sichuan peppercorns; add 2g of chicken bouillon, 5g of black vinegar and 2g of white sugar, and stir-fry evenly;
11. Mix 10g of starch with 50g of water to make a slurry, add it to the wok, stir-fry evenly, and reduce to your preferred thickness
12. Turn off the heat, drizzle with 10g of sesame oil and 5g of Shaanxi chili oil, toss for another 10s, and serve
