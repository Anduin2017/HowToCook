# How to Make Shangzhi Pork

Shangzhi pork is a traditional specialty from Shang County in Shaanxi: reddish and glossy, soft and tender, rich without being greasy, with the unique fresh aroma of shangzhi fern. Pork provides high-quality protein and fat, while shangzhi, a fern vegetable, adds dietary fiber and various trace elements. The process is laborious and demands knife skills and heat control, so it's a challenge for people with some cooking experience. It takes about 2.5 hours in total from prep to finished dish.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 1621 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Skin-on pork belly (boneless)
- Shangzhi (also called royal fern, Osmunda japonica, a fern with edible young fronds)
- Scallion
- Ginger
- Star anise
- Honey
- Vinegar
- Cooking wine
- MSG
- Soy sauce
- Thin egg crêpe
- Fine salt
- Chicken stock
- Sesame oil
- Rendered lard

## Calculation

Per portion: for two people

- Skin-on pork belly: 500 grams
- Shangzhi: 50 grams
- Scallion: 10 grams
- Ginger: 2 grams
- Star anise: 3
- Honey: 15 grams
- Vinegar: 5 grams
- Cooking wine: 15 grams
- MSG: 1.5 grams
- Soy sauce: 10 grams
- Thin egg crêpe: one, about 15 grams
- Fine salt: 1 gram
- Chicken stock: 200 grams
- Sesame oil: 10 grams
- Rendered lard: 2000 grams (about 60 grams is actually used)

## Steps

1. Scrape and wash the pork clean, boil it in a pot until about 60% cooked (it turns white), take it out, and while hot, brush the skin with honey and vinegar.
2. Put the lard in the wok and heat over high heat to 8/10 heat (about 200 degrees; lots of blue smoke on the surface and the oil is calm). Put the pork in skin side down and fry until golden red, then put it back into the cooled pot from the boiling step to soak until soft. Place it on the board and cut into slices 3 cun (10 cm) long and 2 fen (0.6 cm) thick. Keeping them skin side down, arrange them neatly in a steaming bowl.
3. Cut 5 grams of scallion into 2.4 cm segments and 5 grams into 2.4 cm diagonal slices. Peel and wash the ginger, slice 1.5 grams and mince 5 grams. Cut the egg crêpe into isosceles triangles with 2.4 cm sides.
4. Boil the shangzhi in boiling water until soft, take it out, remove the tough stems and debris, rinse clean, and cut into 3 cm segments. Put it in a bowl, add soy sauce (5 grams), fine salt (1 gram) and lard (10 grams), mix well, and spread it over the pork slices. Separately, put chicken stock (100 grams) in a small bowl, add soy sauce (5 grams), fine salt (0.5 grams) and cooking wine (15 grams), stir well, and pour it into the steaming bowl. Add the ginger slices, scallion segments and star anise, and steam over high heat for about half an hour, then turn to low heat and keep steaming for about one and a half hours. When very tender, take it out, pick out the ginger, scallion and star anise, pour off and strain the juices, and turn the pork out into a soup plate.
5. Put chicken stock (100 grams) in the wok, add the strained juices, and bring to a boil over high heat. Add the minced ginger, scallion slices and MSG and stir well, add the egg crêpe pieces, drizzle with sesame oil, and pour it over the soup plate. Done.

## Additional Notes

- Shangzhi is a local specialty from a small area that isn't particularly well known; I at least have never seen it anywhere else. You can substitute other fern vegetables when making this.
