# How to Make Dry-Fried Spring Chicken

![Finished dry-fried spring chicken](../../../dishes/meat_dish/干煸仔鸡/干煸仔鸡成品.jpg)

This is an innovative sweet-and-spicy Sichuan dish from the Peking University canteen: the chicken is marinated and then fried, crispy outside and tender inside, with soft potatoes and crisp green peppers, all coated in a glossy red sweet chili sauce—savory, sweet, slightly spicy and very appetizing. It's rich in protein, carbohydrates and vitamins. It involves deep-frying and making a sauce, which is a little challenging for beginners, but following the steps will get you there. It takes about 1 hour in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1300 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Chicken legs
- Potatoes
- Green bell pepper
- Garlic
- Salt
- Ground pepper
- Light soy sauce
- Dark soy sauce
- Cooking wine
- Starch
- Pixian red oil doubanjiang (note the difference: not the brownish-yellow kind of bean paste)
- White sugar
- Crushed Sichuan peppercorns

## Calculation

For 1-2 people:

- Chicken leg meat 400g
- Potatoes 200g
- Green bell pepper 60g
- Garlic cloves 10g
- Salt 3g
- Chicken bouillon powder 3g (optional)
- Ground pepper 2g
- Light soy sauce 5g
- Dark soy sauce 2g
- Cooking wine 5g
- Starch 20g
- Pixian red oil doubanjiang 40g
- White sugar 30g
- Crushed Sichuan peppercorns 2g

## Steps

1. Debone the chicken legs (skip this step if using boneless thigh fillets), pound the meat with the back of a knife, and cut into 2cm pieces.
2. Add the salt, chicken bouillon (optional), ground pepper, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and cooking wine to the chicken and mix until sticky, then add the starch and mix well. Add a little cooking oil to keep the pieces from sticking together, and marinate for 30 minutes.
3. Peel the potatoes, cut into 2cm pieces, boil in boiling water for 5 minutes, then drain well so they don't spatter when frying.
4. Seed the green pepper, cut into 2cm pieces, and place in a spider strainer.
5. Add plenty of oil to the wok (enough to cover the ingredients, depending on the shape of the wok). When the oil reaches 180℃, add the potatoes and fry for 3 minutes, then remove.
6. When the oil is back up to 180℃, add the chicken and fry for 2 minutes, then remove.
7. When the oil is back up to 180℃, fry the chicken again for 1 minute, then remove.
8. When the oil is back up to 180℃, fry the potatoes again for 1 minute, then pour the oil and potatoes out through the spider strainer holding the green peppers, so the hot oil just cooks the peppers.
9. Add 5ml of cooking oil to the wok, fry the garlic over low heat until golden, add the red oil doubanjiang and fry until fragrant, add the sugar and stir until melted, add the crushed Sichuan peppercorns and 40ml of water, and keep stirring until the sauce is thick.
10. Add the fried chicken, potatoes and green peppers, toss evenly, and serve.

## Additional Notes

- If you don't want to use so much oil for deep-frying, you can pan-fry the chicken and potatoes in less oil instead. It takes longer and slightly affects the texture, but won't affect the overall flavor.
