# How to Make Cold-Eating Spicy Rabbit

This is a classic Zigong "salt merchant" dish from Sichuan (lengchi tu): numbing, spicy, dry and fragrant, with firm, chewy rabbit meat that hits you with heat first and savoriness after, with a long finish. Rabbit is high in protein and low in fat, rich in lecithin and various amino acids, nutritious and easy to digest. The process involves many spices and strict heat control, which is a challenge for beginners. Prep and frying take about 1.5 hours, but it tastes even better after resting overnight, so it's worth making ahead.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 590 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Rabbit
- Salt
- MSG
- Oyster sauce
- Cooking wine
- Garlic
- Ginger
- Green onion / scallion / onion
- Dried chili peppers
- Green Sichuan peppercorns
- Star anise
- Cassia bark
- Bay leaves
- Sand ginger (shannai)
- White cardamom
- Fennel seeds
- White sesame seeds

## Calculation

In the formulas below, "jin of rabbit" means the weight of the rabbit in jin (1 jin = 500g).

- Salt = jin of rabbit * 2 grams
- MSG = jin of rabbit * 1 gram
- Oyster sauce = jin of rabbit * 5 grams
- Cooking wine = jin of rabbit * 10 grams
- Oil = jin of rabbit * 0.9 ～ 1 liter
- Garlic = jin of rabbit * half a bulb
- Ginger = same as garlic
- Green onion / scallion / onion in total = jin of rabbit * 15 grams
- Dried chili peppers = the total volume of chili segments equals the total volume of rabbit
- Green Sichuan peppercorns = for 3 jin (1.5kg) of rabbit, one full small rice bowl of peppercorns
- Star anise = jin of rabbit * 1
- Cassia bark = jin of rabbit * one piece the length of your thumb
- Bay leaves = jin of rabbit * 5
- Sand ginger = jin of rabbit * one piece the size of a soybean
- White cardamom = jin of rabbit * 2 pods
- Fennel seeds = jin of rabbit * 15 grams
- White sesame seeds = jin of rabbit * 25 grams

Use the above to calculate the proportions of ingredients you plan to use.

## Steps

1. Peel and mince the garlic and ginger. Rinse the star anise, cassia bark, bay leaves, sand ginger, white cardamom and fennel seeds and set aside.
2. Cut the dried chilies into 2 cm segments with scissors, rinse and set aside.
3. Wash the green onion / scallion / onion; cut the onion into small pieces.
4. Chop the rabbit into 2 cm pieces, season with the salt, cooking wine and MSG, and marinate for 15 minutes.
5. Pour the oil into the wok. When it reaches 4/10 heat, add the green onion / scallion / onion and fry over medium-low heat until fragrant. When they're slightly charred, scoop them out.
6. Turn to high heat to raise the oil temperature. When it reaches 8/10 heat, add the rabbit, then turn to medium-low heat while frying. When the rabbit is lightly golden brown, scoop it out.
7. Raise the oil temperature and add the dried chilies, green Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, cassia bark, bay leaves, sand ginger, white cardamom and fennel seeds; turn to low heat and fry until the chili segments are crisp.
8. Return the rabbit to the wok, add the oyster sauce, and stir-fry for a few minutes.
9. Turn off the heat, add the garlic, ginger and white sesame seeds, and stir-fry evenly.
10. Let it rest overnight for even more flavor.

## Additional Notes

In step 6, note: the oil should cover the rabbit; if it doesn't, add more right away.

In step 7, note: chilies burn extremely easily and turn black when burnt, and this happens very fast, so you must use low heat. Basically, once there's not much steam coming off, you can add the rabbit back.
