# How to Make Sweet and Sour Pork Ribs

Sweet and sour pork ribs are a traditional dish that's sweet, tangy, crispy outside and tender inside, and very popular. Pork ribs are rich in high-quality protein and calcium and are quite nourishing. This dish involves several steps—blanching, deep-frying, making caramel and reducing the sauce—with some demands on heat and seasoning, so it's not especially suited to beginners, but following the optimized process gives a high success rate. It takes about 45 minutes.

## Ingredients and Tools

- Pork ribs
- Granulated sugar
- Cooking oil
- Light soy sauce
- Oyster sauce
- Dark soy sauce
- Chicken bouillon powder
- Ginger slices
- Sesame seeds
- Ketchup
- Zhenjiang black vinegar
- Five-spice powder

## Calculation

Per portion:

- Pork ribs 300 g
- Granulated sugar 30 g
- Cooking oil 300–350 ml (adjust to the size of your wok; for deep-frying, enough to cover 2/3 of the ribs)
- Light soy sauce 5 ml
- Oyster sauce 5 ml
- Dark soy sauce 5 ml
- Chicken bouillon powder 2 g
- Ginger 2 slices
- Sesame seeds 2 g
- Ketchup 10 g
- Black vinegar 5 ml
- Five-spice powder 0.5 g

## Steps

1. Put the ribs and ginger slices in cold water and heat on high until the water boils and lots of foam appears, then turn to medium heat. While it keeps boiling, turn to low heat and blanch for 2–3 minutes, then remove and set aside.
2. Rinse the ribs repeatedly with boiling water 2–3 times to make sure all the blood foam is removed.
3. Pour enough cooking oil into the wok for deep-frying (depending on the size of the wok; about 300 ml is recommended for a typical household). When the oil reaches about 170°C, add the ribs and fry for 3–5 minutes until the surface is lightly golden, then remove and drain.
4. In a clean wok over low heat, heat 50 ml of hot water, add 30 g of sugar, and stir gently until the sugar has completely dissolved and turned slightly pale yellow. The key here is to watch the sugar dissolving; don't rely too much on color changes.
5. Add the fried ribs to the wok with the sugar syrup and stir-fry quickly for 30 seconds, then add 5 ml of black vinegar, 5 ml of light soy sauce, 5 ml of oyster sauce, 2 g of chicken bouillon, 10 g of ketchup and 0.5 g of five-spice powder in order, stir-fry for another 30 seconds so the seasonings evenly coat the ribs, then add boiling water to just cover the ribs.
6. Bring the liquid to a boil over high heat, add 5 ml of dark soy sauce for color, and quickly reduce the sauce. If the rib pieces are large, you can turn to low heat and braise for 5–10 minutes so they absorb more flavor. Don't stew over medium heat for 20 minutes, as that ruins the texture.
7. Plate, sprinkle with 2 g of sesame seeds, and enjoy.

## Additional Notes

- When frying the ribs, you can lightly dust them with a little dry starch for extra crispness.
- Pay attention to heat changes when blanching: put the ribs in cold water, use high heat until the water boils and foams, then turn to medium, then to low to blanch. Rinse with boiling water afterward to avoid a big temperature difference making the meat tough.
- The key in step 4 is to make sure the sugar has completely dissolved; don't rely too much on the color.
- When reducing the sauce, stir-fry quickly so the ribs are evenly coated; avoid long simmering, which ruins the texture.
