# How to Make Taiwanese Braised Pork Rice

Taiwanese braised pork rice (lu rou fan) is a fragrant Taiwanese home-style dish: tender, well-seasoned strips of pork in a glossy amber sauce, poured over hot rice for a mouthful of rich flavor. It's rich in high-quality protein, collagen and carbohydrates for a quick energy boost. It's very beginner-friendly: just prep the ingredients and manage the heat. The whole process takes about 1.5 hours, of which only half an hour is hands-on; the rest is slow simmering on low heat.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 1495 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Shallots
- Skin-on pork belly (ground pork can be used instead)
- Eggs (optional)
- Cooking oil
- Light soy sauce
- Rice wine (cooking wine can be used instead)
- Garlic
- Bay leaves
- Star anise
- Rock sugar
- Ground white pepper
- Five-spice powder (optional)
- Cooked rice

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. The amounts below are roughly for 2~3 people.

- Shallots 25 g
- Skin-on pork belly 500g
- Eggs 4 (change the number of eggs as you like)
- Cooking oil 15 ml
- Light soy sauce 75 ml
- Rice wine 10 + 25 ml
- Garlic 25 g
- Bay leaves 2
- Star anise 1
- Rock sugar 20 g
- Ground white pepper 6 g
- Five-spice powder 6 g
- Cooked rice (according to appetite)

## Steps

1. Cut the skin-on pork belly into thin strips $0.7cm (length) \times 0.7cm (width) \times 2.5cm (height)$

![Example pork strips](../../../dishes/meat_dish/台式卤肉饭/1.jpg)

2. Mince the shallots and garlic and set aside
3. Hard-boil the eggs, peel them, and make cuts in the whites with a knife (so they absorb flavor). Set aside.
4. Heat the wok on **high heat** and add 15 ml of cooking oil, just enough to coat the bottom.

  - The oil keeps the pork belly from sticking when it first goes in. Wait 5s for the oil to heat up a little.

5. Add the pork strips and stir-fry until the meat turns slightly white, then drizzle 10ml of rice wine around the edge of the wok. Keep stir-frying until the pork belly stops releasing fat.
6. Add the minced shallots to the wok and stir-fry for 1 minute to bring out the fried shallot aroma.
7. Add the minced garlic to the wok and stir-fry for 30 seconds.
8. Push the pork to the side, add the rock sugar and heat until it melts, bubbles and turns into caramel, then toss the pork in it so the caramel coats it evenly.
9. Add the light soy sauce and stir-fry until fragrant.
10. Splash in 25 ml of rice wine, add water to cover the pork, add the ground white pepper, five-spice powder, star anise, bay leaves and boiled eggs. Once boiling, turn to low heat and braise for 1 hour.

  - Note: different soy sauces vary in saltiness, so be sure to taste while braising. If it's too sweet, add soy sauce; if too salty, add a little sugar or water to adjust.
  - While simmering on low heat, stir and check every 15 minutes to prevent sticking.
  - If it gets too dry, add 100ml of water and keep simmering.

11. After 1 hour, turn to high heat and reduce the sauce until thick and a glossy amber color. Done.
12. When the braising is done, serve a bowl of rice, pour the tender braised pork over the rice, add a braised egg, and enjoy.

## Additional Notes

- For cutting the pork belly into strips, the definition is: place the pork belly skin side up on the table. The plane of the skin is the plane formed by length and width; perpendicular to it is height. The final strips should be thin strips with alternating fat and lean. (Note: the strips must be thin, i.e. small in length and width. Thin strips braise into tender, melting pieces more easily.)
- Tips for cutting pork belly into strips:
  - If the pork belly is **not frozen**, put it in the **freezer for 30 minutes** before cutting.
  - If it's **frozen pork belly**, leave it at **room temperature** for **30 minutes** before cutting.
  - When cutting, use light pressure and quickly saw the blade against the meat; this makes it easier to cut and preserves the layered fat-and-lean structure of the pork belly.
