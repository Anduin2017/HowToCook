# How to Make Southern-Style Red-Braised Pork

This red-braised pork is glossy red when finished: the fat melts in your mouth, the lean is tender rather than dry, savory with a touch of sweetness—a typical southern Chinese sweet-leaning flavor. Pork belly provides high-quality protein and plenty of collagen, and the spices remove gaminess and warm the stomach. It requires making caramel and slow braising, but every step is broken down clearly, so beginners can succeed by following along. It's on the table in about 1.5 hours.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 3248 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Note: if possible, sharpen your knife as much as you can.
* Tool: `pot` (a clay pot is best, aluminum second; a pressure cooker also works; preferably not iron or copper)
* Main ingredient: `pork belly`
* Supporting ingredients: `oil`, `rock sugar`, `dark soy sauce`, `cooking wine`, `bay leaves`, `star anise`, `ginger`, `salt`, `green onion`, `boiling water`, `cold water`, `garlic`, `Sichuan peppercorns`

## Calculation

One portion is just enough for 2-3 people (for 1 person, halve all amounts).

- Pork belly: 1000g (about 2 jin). *Note: raw meat shrinks and loses a lot of fat when cooked; this amount yields enough meat for 2-3 people with normal appetites. For just 1 person, halve all ingredients.*
- Oil: 100-150ml; vegetable oil, lard or peanut oil all work
- Ginger: 6 slices
- Green onion: about 30g (3-4 stalks), white parts cut into segments
- Garlic: about 25g (5-6 cloves), cut in half
- Rock sugar: 50g (about 15 pieces of broken single-crystal rock sugar, each about the size of a thumbnail). If you already used 50g making the caramel in the previous step, this 50g is for a second seasoning during braising; adjust down to 30g depending on how much you like the "southern sweet" flavor.
- Granulated sugar: 30g
- Dark soy sauce: 15ml
- Cooking wine: 30ml in total (20ml for blanching to remove gaminess, 10ml for braising later to add flavor)
- Cold water: enough to cover the ingredients, depending on your pot size
- Boiling water: enough to cover the ingredients, depending on your pot size
- Bay leaves: 4
- Star anise: 3
- Salt: 2-3g
- Sichuan peppercorns: 10g

## Steps

It's recommended to read the [caramel color](../condiment/simple-caramel-color.md) tutorial first

1. Don't cut the `pork belly` into small pieces yet; cut it into large pieces that fit in the pot (about 5–6cm wide)
2. Slice the `ginger` (each slice about 3mm thick)
3. Bring the `boiling water` to a boil
4. Tap water is fine for the `cold water`
5. Cut the white part of the `green onion` (the `scallion whites`) into short segments (green onions are best, but large scallions also work)
6. Cut the `garlic` in half; don't smash it, or it will be hard to fish out and will affect the flavor during the final `reduction`
7. Divide the supporting ingredients into two "prep packs":

  - **Pack A (for removing gaminess)**: 2 slices of ginger, 2 green onion segments.
  - **Pack B (for braising)**: the remaining 4 slices of ginger, the scallion whites, garlic, Sichuan peppercorns, star anise and bay leaves, all together in a small bowl.

8. Put the large pieces of pork belly in a pot of cold water, add **20ml of cooking wine** and **Pack A** (2 slices of ginger, 2 green onion segments), and boil for 15 minutes to remove the blood and gaminess. Take them out and rinse clean;
9. Take out the blanched pork belly and let it cool a little (until it's not too hot to handle), then cut into cubes about **3cm × 3cm × 3cm** (after blanching the meat firms up, so it's easier to cut than raw meat and the pieces come out more uniform. 3cm cubes end up about 2.5cm when finished, the ideal bite-sized piece that also cooks through easily)
10. In another wok, make the [caramel color](../condiment/simple-caramel-color.md). Note: **for this recipe, use "Operation 2" from that guide**, i.e. when the syrup forms dense small bubbles, quickly add **Pack B** (ginger, scallion whites, garlic, Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, bay leaves) and fry until fragrant.
11. Once fragrant (within about 5-10 seconds), **don't add the boiling water yet**; instead immediately add the blanched `pork belly` and stir-fry over `high heat` so the pieces are evenly coated in caramel and render some fat. When you smell the meat, pour in boiling water to cover all the meat, cover, and braise over medium-low heat for 50-60 minutes (40 minutes in a pressure cooker);
12. Add **10ml of cooking wine**;
13. Cover and bring to a boil, then every 25 minutes open the lid and skim off the oil and foam floating on top;
14. When the water level has dropped to 3/5 of the height from the bottom of the pot to the top of the meat, turn to medium heat, fish out all the supporting ingredients except the meat and liquid, and start reducing the sauce;
15. With the lid off, serve when the sauce is almost gone and thick (remember: never reduce it completely dry);
