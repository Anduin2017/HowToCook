# How to Make Sichuan Crispy Fried Pork

Xiao su rou (小酥肉) is crispy outside and tender inside, savory with a slight numbing tingle from Sichuan pepper—a home-style fried dish that every household in Sichuan and Chongqing makes. Pork is rich in protein and fat; fried, it's fairly high in calories, so it's best as an occasional treat. There are a few more steps, and you need to master the heat for the first fry (to set the shape) and the second fry (to brown it), which is a bit challenging for beginners. It takes about 1 hour 30 minutes from start to finish.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 1939 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Skinless pork (choose fattier or leaner as you prefer)
- Vegetable oil
- Mature ginger
- Green onion
- Cooking wine
- Salt
- Thirteen-spice powder
- Ground pepper
- MSG
- Chicken bouillon powder
- Crushed Sichuan peppercorns
- Whole Sichuan peppercorns
- Light soy sauce
- Eggs
- Flour
- Sweet potato starch

## Calculation

### Main Ingredient

- Pork 500 grams

### Coating

- Eggs 2
- Flour 30 grams
- Sweet potato starch 120 grams

### Scallion-Ginger Water

- Mature ginger 20 grams
- Green onion 15 grams
- Cooking wine 15 grams
- Water 80 grams

### Marinade Seasonings

- Salt 4 grams
- Thirteen-spice powder 2 grams
- Ground pepper 3 grams
- MSG 5 grams
- Chicken bouillon powder 5 grams
- Crushed Sichuan peppercorns 3 grams
- Whole Sichuan peppercorns 3 grams
- Light soy sauce 8 grams

## Steps

### Making the Scallion-Ginger Water

1. Cut the ginger into shreds; no need to cut the green onion.
2. Add the cooking wine and water according to the amounts above.
3. Squeeze and knead by hand for 5 minutes, so the flavor of the ginger and green onion fully dissolves into the water.

### Preparing the Pork

1. Remove the skin from the pork and rinse
2. Cut into strips 8~10 cm long and 1.5 cm thick.
3. Add the salt, thirteen-spice powder, ground pepper, MSG, chicken bouillon, crushed Sichuan peppercorns, whole Sichuan peppercorns and light soy sauce according to the amounts above.
4. Pour in the scallion-ginger water you made earlier
5. Mix and knead thoroughly for 10 minutes, until the meat absorbs all the liquid and becomes sticky.
6. Cover with plastic wrap and let it rest in the refrigerator for 30 minutes.

### Coating the Pork

1. Add the flour and sweet potato starch to the marinated pork, and add the egg whites.
2. Knead thoroughly for 15 minutes.

### First Fry to Set the Shape

1. Pour vegetable oil into the wok; adjust the amount to the size of the wok, with the oil at least 3 cm deep.
2. Heat the oil to 150° over high heat, then turn to low heat to hold the temperature.
3. Use chopsticks to put the coated pork strips into the oil, shaping them as you like, and fry for 3~5 minutes to set. It's done when the color looks slightly golden and the pieces feel a little firm when turned with a spatula. The exact time depends on the size of the pieces, the oil temperature and the thickness of the coating.
4. Remove and drain.

Tip: if you're not sure about the time, fry them a bit longer, or the meat may taste a little raw. If you're inexperienced, add the strips one at a time to keep them from sticking together.

### Second Fry for the Finished Dish

1. Raise the oil temperature to 180°, add the once-fried pork strips, and fry until golden, then remove.

## Additional Notes

- When coating, make sure to knead the sweet potato starch until it breaks up completely. Don't let dry granules get wrapped inside, or they will burst in the oil!!!
- If you have too much to finish, you can fry it just once and store it in the refrigerator. When you want to eat it later, take it out and do the second fry; the texture is basically unaffected.
