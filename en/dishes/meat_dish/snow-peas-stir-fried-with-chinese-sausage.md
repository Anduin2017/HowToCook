# How to Make Snow Peas Stir-fried with Chinese Sausage

![Snow peas stir-fried with Chinese sausage](../../../dishes/meat_dish/荷兰豆炒腊肠/1.png)

Snow peas stir-fried with Chinese sausage is a fresh, savory home-style stir-fry: the fat from the sausage coats the bright green snow peas, crisp, tender and not greasy. Snow peas are rich in high-quality protein and various vitamins, which help strengthen the body's resistance to disease. It's simple and needs almost no knife skills; beginners can pick it up quickly, and it takes only about 15 minutes from prep to plate.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 606 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Snow peas
- Chinese sausage (lap cheong)
- Light soy sauce
- Cooking oil

## Calculation

The following is for one person.

Per portion:

- Snow peas about 50
- Chinese sausage about 100 g
- Cooking oil 10ml
- Light soy sauce 10ml

## Steps

1. Remove the stem ends of the snow peas; if you have time, you can remove the tail ends too

2. Before buying the sausage, ask the vendor whether it's raw or cooked. If raw, steam it in advance; if cooked, you can use it directly

3. Rinse the snow peas, then blanch them for about 45s, just until they change color. Remove, rinse in cold water and set aside

4. Heat the wok and add about 10ml of cooking oil. Wait 10 seconds for the oil to heat up

5. Add the sausage and keep stir-frying until it *curls slightly at the edges*. Be sure to **keep the heat low**—as low as it goes—or it will burn easily

6. Add the snow peas, turn to medium-high heat, stir-fry for 30s, add the light soy sauce, then stir-fry for another 20-30s

  ![Snow peas stir-fried with Chinese sausage](../../../dishes/meat_dish/荷兰豆炒腊肠/2.png)

## Additional Notes

- No salt is added because the sausage itself is seasoned, and the light soy sauce adds some saltiness too.
- Although sausage tastes good, it isn't very healthy; use it sparingly.
