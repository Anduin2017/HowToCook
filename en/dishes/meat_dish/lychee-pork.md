# How to Make Lychee Pork

Lychee pork (lizhi rou) is a sweet, tangy and crispy traditional Fuzhou dish from Fujian cuisine: pork pieces, crispy outside and tender inside, coated in a glossy red sauce, with fruit adding a fresh aroma. Lean pork is rich in high-quality protein, and pineapple contains dietary fiber and vitamins that whet the appetite and cut the richness. It involves cutting the pork, battering and frying twice, which is a little challenging but the steps are clear, suitable for food lovers with some cooking experience. It's on the table in about 45 minutes.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 681 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Lean pork
* Pineapple
* Egg
* Cooking oil
* Granulated sugar
* Starch
* Light soy sauce
* Chicken bouillon powder
* Minced ginger
* Sesame seeds
* Ketchup
* Zhenjiang black vinegar

## Calculation

Per portion:

* Fresh shiitake mushrooms 2
* Shimeji mushrooms 30 g
* Lean pork 150 g
* Pineapple 100 g
* Egg 1
* Cooking oil 500 ml
* Granulated sugar 5 g
* Starch 100 g
* Light soy sauce 5 ml
* Chicken bouillon powder 5 g
* Minced ginger 5 g
* Sesame seeds 2 g
* Ketchup 20 g
* Black vinegar 2 ml

## Steps

1. Cut the lean pork into pieces (2-3 cm each) and put them in a large bowl. Add 1 egg, 50 g of starch, 3 ml of light soy sauce and 2 g of chicken bouillon
2. Mix thoroughly until the starch coats the pork pieces (if too runny, add more starch; if too dry, add water), then add 5 ml of oil, mix thoroughly again, and set aside
3. In another bowl, add the ketchup, 3 g of chicken bouillon, 2 ml of light soy sauce, minced ginger, sugar, 10 g of starch, the black vinegar and 200 ml of cold water, mix thoroughly and set aside
4. Cut a pineapple and prepare 6 pineapple chunks (each 1.5-2 cm)
5. Heat the wok with 500 ml of oil until you hear it sizzle
6. Add the pork pieces one at a time (never dump in the whole bowl), making sure they don't stick together
7. Once all the pork is in, flip the pieces back and forth with a spoon every 30 s until golden on the surface
8. Take out the pork, and after one minute put it back in the oil and keep frying until the surface is golden brown, then remove to a large bowl
9. In a clean wok, pour in the sauce, and after 30 s add the pork and pineapple, toss thoroughly, and serve
10. Sprinkle with sesame seeds

## Additional Notes

* More often, locals in Fuzhou use water chestnuts (to cut the richness) and potatoes (to absorb oil), but these weren't available during the pandemic, so pineapple was used instead
* Fujian cuisine leans sweet; if you're not used to it, you can leave out the white sugar
* You can tap the pork pieces with a spoon and listen to the sound to tell whether they are fried through
