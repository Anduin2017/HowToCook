# How to Make Tiger-Skin Pork Knuckle

The finished dish is glossy red, and the knuckle skin shows tempting "tiger-skin" wrinkles. It's tender and flavorful, rich without being greasy, with soft lean meat—a traditional Chinese banquet centerpiece. Pork knuckle is rich in collagen and high-quality protein, which is easier to absorb after long braising and has some benefit for skin and joints. The process includes scorching the skin, deep-frying, making caramel color and pressure-cooking, which is quite challenging for beginners; try it once you have some cooking experience. It takes about 2.5 hours from prep to table.

## Steps

### Prep

1. Thaw the knuckle and soak it in water for 1 hour to remove the blood.
2. If you have a blowtorch, scorch the surface of the **knuckle skin** until **brownish black** to remove hairs and destroy the sweat glands. Don't hold it on one spot too long, to avoid burning through; stop when the skin is almost entirely brownish black.
3. If you don't have a blowtorch, heat an iron wok above 200 degrees and put the knuckle straight in. Use a spatula or chopsticks to press the skin firmly against the wok. When the skin in contact with the wok turns brown, move it and keep searing other parts until the whole knuckle has been seared. Keep an eye on the wok temperature throughout and don't let it get red hot.
4. Scrub the knuckle in water with steel wool to remove the scorched parts. After scrubbing, the knuckle looks as it did before scorching.
5. Put the knuckle in the wok and add as much cold water as possible, depending on the depth of the wok and size of the knuckle. As long as you can still lift the wok and its contents, covering at least 3/4 of the knuckle is best.

### Removing Gaminess

1. Take the white part of 1 scallion, cut into 3 segments, and add to the wok.
2. Take 3 garlic cloves, smash each with the flat of the knife, and add to the wok.
3. Add 3 grams of ginger to the wok.
4. Add 2 tablespoons of cooking wine to the wok.
5. Once the water boils, wait five minutes, then take out the knuckle, pick out all the aromatics, and save all the broth in another container.

### Deep-Frying

1. Add cold oil to the wok; using the previous water level as a reference, enough to cover at least 3/5 of the knuckle is best. Heat over medium heat.
2. When the [oil temperature](../../tips/advanced/oil-temperature-guide-and-conversion-table.md) reaches 5/10 heat, turn to low heat and add the knuckle to fry.
3. Keep yourself safe while frying.
4. While frying, use a spatula or other heat-resistant utensil to ladle the oil evenly over the parts of the knuckle not submerged. If possible, turn the knuckle every 3 minutes so it fries evenly.
5. Frying takes about 20 minutes. When the skin is entirely light brown and the lean parts are slightly charred, remove and set aside.
6. The frying oil can be used for other fried foods, but don't reuse it too many times.

### Making Caramel Color

1. Make 200ml of [caramel color](../../tips/advanced/making-caramel-color.md) and set aside.

### Braising

1. Put the knuckle in the pressure cooker and add all the broth, the caramel color, bay leaves, cinnamon bark, nutmeg, Sichuan peppercorns, star anise, dark soy sauce, light soy sauce and white vinegar. If you like it sweet, add an extra 2-3 grams of rock sugar.
2. Take the white part of 1 scallion, cut into 3 segments, and add to the pot.
3. Take 3 garlic cloves, smash each with the flat of the knife, and add to the pot.
4. Add 3 grams of ginger to the pot.
5. Close the lid and pressure cook for 40 minutes.

### Reducing the Sauce

1. While it braises, make a starch slurry: in a bowl, mix 1 tablespoon of starch with 100ml of water into a white suspension
2. When braising is done, open the pressure cooker lid and pick out all the aromatics, leaving only the knuckle.
3. Transfer the remaining broth from the pressure cooker to the wok, and the knuckle to a plate or basin
4. Put the wok on the stove over high heat. While reducing, you can dip a chopstick in the sauce to check the saltiness and add salt to taste. Note that the sauce tastes milder when there's more of it than when there's less, so keep that in mind when adding salt.
5. When the broth boils, watch how much remains
6. When less than 1/2 of the original volume of broth remains, stir the prepared starch slurry again and add half of it
7. Wait for the broth to boil, then add the remaining half
8. Wait for it to boil, then after 1-2 minutes turn off the heat. The sauce in the wok should now be reddish brown and thick
9. Spoon the sauce evenly over the knuckle, trying to cover every part. If the sauce runs out before the knuckle is fully covered, just serve; otherwise there's no need to pour on the rest—just serve.
