# How to Make Liangpi (Cold Skin Noodles)

Liangpi is a home-style snack from Shaanxi: chewy and smooth, tangy and spicy, with a rich sesame paste aroma. Sesame paste and wheat gluten (mianjin) provide plenty of plant protein and calcium, and crisp cucumber and mung bean sprouts add vitamins and dietary fiber. The overall difficulty is medium—just simple blanching, shredding and mixing sauces—and beginners can manage it calmly. It takes about 40 minutes from prep to table, perfect for summer.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 761 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Liangpi (cold skin noodles), wheat gluten (mianjin)
* Salt, chicken bouillon powder, oyster sauce, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce, sesame oil, Zhenjiang black vinegar, sesame paste (plain sesame paste is best)
* Cucumber, garlic, mung bean sprouts
* Basin, bowls, plates, garlic mortar

## Calculation

* Liangpi: 300 g/person, rounded down.
* Sesame paste: 30 g/person, rounded down.
* Cucumber 100g/person, mung bean sprouts 50g/person.

## Steps

### Prep

1. Add 500ml of water to the pot and bring to a boil.
2. Add the mung bean sprouts and boil on high heat for 60 seconds. Take them out, rinse in cold water, and set aside on a plate.
3. Julienne the cucumber and set aside on a plate
4. Peel 10g of garlic, put it in the garlic mortar with 1g of salt, and pound into a paste. Add 10g of tap water and set aside.
5. Note: store-bought liangpi usually has cooking oil on the surface; you can rinse it with tap water. Same for the gluten.
6. Note: after rinsing the gluten, squeeze out most of the water by hand (no need to squeeze too hard).

### Mixing the Salt Water

1. In a small bowl, add 3g of salt, 2g of chicken bouillon, 5g of light soy sauce, 1g of dark soy sauce, 1g of sesame oil, 2g of oyster sauce and 5g of black vinegar (adjust salt and vinegar to taste; the amounts above suit most people).
2. Add 25-35g of warm water to the seasonings (depending on how salty you like it) and stir with chopsticks until dissolved. Set aside to cool.

### Mixing the Sesame Paste

1. Note: the amounts below are for one person; for n people, multiply by n
2. Put the prepared sesame paste in a small bowl.
3. Add 4g of salt, 3g of chicken bouillon, 5g of light soy sauce, 1g of dark soy sauce and 3g of oyster sauce.
4. Stir the seasonings into the sesame paste with chopsticks.
5. Add 10g of water and stir well.
6. Repeat the previous step 2 or 3 times (depending on how thick you like the sesame paste).

### Final Steps

1. Take the small basin and add the prepared liangpi.
2. Pour in the salt water and mix with chopsticks. Then transfer to a small bowl (along with the salt water).
3. Place the bean sprouts on top of the liangpi, followed by the gluten.
4. Pour the sesame paste over the top of the gluten.
5. Sprinkle with the cucumber shreds.
6. Add chili oil if you like.
7. Your home-style liangpi, looking, smelling and tasting great, is ready!

## Additional Notes

Tastes vary by region, weather and time, so adjust the exact amounts of seasoning to your situation.
