# How to Make Masala Chai

Masala chai (Indian milk tea) is an exotic home-style drink brewed fresh from black tea, milk and a variety of spices. It's rich and smooth, with a warming spiciness from ginger, cardamom and cinnamon. Milk provides high-quality protein and calcium, and the spices help warm the body and aid digestion. The process is simple and intuitive, beginner-friendly, and takes only about 10 minutes from prep to sipping.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 135 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Black tea (CTC black tea is best, or Assam)
- Whole milk
- Ginger
- Green cardamom
- Cinnamon stick
- Cloves
- Black peppercorns
- Sugar
- Small milk pan

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make.

Per portion (about 200ml):

- Water 120ml
- Whole milk 120ml
- Black tea 8g (about 2 teaspoons)
- Ginger 5g (a piece about 1cm), crushed
- Green cardamom 2 pods, cracked open
- Cinnamon stick 1 small piece (about 2cm)
- Cloves 2
- Black peppercorns 2, crushed
- Sugar 10-15g (adjust to taste)

## Steps

1. Crush the ginger (no need to chop it finely; just crush it to release the juice)
2. Crack the cardamom pods with the back of a knife to expose the seeds inside
3. Crush the black peppercorns
4. Pour 120ml of water into the small milk pan
5. Add the crushed ginger, cardamom, cinnamon stick, cloves and black peppercorns
6. Bring to a boil over high heat, then turn to low heat and **simmer for 2 minutes** to fully release the flavor of the spices
7. Add 8g of black tea and keep simmering on low for **2 minutes**, until *the tea darkens*
8. Pour in 120ml of whole milk
9. Heat over medium heat and **watch carefully**: as soon as the chai starts *bubbling up*, turn to low heat immediately (milk boils over very easily)
10. Simmer on low for **2-3 minutes**. Meanwhile, you can lift the pan and pour it back (3-4 times) to fully combine the tea and milk
11. Add the sugar and stir to dissolve
12. Turn off the heat
13. Strain into cups through a strainer to remove the tea leaves and spices
14. Drink while hot

## Additional Notes

- CTC black tea (made by the Crush, Tear, Curl process) is the best choice for chai, because its fine granules release a strong tea flavor in a short time. Chinese Keemun or Dianhong black tea can be used instead.
- Adjust the ratio of milk to water to your preference: more milk and less water for stronger chai, more water and less milk for lighter chai.
- Ginger is the soul of masala chai; leaving it out isn't recommended.
- Indians like their chai very sweet; if you're not used to it, use less sugar.
- In India, chai isn't just a drink but a social culture. Friends' gatherings, business negotiations, even first meetings all start with a cup of chai. By some estimates, India drinks over 1 billion cups of tea every day.
- Indian street chai stalls (chai wala) usually serve tea in clay cups (kulhad), giving it a unique earthy aroma.
