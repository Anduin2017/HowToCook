# How to Make Taro Paste Mochi (Xuemeiniang)

![Finished taro paste mochi](../../../dishes/dessert/芋泥雪媚娘/芋泥雪媚娘成品.jpg)

Taro paste xuemeiniang has a soft, chewy, bouncy skin, filled with smooth, sweet taro paste and light cream for layered textures. It's a Chinese dessert born of home-style creativity. Taro and purple sweet potato provide dietary fiber and anthocyanins, while milk and whipping cream add calcium for balanced nutrition. No oven needed and the steps are simple, so it's quite beginner-friendly; even if you're not good at baking you can make it. It takes about two hours from start to finish to make little homemade treats that kids will love.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 160 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Lipu taro (just buy it online; it's affordable and fresh)
- Purple sweet potato powder
- Milk
- Glutinous rice flour
- Corn starch
- Butter
- Whipping cream
- Granulated sugar
- Food processor (an electric mixer also works)
- Sieve
- Plastic wrap
- Granulated sugar

## Calculation

- Lipu taro 200g
- Purple sweet potato powder 3g
- Milk 165g
- Glutinous rice flour A 50g
- Glutinous rice flour B 75g
- Corn starch 22g
- Butter 30g
- Whipping cream (Anchor recommended) 145g
- Granulated sugar 26g

## Steps

1. Cut the taro into chunks and boil over high heat until soft (40 minutes is enough), then put it all in the food processor
2. Add 30g of milk and 25g of whipping cream and blend into a paste
3. Add 3g of purple sweet potato powder and 18g of sugar and keep blending into a smooth taro paste
4. In another bowl, add all of glutinous rice flour B, 22g of corn starch, 135g of milk and 50g of sugar. Mix well and strain once, cover with plastic wrap, poke small holes in it, and steam over medium heat for half an hour
5. While it steams, toast glutinous rice flour A in a frying pan over low heat until slightly yellow (i.e. cooked), to use as dusting flour
6. While the glutinous rice and milk mixture (jelly-like) is still hot from steaming, add 30g of butter and knead until the dough fully absorbs it, then refrigerate for one hour
7. In another bowl, add 120g of whipping cream and 8g of sugar, whip until it holds ridges, and put it in a piping bag
8. Take the chilled dough out and knead it for 5 minutes. Divide it into 30g pieces, dust each evenly with 2g of dusting flour to prevent sticking, and roll into a circle. First pipe on 5g of cream, then add 30g of taro paste, and finally wrap the dough up like a bun (you can trim off excess dough)
9. After wrapping, dust evenly with another 2g of dusting flour to prevent sticking
10. Repeat the last two steps until the ingredients are used up

## Additional Notes

- It's best to wear non-stick gloves while making these
- They can be eaten as soon as they're made; no need to heat them again
