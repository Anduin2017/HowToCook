# How to Make Guilinggao (Herbal Jelly)

![Finished guilinggao](../../../dishes/dessert/龟苓膏/龟苓膏成品.jpg)

Guilinggao (turtle jelly) is a traditional Cantonese dessert: smooth and tender, slightly bitter with a sweet aftertaste, often eaten as a summer refreshment. In traditional Chinese food therapy it's said to clear heat, remove dampness, nourish yin and benefit the complexion, making it a healthy snack. It's simple; beginners can master it easily. You just prepare the ingredients, stir and heat, then wait about two hours for it to chill before enjoying.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 129 kcal

---

## Ingredients and Tools

- Guilinggao powder 25 grams  
- Cold water 120 ml  
- Boiling water 500 ml  
- Granulated sugar 100 grams  
- Small pot  
- Stirring tool  
- Molds or bowls

---

## Calculation

Each batch makes about 3-4 small bowls, enough for 2-4 people.

---

## Steps

1. Pour 25 grams of guilinggao powder and 120 ml of cold water into a pot and stir thoroughly until there are no lumps.
2. In another container, add 100 grams of sugar and pour in 500 ml of boiling water, stirring until the sugar has completely dissolved.
3. Slowly pour the sugar water into the guilinggao mixture, stirring immediately to keep it from clumping.
4. Heat the mixture in the pot over medium-low heat, stirring constantly to prevent sticking.
5. When the liquid thickens and starts forming small bubbles, turn off the heat.
6. Quickly pour the liquid into the molds and let it cool and set naturally. Chilling for 1~2 hours before eating gives a better texture.

---

## Additional Notes

- Don't move the containers while the guilinggao is cooling, or it may not set properly.  
- Avoid high heat when cooking, to prevent clumping or burning on the bottom.  
- Add honey, condensed milk or fruit to taste.  
- The concentration of guilinggao powder varies slightly between brands; adjust the ratio according to the package instructions.
