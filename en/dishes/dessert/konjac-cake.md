# How to Make Konjac Cake

Konjac cake is a low-calorie, gluten-free dessert that uses erythritol instead of granulated sugar. It's light and moist with a cocoa aroma, especially suitable for people trying to lose fat. It's rich in dietary fiber and protein and very filling. It takes some skill—you need to whip egg whites and use the right folding technique—but overall it takes only about half an hour, making it a quick, healthy baking option.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 234 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

### Tools

* Oven
* Electric mixer
* Metal cake pan (any shape)
* Parchment paper
* Table knife (for folding and smoothing the batter)
* Oven mitts
* [Optional] Egg separator

### Ingredients

* Eggs
* Erythritol
* Cocoa powder
* Konjac powder
* Cream of tartar

## Calculation

Per portion (9.25 × 5 × 1 inches):

* 3 eggs
* Erythritol 50g
* Cocoa powder 10g
* Konjac powder 10g
* Cream of tartar 1g

## Steps

### Preheating the Oven

1. Before making the batter, you can start preheating the oven to 350 ℉ (150 ℃) to save time

### Separating the Eggs

1. Take fresh eggs out of the refrigerator
2. Prepare two containers and wipe them dry, one for the whites and one for the yolks
3. The container for the whites may have a few drops of water, but **must not have any oil at all**; the container for the yolks must not have water droplets
4. Crack the eggs and separate the whites and yolks into the two containers, by hand or with an egg separator.
5. Don't break the yolks while separating. **The whites must not contain any yolk at all**, or whipping will be seriously affected. (The white chalazae can go in with the whites; it doesn't matter)
6. (Note: without a stand mixer, the container for the whites is also the whipping bowl. To avoid overflow, once all the whites are in, they shouldn't fill more than **1/8** of the container)

### Whipping the Whites

1. Add 1g of cream of tartar to the whites
2. With the mixer on high, whip until the whites have *large, coarse bubbles*, then add 50g of erythritol
3. With the mixer on medium-low, whip to *stiff peaks* (when you lift the whisk there are short, upright peaks; if you turn the bowl upside down, the whites stick to it and don't fall out)
4. The whites are now whipped as required

  * Keep the whisk close to the bottom of the bowl, so you don't end up with whipped foam floating on top and liquid still at the bottom)

### Adding the Other Ingredients

1. Add the separated yolks to the whipped whites and mix evenly with the mixer on medium-low
2. Add **10g of cocoa powder** and **10g of konjac powder** to the whites and fold with the table knife first, because mixing directly with the mixer would send the powder flying
3. The folding motion is:

  * Holding the spatula in your right hand, insert it into the center of the bowl down to the bottom of the batter
  * Scrape toward 8 o'clock until you hit the side of the bowl, scoop up the batter and lift it into the air, then move back to the center and drop the batter into the bowl
  * With your left hand, turn the bowl from 9 o'clock to 7 o'clock, exactly 60 degrees, completing 1 cycle
  * The speed is about 2 times per second
  * This method comes from *Kojima Rumi's Cake Classroom*. In plain words: toss it like you're stir-frying.

4. After folding, mix evenly with the mixer on low

### Baking

1. Line the pan with parchment paper, pressed against the bottom and sides
2. Pour the batter into the pan, smooth it with the table knife, then tap it a few times to get rid of large bubbles
3. Bake for 25 minutes
4. When done, put on oven mitts and take it out

### Cooling and Unmolding

1. (Optional) Drop the pan from a height to knock out the hot air inside
2. Turn the pan upside down for 10 minutes to let the cake cool

  * Unmolding before the cake has cooled will damage it
  * This may **burn your hands**; wear oven mitts

3. Unmold, cut into pieces with the table knife, and eat
