# How to Make Sugar-Frosted Taro

![Finished sugar-frosted taro](../../../dishes/dessert/反沙芋头/反沙芋头成品.jpg)

Sugar-frosted taro (fansha yutou) is a traditional Chaoshan snack: crispy outside, soft and starchy inside, sweet and fragrant, often served as an afternoon tea snack. Lipu taro is rich in dietary fiber, potassium and various vitamins, and keeps you full for a long time. It isn't hard, but you need to master the heat when cooking the syrup, so it suits beginners with a little experience. It takes about 20 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 397 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Lipu taro (just buy it online; it's affordable and fresh)
- Granulated sugar or rock sugar
- Water
- Green onion

## Calculation

- Lipu taro 200g
- Granulated sugar 30g
- Water 15g

## Steps

1. Cut the taro into long sticks (a bit on the thick side, so they don't fall apart when tossed)
2. Add enough oil to cover the taro and let it heat up (small bubbles form around a chopstick dipped in)
3. Put the taro in the oil and fry until it floats, usually when it's slightly yellow and a chopstick pokes through easily
4. Save the frying oil; don't waste it. You can use it later for stir-frying and so on
5. Now the key step: heat the sugar (30g) and water (15g) in a 2:1 ratio until it stops changing color and forms small bubbles
6. Add the chopped green onion and taro, turn off the heat and toss. As the temperature drops, the sugar crystallizes into a frosty coating
7. Plate and serve!

## Additional Notes

- Freshly made sugar-frosted taro is very hot; be careful not to burn your mouth
- Pair it with tea—ah~ how relaxing
