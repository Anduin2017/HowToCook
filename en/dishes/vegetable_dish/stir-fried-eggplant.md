# How to Make Stir-fried Eggplant

This home-style stir-fried eggplant is soft, tender and full of flavor with a rich soy aroma, and star anise makes it even more flavorful. Eggplant is rich in dietary fiber, vitamin P and potassium, which help protect the cardiovascular system. It's simple and beginner-friendly, stir-fried over high heat the whole way, and takes about twenty minutes from prep to plate.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 610 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Eggplant
- Star anise (optional)
- Dried small shrimp (optional)
- Green onion (optional)
- Minced pork (optional)
- Soy sauce
- Sugar (optional)
- Vinegar (optional)
- Rapeseed oil or peanut oil

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 1-2 people.

Total:

- Number of eggplants = portions * 1.8
- Star anise = portions * 1
- Dried small shrimp = portions * half an adult handful
- Green onion = portions * 2 stalks
- Minced pork = portions * 50 g
- Soy sauce = portions * 40 ml
- Sugar = portions * 5 g
- Vinegar = portions * 10 ml

> Note: the amounts of oil and soy sauce are also described in the steps; cross-reference them to decide how much to add

## Steps

1. Wash the eggplant and cut it into two pieces (lengthwise). Cut each piece into diamond-shaped chunks and put them in a bowl, ready to go.
2. Wash the green onion, chop it, and leave it on the board, ready to go.
3. Cut the star anise and leave it on the board, ready to go.
4. If adding minced pork, first stir-fry it in 10 ml of oil (15 ml if it's pure lean) over medium heat until it changes color (about 1 minute), then take it out and set aside.
5. Turn on the heat and heat the wok until there's no water left in it.
6. Pour cooking oil into the wok, twice as deep as needed to cover the bottom (more oil is fine, but never too little).
7. When the oil is about 60% hot, add the star anise, dried shrimp and green onion (the optional ingredients).
8. If you don't have the optional ingredients like star anise, heat the oil to 90% hot.
9. When the oil is 90% hot, pour the eggplant from the bowl into the wok and stir-fry with the spatula.
10. After stir-frying for about 40 seconds, hold the spatula level just above the wok and pour the soy sauce onto it. About 2.5 spatula-fuls per person (you can add less soy sauce but not more—it'll be too salty).
11. When the eggplant is half soft (after about 1 minute), add the pre-cooked minced pork and stir quickly to combine.
12. If you plan to add sugar and vinegar, add them now.
13. Keep stir-frying.
14. When all the eggplant has changed color and softened, take it out.

## Additional Notes

- Because of the soy sauce, this dish doesn't need salt. Taste before serving; if it's not salty enough, add a tiny bit of salt, and use more soy sauce next time
- If you added dried shrimp, you can reduce the soy sauce accordingly.
- Use high heat throughout.
- Important! Never add water, or it will become boiled eggplant with a poor texture. So more oil is fine, but never too little.
- If your home stove stops getting noticeably hotter after the oil reaches 70%, just go to the next step; no need to insist on 90%.
- Add the sugar and vinegar at the very end, stir-fry for 20 seconds and serve; don't add the vinegar too early, or the sourness evaporates.
