# How to Make Steamed Egg Custard

![Finished steamed egg custard](../../../dishes/vegetable_dish/鸡蛋羹/鸡蛋羹.jpg)

This is a simple, quick home-style steamed dish: smooth and jelly-like, pleasantly savory, often served for breakfast or as a side dish on tables across China. Eggs provide high-quality protein and lecithin and are easy to digest and absorb. It's very beginner-friendly—just a few simple steps give you a smooth, delicate custard—and takes about 15 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 168 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Eggs
- Salt
- Sesame oil
- Light soy sauce / Weijixian
- White vinegar (optional)
- Green Sichuan pepper oil (tengjiao oil, optional)

## Calculation

For 1 person:

- Eggs 2
- Salt 3g
- Sesame oil 2-4ml
- Light soy sauce / Weijixian 8ml
- White vinegar (or cooking wine) 2ml (optional)

## Steps

1. Crack two eggs into a bowl and beat
2. Add 3g of salt

  - Salt plays an important role in adding base flavor and helping it set

3. Add 2ml of white vinegar to remove any eggy smell (optional)

  - Rice vinegar also works, but its color will darken the eggs slightly
  - You can also add cooking wine, which also removes the smell without the sourness of vinegar

4. Add 1-1.5 times the egg's volume of 70-degree purified water to the bowl and stir well

  - With 1 times the water the custard is springier; with 1.5 times it's more tender

5. Strain the egg mixture and skim off the foam (optional; without straining, the steamed custard will have bubbles and look less nice)
6. Add 300-500ml of water to any pot; when it boils, put in the bowl of egg mixture

  - Cover the bowl with a lid, an upside-down grooved metal plate, or plastic wrap to keep water vapor out; this keeps it from turning honeycombed

7. Steaming (choose one of two)
1. Steam over **medium heat** for **10 minutes**, then turn off the heat and let it sit covered for another **5 minutes**.
2. Steam over **high heat** for **5 minutes**, keeping the water boiling the whole time, or extend the time. Open the lid every **2 minutes** to release steam; opening too late tends to make the custard honeycombed, while opening too early or too often means it needs to steam longer.
8. How to tell it's done?

  - Shake the bowl: when the custard is no longer liquid and wobbles like jelly, it's ready.

9. Take it out
10. Add sesame oil and light soy sauce and enjoy

  - You can also use green Sichuan pepper oil instead of sesame oil for a tingling kick

## Additional Notes

The above is the basic steamed egg method; you can build on it by adding ingredients like ham sausage, minced meat or dried tiny shrimp to enrich the custard.
