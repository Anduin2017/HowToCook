# How to Make Kabayaki-style Eggplant

Kabayaki-style eggplant (pushao qiezi) is a savory-sweet, soft home-style dish that borrows the flavor of Japanese kabayaki but is cooked with Chinese pan-frying and braising. Eggplant is rich in dietary fiber and various vitamins. The difficulty is moderate and beginners need a little patience; it takes about 30 minutes.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 326 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Eggplant
- Kabayaki sauce
- Honey
- White sugar
- Light soy sauce
- Dark soy sauce
- Cooking wine
- Water

## Calculation

- Use a different amount of oil depending on the type of pan
  - Nonstick pan: enough oil that, once it gathers into drops, it can spread over the area of the eggplant
  - Iron wok: enough oil to just cover the bottom of the wok when spread out
- 1 long eggplant, narrow at the top and wide at the bottom (don't use Zhejiang eggplant or round eggplant)
- 1 portion of kabayaki sauce
  - 20 ml honey
  - 15 ml white sugar
  - 40 ml light soy sauce
  - 10 ml dark soy sauce
  - 20 ml cooking wine
  - 100 ml water

## Steps

1. Peel the eggplant and cut it crosswise into two pieces
2. Steam for 5 minutes
3. Cut each piece open lengthwise without cutting all the way through, and score each cut face 2~3 times so it can lie flat
4. Pan-fry until golden on both sides
5. Pour the kabayaki sauce over the eggplant until it comes halfway up the eggplant
6. Fry until the underside is colored, then flip

  - Reduce the sauce at the same time; if it won't thicken, add a starch slurry (starch to water from 1:4 to 1:10, depending on how it's reducing)

7. Pour the remaining kabayaki sauce over the eggplant
8. Serve—one portion of kabayaki eggplant is done

## Additional Notes

- This "kabayaki" is actually very Chinese; Japanese kabayaki is grilled.
- Maybe the soul really is the kabayaki sauce? You can also buy ready-made kabayaki sauce.
- If it feels too rich, sprinkle with green onion cut into 2~5 mm pieces after plating, which also makes the colors less monotonous.
- For stir-fried eggplant (yuxiang eggplant, eggplant in bean sauce), Zhejiang eggplant is highly recommended: it's long and thin, perfect for roll-cut chunks, the skin is tender so it doesn't need peeling like here, and the flavor is mild.
