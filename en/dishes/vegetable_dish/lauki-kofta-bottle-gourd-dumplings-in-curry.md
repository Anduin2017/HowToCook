# How to Make Lauki Kofta (Bottle Gourd Dumplings in Curry)

This North Indian vegetarian dish mixes grated bottle gourd with gram flour into balls, deep-fries them, and simmers them in a tomato-onion curry—crispy outside, soft inside, with a rich, spicy sauce. Bottle gourd is light and moistening, and gram flour provides plant protein and dietary fiber, making this a filling, balanced vegetarian dish. It involves deep-frying and making a sauce, so there are a few more steps, but it's not hard if you follow them in order. It takes about 40 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 571 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Bottle gourd (lauki)
- Gram flour (besan)
- Onions
- Tomatoes
- Ginger
- Garlic
- Green chili
- Cilantro
- Turmeric powder
- Red chili powder
- Ground coriander
- Ground cumin
- Garam masala
- Salt
- Cooking oil
- Deep pot or wok

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2-3 people.

Per portion:

**Kofta (dumplings):**

- Bottle gourd 1 (about 400g)
- Gram flour 80g
- Salt 3g
- Red chili powder 3g
- Ground coriander 3g
- Cooking oil (for frying) 500ml

**Sauce:**

- Onions 2 (about 200g), chopped
- Tomatoes 3 (about 300g), chopped
- Ginger 10g, grated
- Garlic 4 cloves (about 12g), grated
- Green chili 1, chopped
- Cooking oil 30ml
- Turmeric powder 3g
- Red chili powder 5g
- Ground coriander 5g
- Ground cumin 3g
- Garam masala 3g
- Salt 5g
- Water 200ml
- Cilantro (for garnish) 10g

## Steps

### Making the Kofta

1. Peel the bottle gourd and grate it into fine shreds
2. Squeeze the water out of the grated gourd by hand—*squeeze it as dry as possible*, or the balls won't hold their shape when fried
3. Add 80g of gram flour, 3g of salt, 3g of red chili powder and 3g of ground coriander to the squeezed gourd
4. Mix thoroughly by hand into a dough that doesn't stick to your hands
5. Roll the mixture into small balls about 3cm across and set aside
6. Pour 500ml of cooking oil into the deep pot and heat over medium heat to about 170°C (a small piece of dough should float up quickly and bubble)
7. Fry the balls in batches for **4-5 minutes**, until *golden and crispy*
8. Drain the fried balls on paper towels and set aside

### Making the Sauce

1. Pour 30ml of cooking oil into the wok and heat over medium heat for 10 seconds
2. Add the chopped onions and stir-fry until *golden brown* (about 5-7 minutes)
3. Add the grated ginger and garlic and stir-fry for 1 minute until *fragrant*
4. Add the chopped tomatoes and stir-fry until *completely broken down and the oil separates* (about 5 minutes)
5. Add 3g of turmeric, 5g of red chili powder, 5g of ground coriander, 3g of ground cumin and 5g of salt
6. Stir-fry for 2 minutes so the spices blend fully with the tomatoes
7. Add 200ml of water, stir well, bring to a boil, then turn to low heat and **simmer for 5 minutes**
8. Gently add the fried kofta to the sauce and **simmer on low for 3-5 minutes** so they absorb the sauce
9. Sprinkle with 3g of garam masala and the chopped green chili
10. Turn off the heat, garnish with cilantro, and plate

## Additional Notes

- Squeezing the water out of the bottle gourd is a key step; too much water makes the balls fall apart.
- Don't simmer the kofta in the sauce too long, or they'll get soft and lose their texture.
- In India this dish is usually served with naan or steamed rice (chawal).
- The word "kofta" comes from Persian/Arabic, meaning "crushed, ground", and is widespread in South Asia and the Middle East.
