# How to Make Blanched Choy Sum

![Blanched choy sum](../../../dishes/vegetable_dish/白灼菜心/白灼菜心.jpg)

This classic Cantonese dish uses the "baizhuo" (quick-blanch) technique: a fast dip in boiling water keeps the choy sum (caixin) sweet and crisp, the stems especially refreshing, with a light, delicate flavor. It's rich in vitamin C and dietary fiber, low in fat and calories, and suits anyone looking to eat healthily. It's very simple—estimated difficulty is only two stars—and most beginners can finish in 15 minutes, making it an ideal way to get your leafy greens quickly.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 86 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Fresh choy sum
- Light soy sauce, oyster sauce, salt
- Garlic, bird's eye chili
- Cooking oil

## Calculation

The amounts below are enough for two people; one person can have it with a bowl of rice as a meal.

- Fresh choy sum 250g
- Cooking oil 10g
- Mix up the all-important sauce
  - Light soy sauce 5g
  - Oyster sauce 5g
  - Salt, sugar 5g
- Garlic four or five cloves, bird's eye chili one or two

## Steps

1. Wash the choy sum and **remove any hard or old parts at the base**. Here I also **scraped the stems**, removing the tougher outer layer so the inside is more tender, but watch the blanching time for the stems—too long and they lose their crunch

2. Mince the garlic; I had an onion, so I added a little onion too

3. Make the all-important sauce: about 5g of light soy sauce and 5g of oyster sauce, add 3g of sugar and 100g of water (half a bowl) to make a bowl of sauce
   >If you add starch, the sauce clings to the choy sum more easily once it boils, giving a stronger flavor, but Cantonese people may not be used to that

4. Bring a pot of 500ml of water with 5g of salt and 10g of cooking oil to a boil

5. Dip the choy sum stems in the boiling water for 1 minute until they turn dark green. Then put the whole choy sum into the pot and blanch for 1 minute until cooked, take it out and arrange on a plate

6. In another small pot, pour in the prepared sauce, bring to a boil over low heat, and add half the minced garlic, a few ginger shreds and the chopped bird's eye chili. When I made it I added a little chopped onion and garlic: first add oil to the pot, and when it's 50% hot add the garlic and onion, fry briefly until fragrant, then add the sauce and the bird's eye chili and bring to a boil

7. Reduce the sauce slightly—after it boils, wait about ten seconds—then pour it straight over the choy sum. Not too much, but the garlic really makes it, so don't skimp on it

## Additional Notes

- The choy sum must be fresh! Fresh!
- Add oil and salt to the blanching water
