# How to Make Garlic Water Spinach

Garlic water spinach is a crisp, refreshing quick home-style dish with a strong garlic aroma, a typical Sichuan-style stir-fry. Water spinach (kongxincai) is rich in dietary fiber, vitamins and calcium, which help intestinal movement. It's very simple and beginner-friendly, taking only about 15 minutes from prep to plate.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 440 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Water spinach
* Minced garlic
* Salt
* Light soy sauce
* Chopsticks
* Spatula

## Calculation

Per portion:

* Fresh water spinach 250 g
* Half a head of garlic, minced
* Cooking oil 45 ml
* Salt 2 g
* White sugar 3 g
* Light soy sauce 8 ml

## Steps

1. Wash the water spinach, remove any rotten leaves or old stems, and cut evenly into 2 or 3 sections (so it's not too long to stir-fry)
2. First pour a little oil into the wok and heat until it smokes slightly; then lift the wok and swirl the hot oil around so it coats the bottom evenly, then add the rest of the oil (the [hot wok, cold oil method](../../tips/learn/stir-frying-and-pan-frying.md)).
3. Add the minced garlic and stir-fry over low heat for 10 to 15 seconds until fragrant
4. Add the water spinach evenly as quickly as possible, **turn to high heat**, and with the spatula in your left hand and chopsticks in your right, keep turning the water spinach **until it softens and turns green**.
5. Then put down the chopsticks and stir-fry the softened water spinach quickly with the spatula for 15 - 20 seconds so it heats evenly, and add 2 g of salt, 3 g of sugar and 8 ml of light soy sauce.
6. Keep stir-frying over high heat for 10 seconds, then serve.

## Additional Notes

* Choose fresh, tender water spinach if possible; otherwise it may be tough
* Adjust the amount of water spinach to the size of your wok; no more than one wokful is recommended, or a little will spill onto the stove during the turning and stir-frying
* Be sure to **use high heat** when stir-frying water spinach, so it softens quickly while staying crisp and tender; high heat also helps bring out the aroma of the soy sauce.
