# How to Make Blooming Onion

The blooming onion is a classic fried appetizer from American restaurants: a whole onion is cut without slicing through the base, coated and deep-fried, and the petals open naturally like a big golden flower. The coating is crispy, the inside has the onion's own sweetness, and with a dipping sauce it makes a great party snack. The difficulty lies in choosing the onion, the knife work and the oil temperature: the onion must be large and sweet; if you cut through the base the petals fall apart; if the coating is incomplete you won't get a crispy shell; and if the oil is too hot the outside burns. It takes about 40 minutes from prep to serving.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 850 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Yellow onion (i.e. sweet onion; choose a large one at least 9cm in diameter with thick layers and high sweetness—this is key to the dish)
- Eggs
- Cake flour (low-gluten flour)
- Cornstarch
- Chili powder (optional; leave out if you don't eat spicy food)
- Ground black pepper
- Salt
- Cooking oil (for deep-frying; enough to submerge the whole onion)
- Deep pot (a deep saucepan or stockpot no more than 16cm in diameter, with sides taller than the onion; a shallow frying pan won't work)
- Kitchen thermometer
- Slotted spoon

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2 people; the amounts below are per portion.

Per portion:

- Yellow onion 1 (at least 9cm in diameter, about 350g)
- Egg 1 (about 50g)
- Cake flour 120g (40g of it for the first dusting)
- Cornstarch 40g
- Ice water 100ml
- Chili powder 2g
- Ground black pepper 1g
- Salt 3g
- Cooking oil 1200ml (for frying; about 80ml is actually absorbed)

## Steps

### Cutting the Bloom

1. Peel off the dry outer skin of the onion and cut 5mm off the top, leaving the root end intact
2. Starting from the top, cut straight down along the diameter, stopping 1cm from the root end, i.e. without cutting through the root
3. Make the same cut about every 45 degrees, 16 cuts in total, dividing the whole onion into 16 petals
4. Turn the onion over, root end up, and make 1 extra cut between every two petals in the same way, 8 extra cuts in total

### Soaking

1. Put the cut onion root end up into ice water and soak for 20 minutes
2. Take it out and let it rest root end up for 5 minutes to drain

### Coating

1. Take 40g of cake flour and sprinkle it evenly between the petals, spreading the petals apart as you go so the inside of every layer gets coated with dry flour
2. Mix the remaining 80g of cake flour, 40g of cornstarch, 2g of chili powder, 1g of black pepper and 3g of salt evenly. Set aside
3. Mix 1 egg with 100ml of ice water and beat for 30 seconds until completely smooth. Set aside
4. Dip the onion root end up into the egg wash and turn it over 3 times so both sides of every petal are coated
5. Put the onion root end up into the seasoned flour; grab handfuls and sprinkle it from the center outward into the gaps between the petals, turning it over 3 times
6. Lift the onion and shake off excess flour; check whether any bare white spots remain at the base of the petals and sprinkle more flour on them
7. Let it rest for 2 minutes so the coating absorbs moisture; the shell should now be an even white layer of flour

### Frying

1. Pour 1200ml of cooking oil into the deep pot and heat over medium heat to 170℃
2. Lower the onion into the oil root end down; the oil will bubble vigorously
3. Keep the oil at 165℃ - 175℃ and fry for 3 minutes, repeatedly ladling hot oil over the top of the onion with the slotted spoon
4. Turn the onion over and keep frying root end up for 2 minutes
5. Take it out when the petals are golden and the shell has hardened; the onion should float on the oil and the petals should be fully open
6. Drain on the slotted spoon for 2 minutes

### Plating

1. Place the onion root end down on a plate and let the petals spread naturally
2. Put 30ml of dipping sauce in the center (ketchup, mayonnaise or honey mustard all work)

## Additional Notes

- The onion must be a large yellow (sweet) onion: red onions are pungent, white onions are watery, and small onions have petals so thin they break easily
- When cutting, you can place a chopstick on each side of the onion and stop when the knife hits the chopsticks, so you don't cut through the root
- Soaking in ice water makes the petals crisp and open naturally; don't skip this step
- Incomplete coating is the number one cause of failure: any spot on the inside of a petal without flour will turn soft and collapse after frying
- A deep pot rather than a frying pan is used so the oil can cover the whole onion; otherwise the top petals won't get fried
- Above 180℃ the outside burns before the inside is cooked, so keep it below 180℃ the whole time
- Without a kitchen thermometer, stick a chopstick into the oil; when dense small bubbles form around it, it's about 170℃
- For a dipping sauce, see [Garlic Soy Sauce](../condiment/garlic-soy-sauce.md)
