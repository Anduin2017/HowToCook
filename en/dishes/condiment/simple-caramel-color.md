# How to Make Simple Caramel Color

Caramel color (tangse) is the key technique for coloring Chinese red-braised dishes. Done right, it gives a glossy red sheen and a unique toasty flavor. It's mainly sugar, which provides energy, but its real job is to balance a dish's color and flavor. This simplified version still requires some heat control; beginners need practice to keep it from turning bitter or staying too sweet. It takes about ten minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1028 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Sugar (pick one):
  - Rock sugar: gives the brightest, glossiest red `caramel color`; must use the water-and-oil method, as it melts very slowly without water
  - Granulated sugar: must use the water-and-oil method, as it melts very slowly without water
  - Soft white sugar: can be done without water
- Don't use too much heat when making caramel color!!! An induction cooker may not get hot enough. Overcooked, it turns bitter; undercooked, it's just sweet

## Calculation

- `Oil`: 100ml
- `Boiling water`: 500ml
- Sugar: 50g (about 15 pieces of single-crystal rock sugar, or the same weight of granulated sugar. **Base ratio**: 50g of sugar per 1000g of meat; adjust by ±10g to taste)

## Steps

⚠️ **High-intensity warning and prep**:
The last step of making caramel color (adding water/ingredients) is a continuous operation that requires a fast response. Once the caramel reaches its critical point, a few seconds' hesitation will make it bitter.
**Requirement**: before turning on the heat, place 400ml of boiling water and all the prepared scallion, ginger, garlic and other aromatics right next to the stove, so you can grab them within 1 second.

1. Turn on the heat and pour 100ml of boiling water into the wok
2. Then pour 100ml of oil into the wok—the shorter the interval after step 1 the better. The heat can be high or medium at this point; use high if you're in a hurry
3. Add the rock sugar (if the rock sugar is too tightly coupled, crush it in advance—the lower the coupling, the better)
4. Adjust to medium heat
5. Start stirring

  - Keep stirring, stirring, stirring until it turns tan brown, then **switch to low heat**
  - It will thin out again, turn the color of black tea, then turn a sauce-like reddish brown and form small bubbles. Get ready to perform `Operation 1` or `Operation 2` below
  - The small bubbles gradually disappear, and then large bubbles appear. When the large bubbles appear, the caramel color is done
  - You need to do the next step quickly at this point (whichever operation you choose, prepare in advance and move fast! Otherwise it overcooks and turns bitter). Choose according to the style of the dish and your taste
    - Operation 1: add 400ml of boiling water directly to cool it down
    - Operation 2 (advanced seasoning version): when the syrup turns jujube red and forms dense small bubbles, quickly add the prepared scallion, ginger, garlic, Sichuan peppercorns and other aromatics and stir-fry. **Once fragrant (within about 5-10 seconds), you must immediately pour in 400ml of boiling water to cool it down and stop the reaction**. (*Note: if the recipe calls for stir-frying meat to color it, add the meat at this step and stir-fry, then add the water last.*)
