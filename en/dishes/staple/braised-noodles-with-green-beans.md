# How to Make Braised Noodles with Green Beans

Braised noodles with green beans (doujiao menmian) is a northern Chinese home-style noodle dish: the noodles soak up the sauce, becoming soft yet chewy and savory, with the freshness of green beans and the richness of pork. Pork belly provides protein and fat, and green beans are rich in dietary fiber and vitamins, a balanced mix of meat and vegetables. It's not hard overall, but you need to watch the heat and when to add broth, so it suits people with some cooking experience. It's usually on the table in about 30 minutes.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 1386 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Fresh noodles (jiuye or erxi width; see the noodle width section at the bottom)
* Pork (pork belly is best)
* Salt
* Green beans
* Chicken bouillon powder
* Oyster sauce
* MSG
* Thirteen-spice powder
* Light soy sauce
* Dark soy sauce
* Green onion
* Garlic
* Ginger
* Hot water
* Kitchen knife

## Steps

### Prep

1. Cut the green beans into 5cm - 6cm pieces.
2. Cut the green onion into 1cm - 2cm pieces.
3. Cut the ginger into 1mm x 1mm x 3cm strips
4. Smash the garlic on the board and mince to 1mm pieces.
5. Cut the pork belly into 2mm thick slices.

6. First heat the wok to drive off all moisture; holding your hand 10cm above the bottom, you should clearly feel some heat.
7. Add the measured cooking oil, hold the handle 5cm above the stove, and swirl the wok so the oil coats two thirds of it (from the bottom up).

> PS: if using a non-stick pan, skip the above and just wait 3-5 seconds for the oil to heat.

8. Add all the ginger and green onion and stir-fry for 5 seconds until fragrant (careful! Oil may spatter at this point; wear gloves or take precautions).
9. Add all the pork slices; don't rush to stir—leave them for 5 seconds, then stir-fry so all the pork is coated in oil.
10. Keep stir-frying until all the pork has changed color, then drizzle the light soy sauce evenly around the edge of the wok and stir-fry evenly.
11. Add the salt, dark soy sauce, oyster sauce, thirteen-spice powder, chicken bouillon and all the green beans in order, and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
12. Add the hot water.
13. When it boils, ladle out half of the broth in the wok (careful! don't scoop out the vegetables).
14. Spread all the noodles flat on top of the vegetables.
15. Cover and braise over medium heat for 5 minutes.
16. Open the lid and sprinkle the broth you ladled out evenly over the noodles, one ladleful at a time.
17. Cover and braise over medium heat for 3 minutes.
18. Open the lid and sprinkle in all the garlic and MSG evenly.

> PS: there may still be some liquid in the wok; if you like it wetter, turn off the heat now, otherwise leave it on.

19. Keep tossing with chopsticks, mixing the vegetables and meat evenly with the noodles.
20. Turn off the heat

## Additional Notes

### Noodle Widths

* Maoxi (as the name suggests, as thin as hair, like thin glass noodles)
* Xi (a bit thicker than maoxi, like an incense stick)
* Erxi (about as thick as typical stir-fried noodles)
* Sanxi (a bit thicker than erxi, like eating thick glass noodles; if the noodles are pulled chewy, they're very satisfying to chew)
* Jiuye (as wide as a garlic chive leaf)
* Dakuan (like the wide sweet potato noodles in malatang)
