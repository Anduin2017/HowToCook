# How to Make Fresh Pork Shumai

This is a refined dim sum with Jiangnan flavor: wrappers as thin as paper, plump and juicy filling, and when you bite in, the aroma of pork, the freshness of bamboo shoots and the juices of melted aspic blend together—savory with a hint of sweetness, tender and springy. Pork and shrimp provide high-quality protein, winter bamboo shoots and shiitakes add dietary fiber and trace elements, and the low-fat version further reduces saturated fat. Wrapping takes some patience, but the steps are clear overall, and beginners can master it with practice. It takes about an hour from mixing the filling to serving.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1485 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Shumai wrappers
- Minced pork (3:7 fat to lean; for a low-fat version, use pork tenderloin or front leg meat)
- Minced ginger
- Minced green onion (green part only)
- Light soy sauce
- Cooking wine
- Salt
- Sugar (optional)
- Ground white pepper (optional)
- Sesame oil
- Stock or stock concentrate
- Winter bamboo shoots (optional)
- Pork skin aspic (optional but recommended)
- Shiitake mushrooms (or rehydrated dried shiitakes, optional)
- Shrimp (optional)
- Lard (or chicken fat) (low-fat version)
- Compound food thickener (κ-carrageenan 45%, guar gum 35%, potassium chloride 20%) (or corn starch) (low-fat version)
- Steamer liner (in order of preference: oiled bamboo mat, straw mat, bamboo leaves, perforated parchment paper; traditionally a straw mat, nowadays more often perforated parchment)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is enough for 2-3 people.

Per portion:

- Shumai wrappers 240-465 g (about 24-31 wrappers; 30 wrappers of 10g each are recommended)
- Minced pork 300 g (280 g of pork tenderloin or front leg meat for the low-fat version)
- Minced ginger 5-10 g
- Minced green onion 10-20 g
- Light soy sauce 15 mL
- Cooking wine 10 mL
- Salt 3-5 g (reduce by 3 g if using stock concentrate)
- Sugar 2-3 g
- Ground white pepper 2 g
- Sesame oil 5 mL
- Stock 30 mL
- Stock concentrate 6 g (Knorr recommended, about 1/8 of a cube)
- Winter bamboo shoots 50 g
- Pork skin aspic 100 g
- Shiitake mushrooms 75 g (or 30 g of dried shiitakes, rehydrated)
- Shrimp 100-200 g (about 20-25)
- Compound food thickener 1.2 g (or 2 g of corn starch)
- Lard (or chicken fat) 15 g

## Steps

1. (Low-fat version) Mix the compound food thickener into 10 mL of cold water to make a paste
2. (Low-fat version) Add 25 mL of boiling water and stir until it becomes a translucent gel
3. (Low-fat version) Once cooled, mix it into 280 g of pure lean minced pork + 15 g of melted lard (or chicken fat)
4. Mix the minced pork with the minced ginger and green onion, add the soy sauce, cooking wine, salt, sugar and ground white pepper, and stir in one direction until cohesive
5. If using stock concentrate, mix the concentrate with 15 mL of hot water and stir until the emulsion is completely dispersed, then add 15 mL of room-temperature water
6. Slowly add the water or stock while continuing to stir, until the filling has absorbed the liquid and become sticky. If the filling seems too thick, add another 5 mL of water and keep stirring
7. Add the optional ingredients (bamboo shoots, aspic, shiitakes), drizzle with sesame oil, and mix well. Refrigerate for 30 minutes to let the flavors meld
8. Take a shumai wrapper and put about 20-25 g of filling on it (don't be greedy, or it won't close)
9. Gently gather the edges of the wrapper with the web of your hand into a "vase" shape, leaving the top open
10. Gently press the bottom with your fingers so it stands steadily
11. If adding shrimp, put one shrimp on top
12. Repeat until all are wrapped
13. Arrange them in the steamer, spaced apart so they don't stick together
14. Steam over high heat for 8-10 minutes

## Additional Notes

- The fat in shumai mainly comes from fatty pork; in the low-fat version, with the fatty pork removed, a small amount of animal fat is needed to compensate for the texture
- If you don't have shumai wrappers, use large wonton wrappers or roll dumpling wrappers thinner
- Wrappers should be 8-10 cm across, each weighing about 10-15g, with a wrapper-to-filling ratio of 1:2 - 1:2.5
- Use the medium setting on the meat grinder, so the meat isn't too fine (like paste) or too coarse (like chunks); a moderate texture keeps the filling springy
- 5 g of ginger and 10 g of green onion won't mask the flavor of fresh pork; 10 g of ginger and 20 g of green onion tastes a bit like dumpling filling
- Rub a little oil on the web of your hand when wrapping so the opening doesn't stick to your hand
- Only put them in the steamer once the water is boiling, so the wrappers don't collapse
- Extras can be frozen at -18 ℃; frozen shumai need 12-15 minutes of steaming
- They taste even better dipped in rose rice vinegar; you can also serve them with Tongxiang chili sauce, Shaanxi chili oil, light soy sauce or shredded ginger to taste
- Reference: [Step-by-step photos of fresh pork shumai by Xiao Shenme l - Xiachufang](https://www.xiachufang.com/recipe/106527847/)
