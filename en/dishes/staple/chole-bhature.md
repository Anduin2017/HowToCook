# How to Make Chole Bhature

Chole bhature—chickpea curry with fried bread—is a classic North Indian street food: soft, puffy fried bread with richly spiced chickpeas, layered textures, tangy, spicy and savory. Chickpeas are rich in plant protein and dietary fiber, filling and nutritious. There are a few more steps, but it's not hard if you follow the process, suitable for home cooks with some basics. It takes about 50 minutes (the chickpeas need to soak for 8 hours in advance).

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1098 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Chickpeas (dried)
- All-purpose flour
- Yogurt
- Onions
- Tomatoes
- Ginger
- Garlic
- Green chilies
- Cilantro
- Black tea bag (for coloring the chickpeas)
- Cooking oil
- Turmeric powder
- Red chili powder
- Ground coriander
- Ground cumin
- Garam masala
- Pomegranate powder or mango powder (anardana/amchur) — lemon juice can be used instead
- Salt
- Baking soda
- Deep pot (for frying the bread)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2-3 people.

Per portion:

**Chickpea curry (chole):**

- Dried chickpeas 200g (soaked 8 hours in advance)
- Onions 2 (about 200g), chopped
- Tomatoes 3 (about 300g), chopped
- Ginger 10g, grated
- Garlic 4 cloves (about 12g), grated
- Green chilies 2, slit
- Cooking oil 30ml
- Black tea bag 1
- Turmeric powder 3g
- Red chili powder 5g
- Ground coriander 8g
- Ground cumin 5g
- Garam masala 5g
- Pomegranate powder 3g (or 15ml of lemon juice)
- Salt 6g
- Water 500ml

**Fried bread (bhature):**

- All-purpose flour 250g
- Yogurt 60ml
- Cooking oil 15ml (for the dough)
- Baking soda 2g
- Salt 3g
- Warm water about 60ml
- Cooking oil 500ml (for frying)

## Steps

### Prep (8 Hours Ahead)

1. Soak the dried chickpeas in water overnight (at least 8 hours); they'll roughly double in volume

### Cooking the Chickpeas

1. Drain the soaked chickpeas
2. Put them in a pot with 500ml of water and 1 black tea bag (for color)
3. Bring to a boil on high heat, then turn to medium and **cook for 25-30 minutes**, until *the chickpeas can be easily crushed between your fingers*
4. Remove the tea bag, keep the cooking liquid, and set aside

### Making the Curry Sauce

1. Pour 30ml of cooking oil into the wok and heat over medium heat
2. Add the chopped onions and stir-fry until *golden brown* (about 6-8 minutes)
3. Add the grated ginger and garlic and stir-fry for 1 minute
4. Add the chopped tomatoes and stir-fry until *the tomatoes are completely broken down and the oil separates* (about 5 minutes)
5. Add 3g of turmeric, 5g of red chili powder, 8g of ground coriander, 5g of ground cumin and 6g of salt
6. Stir-fry for 2 minutes so the spices fully release their aroma
7. Pour in the cooked chickpeas together with their cooking liquid
8. Add the green chilies and stir well
9. Bring to a boil on high heat, then turn to low and **simmer for 15-20 minutes**; meanwhile, mash some chickpeas with the back of the spatula to thicken the sauce
10. Add 5g of garam masala and 3g of pomegranate powder (or 15ml of lemon juice)
11. Stir well, turn off the heat, and set aside

### Making the Bhature Dough

1. In a large bowl, mix 250g of all-purpose flour, 2g of baking soda and 3g of salt
2. Add 60ml of yogurt and 15ml of cooking oil
3. Gradually add warm water, kneading as you go, into a *smooth, soft* dough (it should be a bit softer than ordinary dough)
4. Cover the dough with a damp cloth and let it rest for **30 minutes**

### Frying the Bread

1. Divide the dough into 8 equal pieces
2. Take one piece, with a little oil on your hands, and roll it into a round about 15cm across and 3mm thick
3. Pour 500ml of cooking oil into the deep pot and heat over medium-high heat to about 180°C
4. Gently slide the bread into the hot oil and press it gently with a slotted spoon so it *puffs up*
5. **Fry for 1-2 minutes**, flipping, until *golden on both sides*
6. Remove and blot off excess oil with paper towels

### Serving

1. Reheat the chickpea curry
2. Serve the fried bread hot with the chickpea curry
3. Serve with sliced onion, green chilies and lemon wedges

## Additional Notes

- Soaking the chickpeas in advance is a must; unsoaked chickpeas won't get soft. If you're short on time, pressure cook unsoaked chickpeas for 20 minutes.
- The black tea bag colors the chickpeas a deep brown. This is an Indian restaurant secret.
- The yogurt and baking soda in the bhature dough are key to making it soft and puffy. The dough shouldn't be too firm.
- In India this dish is a national-level brunch, served everywhere from street stalls to upscale restaurants. In Indian restaurants in China it's usually called "chickpea curry with fried bread".
