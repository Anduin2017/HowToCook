# How to Make Zhajiangmian (Fried Sauce Noodles)

Zhajiangmian is a traditional Beijing home-style noodle dish with a rich savory sauce and chewy noodles, served with refreshing vegetable toppings for a balanced salty-sweet flavor. Its nutrition comes mainly from the protein in lean pork and the vitamins in vegetables, providing plenty of energy. It's beginner-friendly and takes about 30 minutes; once you're practiced, you can shorten it further by prepping vegetables while frying the sauce.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 1272 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Diced/minced pork
* Noodles (dried noodles or ordinary fresh noodles)
* Garlic
* Vegetable toppings (to taste, usually 4-10 kinds, such as cucumber, napa cabbage, radish, etc.)
* Doubanjiang (chili bean paste)
* Sweet bean sauce (tianmianjiang)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 1 person.

Per portion:

* Diced/minced pork 150g
* If you chose *dried noodles*: 150g  
  If you chose *ordinary fresh noodles*: 250g
* Green onion 15g
* Vegetable toppings 35g in total
* Cooking oil 10g
* Doubanjiang 20g
* Sweet bean sauce 20g

## Steps

1. Julienne the vegetable toppings and set aside.
2. Chop the green onion. Heat oil in the wok, add the green onion and pork, and stir-fry until the pork is fully cooked (no red left)
3. Add the doubanjiang and sweet bean sauce and keep stir-frying until **slightly thick**. Scoop out; this is the *fried sauce*.
4. Fill a large bowl with cold water and set aside.
5. Boil the noodles until just cooked (no white core), then transfer to the bowl of cold water from step 4.
6. Immediately drain and transfer to a clean bowl.
7. Pour the fried sauce from step 3 into the bowl and mix well. Then add the vegetable toppings from step 1 and mix well.

## Additional Notes

* Diced lean pork is recommended for a better texture; buy it fresh and use it right away.
* *Ordinary fresh noodles* are recommended.
* The key to the noodles' texture is chewiness. Don't use very thin noodles like dragon-whisker noodles.
* If you have a second pot and enough practice, steps 2 and 3 can run in parallel with steps 1, 4, 5 and 6.
* Everyone has their own preference for sweet vs. salty; you'll need to iterate and optimize the ratio of the two sauces yourself.
