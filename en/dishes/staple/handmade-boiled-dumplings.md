# How to Make Handmade Boiled Dumplings

Handmade boiled dumplings are a classic Chinese staple: thin wrappers, generous fillings, fresh and juicy, filling, and easy to season however you like. The pork filling provides high-quality protein and fat, and garlic chives add vitamins and dietary fiber. This dish is fairly hard for beginners: you need to master making dough, rolling wrappers, mixing the filling and the heat for boiling. It takes about 3 hours.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 1313 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Rolling pin
- Flour
- Cold water
- A basin at least 30cm across
- Sesame oil

## Calculation

- One person, about 20 dumplings

Per portion:

- Flour 200g
- Cold water 150ml
- Sesame oil 2-3ml
- Minced lean pork 250g
- Minced pork fat 20g (leave out if you don't like it)
- Ginger 3g
- Green onion 15g
- Salt 3g
- Oyster sauce 2ml
- Sesame oil 2ml
- Light soy sauce 2ml
- Egg 1

## Steps

### Making the Wrappers

1. Put all the flour in the basin
2. Add the sesame oil
3. Make a small well in the center of the flour
4. Add the water in 4-5 additions, stirring. When it becomes crumbly, slightly dry clumps of dough
5. Stop adding water and press the dough together by hand
6. Press the dough until it picks up all the flour around the basin; this step is "clean dough, clean basin"
7. Put the dough on the table and turn the basin upside down over it. At an ambient temperature of 25 degrees, let the dough rest for about 45 minutes
8. After resting, roll the dough into a log, fold it into a ball, and roll into a log again; repeat 3 times
9. Roll into a log, cut into 20 equal pieces, and roll each into a ball about 3-3.5cm across
10. Flatten the pieces, and dust your hands, the table, the rolling pin and the dough with flour; this keeps the dough from sticking
11. Roll each piece flat with the rolling pin, about 8cm across and 2mm thick, with the center about 1mm thicker than the edges

### Mixing the Filling

1. Remove the skin from the pork, keeping some of the fat, and cut into small pieces
2. Chop the pork into mince with a cleaver (two cleavers recommended) and put it in a bowl
3. Mince the green onion and ginger, add to the meat bowl, and mix well
4. Wash the garlic chives and cut them shorter than 3mm
5. Mix the chives with the minced meat, add 2ml each of oyster sauce, light soy sauce and sesame oil, and the white of one egg, and mix well by hand
6. Let it rest for 30 minutes, then start wrapping

### Wrapping

1. Put a wrapper in your left hand; try to keep flour off the side where the filling goes, or it won't seal
2. With chopsticks in your right hand, pick up filling about 1/2 the diameter of the wrapper
3. Fold the wrapper closed around its edge and pinch firmly. For eating yourself, no need for decorative pleats—just make sure the wrapper doesn't leak

### Boiling

1. Use a pot big enough for 20 dumplings, or cook in batches
2. Boil water to about 3/4 of the height of the pot
3. Once boiling over high heat, add the dumplings and turn to medium heat
4. After the first addition of dumplings, when the water bubbles, add 50ml of cold water around the edge of the pot (repeat this step twice)
5. When the water boils the third time, add 50ml of cold water; when it boils again, turn to low heat, wait 60s, and serve

## Additional Notes

- Don't cover the pot when boiling dumplings. Adding cold water three times keeps the dumplings from boiling hard continuously, which would break the wrappers and turn them into noodle sheets.

There are some supplementary variations for this dish, including but not limited to:

Add the following ingredients:

* Black vinegar 10ml
* Ginger, one small piece, 50 grams
* Sesame oil, 2 drops
* Garlic / garlic paste, 3 cloves per person

* Consider eating with black vinegar. Recommended amount: 10-20ml.
* Consider julienning the ginger and mixing it with 20ml of black vinegar in a small bowl as a dipping sauce for richer flavor.
* Consider adding 1~3 drops of sesame oil to the black vinegar and stirring as a dipping sauce.
* Consider adding pounded garlic paste to the black vinegar and stirring as a dipping sauce. (Garlic breath will linger; if you need to talk face to face with others after the meal, skip it or clean your mouth)
