# How to Make Litti Chokha

Litti chokha is a traditional staple from Bihar, India: golden roasted wheat balls served with a smoky vegetable mash, crispy outside and soft inside with a rich aroma. The roasted gram flour (sattu) filling is rich in plant protein and dietary fiber, and with eggplant, tomato, potato and other vegetables it's very nutritious. It involves making dough, stuffing, roasting and preparing the vegetables, so there are quite a few steps, which is challenging for beginners. It takes about 60 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 1019 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Eggplant

- Onion
- Garlic
- Ginger
- Green chilies
- Lemon
- Cilantro
- Mustard oil — can be replaced with other cooking oil
- Cumin seeds

- Ghee
- Salt

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2 people.

Per portion:

**Litti (dough balls):**

- Whole wheat flour 200g
- Roasted gram flour 120g
- Onion 1 (about 80g), very finely chopped
- Green chilies 2, very finely chopped
- Ginger 5g, grated
- Mustard oil 15ml
- Lemon juice 10ml
- Cilantro 10g, chopped
- Cumin seeds 3g
- Sesame seeds 5g
- Red chili powder 3g
- Indian black salt 3g
- Salt 3g
- Warm water about 100ml (for the dough)
- Ghee 30g (for brushing after roasting)

**Chokha (vegetable mash):**

- Eggplant 1 (about 250g)
- Tomatoes 2 (about 180g)
- Potatoes 2 (about 240g)
- Garlic 4 cloves (about 12g)
- Green chili 1
- Mustard oil 15ml
- Salt 4g
- Cilantro 10g

## Steps

### Making the Litti Filling

1. In a bowl, mix 120g of roasted gram flour with the chopped onion, green chilies and ginger
2. Add 3g of cumin seeds, 5g of sesame seeds, 3g of red chili powder, 3g of black salt and 3g of salt
3. Add 15ml of mustard oil, 10ml of lemon juice and the chopped cilantro
4. Mix thoroughly; the filling should be loose but hold together when pressed. Set aside

### Making the Litti Dough

1. Put 200g of whole wheat flour in a large bowl
2. Gradually add warm water, kneading as you go, into a *smooth, soft* dough
3. Let the dough rest for **15 minutes**
4. Divide the dough into 8 equal pieces
5. Press each piece by hand into a round about 8cm across (slightly thicker in the middle)
6. Put about 15g of filling in the center of each round
7. Gather the edges around the filling, pinch the seam tightly, and roll into a ball

### Roasting the Litti

1. Preheat the oven to 200°C
2. Put the litti on a baking tray and bake for **25-30 minutes**, turning once, until *the surface is golden brown and cracked*
3. If using an open flame: roast the litti directly over charcoal or a gas burner on low, turning constantly, until *evenly charred*
4. While hot, brush each litti with ghee so it *soaks it up*

### Making the Chokha

1. Roast the eggplant, tomatoes and potatoes directly over an open flame, or bake in a 200°C oven for **20-25 minutes**, until *the skins are charred and the insides are completely soft*
2. Peel them and mash the eggplant, tomatoes and potatoes separately with a fork or by hand
3. Mix the mashed vegetables together
4. Add the minced garlic, green chili, 4g of salt and 15ml of mustard oil
5. Mix thoroughly
6. Garnish with cilantro

### Serving

1. Plate the litti with the chokha on the side
2. Eat while hot, breaking the litti open by hand and dipping it in the chokha

## Additional Notes

- Sattu (roasted gram flour) is a Bihari specialty with very high nutritional value, rich in protein and fiber. It may be hard to find in China; you can make your own by dry-roasting chickpeas (chana) until dark brown and grinding them into powder.
- Traditional litti is roasted over a cow dung cake fire, which gives it a unique smoky flavor. A home oven or open flame can substitute.
- Mustard oil is the soul of this dish; substituting it isn't recommended. If you can't find mustard oil, rapeseed oil is the next best choice.
- Litti chokha is one of the oldest foods in India, said to be thousands of years old and once used as marching rations for ancient soldiers.
