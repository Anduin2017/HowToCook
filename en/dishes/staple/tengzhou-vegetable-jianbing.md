# How to Make Tengzhou Vegetable Jianbing

Tengzhou vegetable jianbing is a traditional snack from Tengzhou, Shandong Province, said to have been created in the Qing dynasty by Wang Niugao. White flour jianbing (thin crêpes) are wrapped around seasonal vegetables such as napa cabbage, glass noodles and tofu, with egg and ham sausage, seasoned with oil and spices, and griddle-fried. The result is crispy outside, soft inside, fresh and savory. It's good as a staple or breakfast, and takes about 15 minutes from prep to serving.

Estimated difficulty: ★★

Estimated calories: 800 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- White flour jianbing (store-bought Shandong jianbing, or homemade)
- Napa cabbage
- Glass noodles (dry)
- Tofu
- Carrot
- Garlic chives (optional)
- Egg
- Ham sausage (optional)
- Cooking oil
- Salt
- MSG (optional)
- Ground cumin
- Chili powder (optional)
- Ground black pepper
- Griddle (ao) or non-stick frying pan (at least 28 cm across)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 1 person.

Total:

- Number of jianbing = portions * 2
- Napa cabbage = portions * 100 g
- Glass noodles (dry weight) = portions * 50 g
- Tofu = portions * 80 g
- Carrot = portions * 50 g
- Garlic chives = portions * 30 g (optional)
- Egg = portions * 1
- Ham sausage = portions * 40 g (optional)
- Cooking oil = portions * 20 ml
- Salt = portions * 2 g
- MSG = portions * 1 g (optional)
- Ground cumin = portions * 2 g
- Chili powder = portions * 2 g (optional)
- Ground black pepper = portions * 1 g

## Steps

1. Soak the glass noodles in warm water for 10 minutes until soft, drain, and cut into segments no longer than 5 cm. Set aside
2. Wash the napa cabbage and cut into thin shreds no wider than 1 cm; peel the carrot and cut into thin shreds no wider than 1 cm; cut the tofu into cubes no larger than 1 cm; wash the garlic chives and cut into pieces no longer than 1 cm. Put everything in a mixing bowl
3. Add 15 ml of cooking oil, 2 g of salt, 1 g of MSG, 2 g of ground cumin, 1 g of ground black pepper and 2 g of chili powder to the bowl in order, and mix with chopsticks so the vegetables are evenly coated. Set aside
4. Preheat the frying pan (or griddle) over medium heat, add 5 ml of cooking oil and swirl to coat. Crack in the egg, break up the yolk and white with the spatula, and spread into a thin round slightly smaller than the jianbing
5. Lay 1 jianbing flat on top of the egg so the egg sticks to it, then flip the jianbing and egg together (egg side up). Turn to low heat
6. Spread the seasoned vegetables evenly over the jianbing (egg side up), leaving about 1 cm around the edge so nothing spills out when folding
7. Cover the vegetables with the second jianbing and press gently with the spatula so the two layers stick together
8. Cook over medium-low heat for 2 minutes until the bottom is lightly browned, flip the whole thing with the spatula, and cook for another 2 minutes until the other side is lightly browned and glossy
9. Turn to the lowest heat and cook for another 1 minute, until both sides are golden and the vegetables are cooked through (poke a chopstick into the center to check there's no raw-vegetable resistance)
10. Turn off the heat, fold both sides toward the middle into a rectangle about 10 cm wide, cut in half with the spatula or a knife, and plate

## Additional Notes

- Swap the vegetables as you like: spinach, pumpkin, radish, shepherd's purse, etc. all work; keep the total vegetables per portion at 300-350 g
- Vegetable jianbing is best eaten hot; after more than 30 minutes at room temperature it gets soft and loses its crispness
- If you don't have a griddle, a non-stick frying pan at least 28 cm across works; don't use too high a heat or the jianbing will burn
- Jianbing can be bought; you can also mix 100 g of flour with 150 ml of drinking water into a thin batter and spread it in a frying pan
- Reference: [Tengzhou vegetable jianbing - Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/item/滕州菜煎饼/2512643)
