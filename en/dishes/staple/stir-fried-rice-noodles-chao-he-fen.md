# How to Make Stir-fried Rice Noodles (Chao He Fen)

Stir-fried rice noodles (chao he fen) are a classic snack popular in Guangdong, prized for strong "wok hei" and a dry-fragrant, silky texture: soft, chewy, translucent rice noodles with shredded meat, bean sprouts and shredded cucumber for layered textures. It mainly provides carbohydrates and protein, and the vegetables add vitamins and dietary fiber. Following this recipe, the difficulty is moderate; even beginners can try it once they have all the ingredients. It's on the table in about 30 minutes.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 822 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

* Flat rice noodles (he fen), pork/beef
* Stir-fry seasonings: salt, MSG, dark soy sauce, light soy sauce, ground cumin (or just use a rice noodle seasoning packet)
* Other seasonings: ground pepper
* Cucumber, wheat gluten pieces, mung bean sprouts, egg, garlic cloves, green onion, starch
* Basin, plates

> Suggested purchase: buy bagged fresh rice noodles on Taobao or a grocery delivery platform. They're usually sold from 500g (1 jin) up, keep refrigerated for up to two weeks, and come with a free stir-fry seasoning packet!

## Calculation

* Rice noodles: 250 g/person; for bigger appetites, add 100g/person, rounded down.
* Shredded cucumber 30g/person, wheat gluten 30g/person, mung bean sprouts 30g/person, beaten egg 1/person.
* Smashed garlic 2 cloves/person, green onion 1 stalk/person
* Rice noodle seasoning packet: 20g/person; if making your own, 10g salt + 2g MSG + 3g ground cumin.
* Starch: 5g per 100g of meat.
* Dark soy sauce / light soy sauce: 10ml / 15ml per 250g of rice noodles.

## Steps

### Prep

1. Chop the green onion (keeping whites and greens separate) and smash the garlic; leave them on the board.
2. Beat the egg, putting a little egg white in one bowl and the rest in another.
3. Put the mung bean sprouts in a pot and boil on high heat for 60 seconds. Take them out, rinse in cold water, and set aside on a plate.
4. Julienne the cucumber and set aside on a plate; it can go with the bean sprouts.
5. Prepare the wheat gluten and put it on a separate plate.
6. Cut the meat into thin strips, add the starch, the egg white from the bowl and ground pepper, and mix clockwise.
7. Note: store-bought products like liangpi usually have cooking oil on the surface; rinse with tap water. Same for the gluten.
8. Note: after rinsing the gluten, squeeze out most of the water by hand (no need to squeeze too hard).

### Heating the Wok and Frying the Meat

1. Add cooking oil, heat the wok, and pour it out.
2. Add the prepared meat, stir-fry until it changes color, and transfer to a bowl.

### Frying the Rice Noodles

1. While the wok is hot, add 20g of cooking oil (people with high blood pressure can use less), and stir-fry the green onion whites and garlic until fragrant.
2. Add the rice noodles, drizzle with dark soy sauce for color, stir-fry evenly, then add the noodle seasoning and keep stir-frying.
3. When the noodles are nearly translucent, add the fried meat and gluten, plus light soy sauce for savoriness, and toss a couple of times.
4. Add the bean sprouts and cucumber and stir-fry until the noodles are completely translucent.
5. Turn off the heat!

### Final Steps

1. Sprinkle with the green onion greens and lift the wok.
2. Pour onto a plate and dig in.

## Additional Notes

Tastes vary by region, weather and time, so adjust the exact amounts of seasoning to your situation; if you like chili, add some.
