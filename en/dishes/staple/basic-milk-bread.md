# How to Make Basic Milk Bread

![Finished milk bread](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/1-1成品.jpg)

This basic milk bread simplifies traditional bread-making by using a pre-fermented starter. The finished bread has a rich milky aroma and a soft, fluffy texture. It's rich in carbohydrates and protein, providing energy for the body. The difficulty is moderate and suitable for baking beginners to try. It takes about 200 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 2048 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

### Ingredients

- Bread flour or all-purpose flour *(use bread flour if possible, but **avoid cake flour**. Any "all-purpose flour" without special labeling is medium-protein flour)*
- Egg *(size and weight aren't critical)*
- Sugar *(syrup is even better.)*
- Dry yeast
- Salt
- Milk, cream, condensed milk or milk powder *(**milk powder** is best for a strong milky flavor, but **don't use any formulated milk powder**, such as adult or infant formula, because its chemical properties are unstable. You can also mix two dairy products. Try to **avoid ingredients with added sugar**, as the actual sugar content will then exceed what this recipe suggests)*
- Butter or corn oil *(if **the dairy you use is very high in fat, you can skip it**. Use unsalted dairy butter if possible. Soybean oil may have a beany smell, so try to avoid it.)*
- Vital wheat gluten (optional)
- Vanilla extract (optional)

### Required Tools

- Oven *(**don't use an oven without temperature control**)*
- Measuring cup
- Cloth
- Rolling pin
- Parchment paper or a mold

>Baking is a separate branch of cooking, so its tools differ a lot from everyday cooking. Common tools are introduced below.
>
>- ① Measuring cup: for measuring ingredients. Measuring cups are usually marked in cups. Don't shake it while measuring, or you'll measure too little.
>- ② Spatula: also called a silicone scraper, for mixing and scraping batter off the sides of containers.
>- ③ Cloth: covers the dough to keep it from losing too much moisture during the process. Use a damp or dry cloth as the situation requires. A steamer cloth (not a disposable one) is recommended; plastic wrap also works.
>- ④ Oven: for baking. Ovens range from small budget models to large professional ones. Budget ovens are poorly sealed and may heat unevenly; in that case, lower the temperature and extend the baking time. Ovens must be preheated. More on this later.
>- ⑤ Bench scraper: scrapes dough off the board and cuts dough into suitable pieces. A clean knife also works.
>- ⑥ Rolling pin: for pressing out dough.
>- ⑦ Spray bottle: evenly coats dough or containers with water or oil, for crisp crusts and preventing sticking.
>- ⑧ Sieve: spreads flour evenly or filters out lumps and impurities.
>- ⑨ Pastry board or silicone mat: the surface for working dough, so neither the dough nor other surfaces get dirty.
>- ⑩ Brush: for brushing egg wash on the dough, making the finished bread shinier and more attractive.
>- 11. Parchment paper: keeps baked goods from sticking to other things and makes unmolding easier. Parchment paper is heat resistant and safe. Some parchment paper has a right and wrong side, so pay attention.
>- 12. Molds: help shape bread while baking. There are few requirements for bread molds, but cake molds must be anodized aluminum, ideally sprayed with an oil-flour mixture to help the cake climb.

>- Vanilla extract: a highly purified natural flavoring extracted from vanilla. Besides vanilla-flavored foods, it's often used to neutralize off-flavors like eggy smells.

## Calculation

**Note: although specific amounts are given, room temperature, humidity and even the amount of yeast in the air all affect the process. This recipe gives adjustment suggestions throughout wherever possible.**

The cup is a common baking unit because it makes converting between volume and weight easy. 1 cup = 250 ml; generally, 1 cup of flour = 120 g. See Additional Notes for more conversions.

Here, larger amounts use cups and smaller amounts use grams.

Per portion:

- **Starter**
  - Flour 1 cup (fill a cup and level off the excess with a spoon; don't shake it)
  - 30 ℃ warm water (not hot to the touch) 1 cup
  - Yeast 2 g
  - Salt 2 g
- **Dough**
  - Flour 2½ cups
  - Egg 1
  - Sugar or syrup ⅛ cup
  - Dairy ¼ cup in total after mixing (milk powder needs to be mixed with water.)
  - Butter or corn oil ⅛ cup
  - Vital wheat gluten ¼ ~ ½ cup (optional)
  - Vanilla extract 3 g (optional)

## Steps

### Making the Starter

A starter is a pre-fermented mixture. Pre-fermenting not only improves your success rate, but the longer fermentation also gives the bread better flavor. If possible, the starter should ferment for 4 days for the best flavor. But for beginners, bread from a starter fermented for 30 minutes is already quite impressive. Now let's make the starter.

![Ideal state of the starter before fermenting](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/4-1酵头1.jpg)

1. First, mix the yeast and the ***30℃ warm water*** evenly with a spatula or utensil and let stand for 5 minutes. Then mix with the flour and stir well. The starter should now be a **very thick** batter.

![Covering the starter with a cloth](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/4-2酵头2.jpg)

2. Cover the batter with a cloth and leave it somewhere **warm** to ferment for 45 ~ 60 minutes.

![Final state of the starter](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/4-3酵头3.jpg)

3. The final batter should have lots of bubbles on the surface and have grown noticeably in volume.

**Fermentation failed? Check here:**

>1. Make sure the yeast hasn't expired
>2. Make sure the water temperature is right
>3. Extend the fermentation time; you can leave it in the refrigerator overnight
>4. It may not be warm enough; preheat the oven to 60℃, turn it off, and let it ferment in the oven
>5. Add the same amount of yeast again, stir well, ferment again, and extend the time
>6. Try adding 3g of sugar and fermenting again

**How to make a "permanent" starter?**

>With a starter, baking bread anytime is no longer a dream! A "permanent" starter can be made as follows:
>
>1. After making the starter as above, keep it somewhere cool (even the refrigerator!). Every 1-3 days, sprinkle a thin layer of flour on top (just enough that you can't see the batter underneath), stir again, then cover with the cloth and keep fermenting. If it ferments too fast and grows too big, discard some.
>2. When you're ready to make bread, keep back an amount equal to the pre-fermentation volume to keep fermenting, and use the rest for the bread.
>3. Over time, the starter develops a unique aroma, the best "ingredient" a bread can have!

### Making the Dough

With the starter done, you're halfway there!

![The dough at this stage](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/4-4此时的面团.jpg)

1. Mix all the remaining ingredients with the starter using the spatula. Be sure to scrape the sides while mixing so everything combines fully. Once a dough has formed with no dry flour left, press the dough with one clean, dry hand while holding the container with the other, forming one big dough ball, then turn it out onto the board or silicone mat

>The dough should now be formed and fairly soft. If it won't come together and is very sticky, there isn't enough flour; add ¼ cup of flour and mix thoroughly again until it forms. If the dough has formed but still has a lot of dry flour, it hasn't been kneaded long enough; knead longer.
>
>When pressing the dough, push it out with your fist, fold it in half and push it out again, repeating until all the flour on the sides of the container is kneaded in.

2. Push the dough out with the heel of your hand, fold it in half and push it out again, until there are no dry lumps on the surface. The dough won't be very smooth yet.

>If you use a pastry board, a little may stick; scrape it off with the bench scraper or a knife.
>
>If the dough has formed but is fairly firm, there isn't enough water. Wet the surface evenly with a damp hand or spray bottle, cover with a damp cloth, and let it ferment for 20 minutes; repeat as needed.

![Transferring to a container](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/4-5转移到容器内.jpg)

3. Spray the inside of a container at least three times the size of the dough with a layer of vegetable oil (or spread it evenly by hand), put the dough in, cover with the cloth, and let it ferment for 1 hour.

>If you have fermentation problems, see the solutions for failed fermentation under making the starter.
>
>After fermenting, the surface of the dough should be tacky. If not, wet the surface evenly with a damp hand or spray bottle and ferment for another 20 minutes.

4. The dough should now be twice its original size. Sprinkle a thin layer of flour on the board or silicone mat (just enough that you can still see the surface), take the dough out, and press it flat by hand to deflate it.
5. Then roll it out with the rolling pin into a 1 cm thick sheet, and roll it up tightly from one side with no gaps.
6. Then rotate 90° and repeat steps 4 and 5.
7. Shape the bread and put it on a parchment-lined baking tray or in a greased mold, seam side down.

>You can spray a layer of oil evenly on the baking tray or spread a thin layer of butter before putting down the parchment paper. That way the paper won't slide around.

8. Let it ferment in the tray or mold for 30 ~ 45 minutes.

>If you have fermentation problems, see the solutions for failed fermentation under making the starter.

9. Meanwhile, preheat the oven.

>Preheating the oven: run the empty oven at its highest temperature for at least 20 minutes.

10. Cut a few slashes in the bread with scissors or a knife as decoration.
11. Brush with egg wash. (Optional)

![Basic milk bread - preview image 9](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/4-6成品面包.jpg)

11. Put it in the oven and bake with top and bottom heat at 180℃ for 30 minutes, then lower to 165℃ and bake for another 10 minutes.

>Oven temperatures vary. Watch the bread while baking; if it hasn't noticeably browned after 30 minutes, the temperature is too low. Raise it by 5 ~ 10℃ or bake 5 ~ 10 minutes longer
>After baking, taste the bread. If the surface tastes bitter (note: as long as it isn't bitter, it isn't overbaked), the temperature was too high; try lowering it by 5 ~ 10℃ or baking 5 ~ 10 minutes less;
>If the outside is already caramel-colored but the inside isn't cooked, lower the temperature by 5 ~ 15℃ and extend the baking time by 10 ~ 20 minutes.
>
>If the crust is very hard or cracks badly, cover the bread with foil and remove it for the last ten minutes, or put another baking tray upside down on the top rack and remove it for the last 10 minutes.

12. Once cool, put it in a food bag; it keeps for a week. Frozen, it keeps for a month.

>After a night sealed in a food bag, the inside and crust soften further. So the best time to eat it is the next morning.

2. How to store leftover bread

  You can slice the bread and freeze it; next time, let it come to room temperature or heat it directly. But **never put it in the refrigerator!** In the refrigerator, bread loses moisture quickly.

3. Recommended way to eat

![Basic milk bread - preview image 10](../../../dishes/staple/基础牛奶面包/5-1成品.jpg)

a. Take a large piece of bread and split it in half horizontally. Toast it in a pan over low heat (usually no oil is needed, but for a crispy crust, spread butter on the surface before toasting) for 1 ~ 2 minutes.
b. Spray 3 ~ 4 pumps of oil into the pan, heat on high until the pan just starts to smoke, then turn to the lowest heat.
c. Crack in an egg and cover. Wait 2 ~ 3 minutes, then flip and cook for another 2 ~ 3 minutes.
(This gives a runny yolk; for a fully cooked egg, add up to 1 ml of water before flipping and turn to medium-low heat until the water has evaporated. That gives you a fully cooked egg)
d. Spread both halves of the bread with any sauce you like and sandwich the egg in between.
