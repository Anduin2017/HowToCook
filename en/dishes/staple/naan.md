# How to Make Naan

Naan is an Indian-style leavened flatbread, slightly charred and crisp outside and soft and chewy inside—the perfect partner for curry. It's rich in carbohydrates and protein, and the yogurt makes the bread easier to digest. It's a little tricky—pay attention to the dough's softness and the heat—but it can be made in a frying pan. With 1 hour of proofing in advance, it takes about 1.5 hours in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 559 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- All-purpose flour (or bread flour)
- Yogurt
- Yeast
- Sugar
- Salt
- Ghee or butter
- Warm water
- Black or white sesame seeds (optional)
- Cilantro (optional)
- Garlic (for the garlic naan version, optional)
- Frying pan (non-stick or cast iron)
- Rolling pin

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion makes 6 naan, enough for 2-3 people.

Per portion:

- All-purpose flour 300g
- Yogurt 60ml
- Dry yeast 3g
- Sugar 10g
- Salt 5g
- Cooking oil 15ml (for the dough)
- Warm water 100ml (about 40°C)
- Ghee or butter 30g (for brushing)
- Black sesame seeds 5g (optional)
- Cilantro 10g (optional)
- Garlic 3 cloves (about 9g), minced (optional, for garlic naan)

## Steps

### Making and Proofing the Dough

1. Dissolve 3g of dry yeast and 10g of sugar in 100ml of warm water (about 40°C; not too hot)
2. Let stand for **5 minutes**, until *fine foam forms on the surface* (showing the yeast is active)
3. In a large bowl, mix 300g of all-purpose flour and 5g of salt
4. Pour in the yeast water, 60ml of yogurt and 15ml of cooking oil
5. Knead by hand into a *smooth, soft* dough (it should be a bit softer than ordinary dough; slightly sticky is normal)
6. Knead for **8-10 minutes**
7. Rub 3ml of oil over the surface of the dough to keep it from drying out
8. Cover the bowl with a damp cloth or plastic wrap
9. Leave it somewhere warm to rise for **at least 1 hour**, until *the dough has roughly doubled in size*

### Dividing and Rolling

1. Take out the risen dough and knead gently to deflate
2. Divide into 6 equal pieces (about 80g each)
3. Take one piece, with 3ml of flour or oil on your hands
4. Roll into a long oval or teardrop shape, about 4-5mm thick
5. For garlic naan: sprinkle the minced garlic and cilantro over the surface and roll gently to press them in
6. You can sprinkle sesame seeds on the surface

### Cooking

**Method 1: frying pan (recommended)**

1. Heat the frying pan (cast iron is best) over high heat until *very hot* (a drop of water evaporates instantly)
2. Brush one side of the naan with a thin layer of water
3. Place it wet side down in the pan
4. Cook for **1-2 minutes**, until *golden brown spots form on the bottom and the naan starts to bubble*
5. Flip and cook for another **1-2 minutes**
6. If you have a gas stove: after flipping, you can pick up the naan with tongs and hold it directly over the open flame for **a few seconds**; it will puff up quickly
7. Brush with ghee or butter immediately after taking it out

**Method 2: oven**

8. Preheat the oven to its highest temperature (usually 250°C)
9. Put the naan on a baking tray
10. Bake for **3-5 minutes**, until *golden and puffed*
11. Brush with ghee or butter after taking it out

### Serving

1. Eat while hot
2. Goes well with any Indian curry, such as butter chicken or rajma (red kidney bean curry)

## Additional Notes

- The softness of the dough is key; dough that's too firm makes dry, hard naan. The dough should be moist and slightly sticky.
- A cast iron pan works better than non-stick; it gets hotter and gives better charred spots.
- You must brush with ghee or butter immediately after cooking; this is key to naan's aroma and texture.
- Besides plain naan, common variations include garlic naan, cheese naan and butter naan.
- Naan has a history of hundreds of years in India; the earliest record dates to 1300, and it's thought to have come from Persian culture.
