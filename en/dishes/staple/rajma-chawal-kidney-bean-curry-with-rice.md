# How to Make Rajma Chawal (Kidney Bean Curry with Rice)

This North Indian home-style dish is a traditional favorite in Punjab and Delhi: a rich, spicy tomato-onion gravy with red kidney beans braised until soft and flavorful, mixed with hot rice for a warm, filling meal. Beans are rich in protein, dietary fiber and various trace elements for balanced nutrition. It's simple, and beginners can make it easily; it takes about 40 minutes in total, but the dried beans need to soak for 8 hours in advance. A rich, home-style flavor that tastes like mom's cooking.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 1842 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Red kidney beans (rajma)
- Basmati rice or regular rice
- Onions
- Tomatoes
- Ginger
- Garlic
- Green chili
- Cilantro
- Cooking oil or ghee
- Turmeric powder
- Red chili powder
- Ground coriander
- Ground cumin
- Garam masala
- Bay leaves
- Salt
- Pressure cooker (recommended) or ordinary stockpot

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 2-3 people.

Per portion:

**Kidney bean curry:**

- Dried red kidney beans 200g (soaked 8 hours in advance)
- Onions 2 (about 200g), chopped
- Tomatoes 3 (about 300g), chopped (or 30ml of tomato paste)
- Ginger 10g, grated
- Garlic 4 cloves (about 12g), grated
- Green chili 1, slit
- Cooking oil 30ml
- Bay leaves 2
- Turmeric powder 3g
- Red chili powder 5g
- Ground coriander 5g
- Ground cumin 3g
- Garam masala 3g
- Salt 6g
- Water 600ml
- Cilantro 15g (for garnish)

**Rice:**

- Rice 200g
- Water 400ml

## Steps

### Prep (8 Hours Ahead)

1. Soak the dried kidney beans in water for at least 8 hours (overnight); they'll roughly double in volume

### Cooking the Kidney Beans

1. Drain the soaked kidney beans
2. **Pressure cooker**: add the beans and 600ml of water, close the lid, bring to pressure on high heat, then turn to medium and **cook for 15-20 minutes**, until *the beans can be easily crushed between your fingers*
3. **Ordinary pot**: add the beans and plenty of water, bring to a boil on high heat, then turn to medium and **cook for 60-90 minutes**, until the beans are completely soft
4. Keep the bean cooking water and set aside

### Making the Curry

1. Pour 30ml of cooking oil into the wok and heat over medium heat
2. Add 2 bay leaves and fry until *fragrant* (about 10 seconds)
3. Add the chopped onions and stir-fry until *golden brown* (about 6-8 minutes)
4. Add the grated ginger and garlic and stir-fry for 1 minute
5. Add the chopped tomatoes and stir-fry until *the tomatoes are completely broken down and the oil separates* (about 5 minutes)
6. Add 3g of turmeric, 5g of red chili powder, 5g of ground coriander, 3g of ground cumin and 6g of salt
7. Stir-fry for 2 minutes so the spices blend with the tomatoes
8. Pour in the cooked kidney beans together with their cooking water
9. Add the green chili and stir well
10. Bring to a boil on high heat, then turn to low and **simmer for 15-20 minutes**
11. Meanwhile, mash some of the beans with the back of the spatula to make the gravy *thick*
12. Add 3g of garam masala and stir well
13. Turn off the heat and sprinkle with cilantro

### Cooking the Rice

1. Rinse the rice
2. Put it in the rice cooker with 400ml of water
3. Cook as usual

### Serving

1. Put the rice on a plate
2. Pour the kidney bean curry over or beside the rice
3. Serve with onion salad and lemon wedges

## Additional Notes

- Red kidney beans must be cooked thoroughly before eating; undercooked kidney beans contain phytohaemagglutinin, which can cause food poisoning.
- In India, rajma chawal is considered the ultimate taste of home, similar in status to "red-braised pork with rice" in China.
- For a richer taste, add 30ml of cream.
- Leftover kidney bean curry keeps for 3-4 days in the refrigerator and tastes even better.
