# How to Make Chicken Biryani

Biryani is a royal dish dating back to the Mughal Empire: long, slender grains with a rich spice aroma, tender chicken, and fluffy separate grains of rice in rich layers. It's rich in protein and the essence of many spices, which stimulate the appetite. The steps are fairly laborious and not very beginner-friendly. It takes about 90 minutes in total (the chicken needs to be marinated 1 hour in advance).

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★★

Estimated calories: 730 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Basmati rice
- Chicken thighs (boneless; lamb can be used instead)
- Yogurt
- Onions
- Tomato
- Ginger
- Garlic
- Green chilies
- Cilantro
- Mint leaves
- Saffron — can be replaced with yellow food coloring
- Milk
- Ghee
- Cooking oil
- Turmeric powder
- Red chili powder
- Garam masala
- Whole spices: bay leaves, cinnamon stick, cardamom, cloves, star anise
- Salt
- Heavy-bottomed pot (with lid, for sealing)
- Dough (for sealing the lid)

## Calculation

Before cooking, decide how many portions to make. One portion is just enough for 3-4 people.

Per portion:

**Marinade:**

- Chicken thighs 500g (boneless, cut into large pieces)
- Yogurt 100ml
- Ginger 15g, grated
- Garlic 6 cloves (about 18g), grated
- Red chili powder 5g
- Turmeric powder 3g
- Garam masala 5g
- Salt 5g
- Lemon juice 15ml

**Rice:**

- Basmati rice 300g (soaked 30 minutes in advance)
- Water 2000ml (for boiling the rice)
- Bay leaves 2
- Cardamom pods 3
- Cloves 4
- Cinnamon stick 1 (about 5cm)
- Star anise 1
- Salt 10g

**Assembly:**

- Onions 3 (about 300g), thinly sliced
- Cooking oil 50ml (for frying the onions)
- Ghee 30g
- Saffron 0.5g + warm milk 30ml (for soaking)
- Mint leaves 20g
- Cilantro 20g
- Green chilies 3, slit

## Steps

### Marinating the Chicken (1 Hour Ahead)

1. In a large bowl, mix the yogurt, grated ginger and garlic, red chili powder, turmeric, garam masala, salt and lemon juice
2. Add the chicken pieces and coat evenly in the marinade
3. Cover with plastic wrap and marinate in the refrigerator for **at least 1 hour** (overnight is even better)

### Preparing the Saffron Milk and Fried Onions

1. Soak 0.5g of saffron in 30ml of warm milk for **15 minutes** and set aside
2. Pour 50ml of cooking oil into a pan and heat over medium heat
3. Add the sliced onions and stir-fry until *deep golden brown and crispy* (about 12-15 minutes; be patient)
4. Take out the fried onions, drain on paper towels, and set aside

### Par-cooking the Rice

1. Drain the soaked basmati rice
2. Bring 2000ml of water to a boil in a large pot
3. Add the bay leaves, cardamom, cloves, cinnamon stick, star anise and 10g of salt
4. Add the rice and boil on high heat for **5-6 minutes**, until *the grains are about 70% cooked* (soft outside but still firm in the center)
5. Drain immediately, discard the whole spices, and set aside

### Assembling and Steaming

1. Brush the bottom of the heavy-bottomed pot with 15g of ghee
2. First layer: spread the marinated chicken evenly over the bottom of the pot
3. Second layer: spread half of the par-cooked rice on top
4. Sprinkle with half of the fried onions, mint leaves, cilantro and green chilies
5. Third layer: spread the remaining rice on top
6. Sprinkle with the remaining fried onions, mint leaves and cilantro
7. Drizzle the saffron milk evenly over the top layer of rice
8. Dot with the remaining 15g of ghee (cut into small pieces)
9. **Seal the lid**: make a rope of dough from flour and water and press it around the rim to seal the gap (the traditional method), or wrap the rim with foil and then put on the lid
10. First heat on high for **3 minutes**, until steam starts forming inside
11. Then turn to the lowest heat and **steam for 25-30 minutes**
12. After turning off the heat, **don't open the lid**; let it rest for another **5 minutes**
13. Open the lid and gently fold from the bottom with a large spatula to mix the layers
14. Serve with yogurt (raita)

## Additional Notes

- Basmati rice is essential for biryani; ordinary rice can't replace its unique long-grain aroma. It's available on major Chinese e-commerce platforms.
- Sealed steaming (dum) is the essence of this dish; steam must not escape, or the rice will dry out.
- Only cook the rice to 70%, because it keeps absorbing water and cooking during steaming. Overcooking makes the final rice mushy.
- The two main schools of biryani: Hyderabadi style steams raw meat on the bottom, while Lucknowi style cooks the meat first before assembling with the rice. This recipe uses the most common hybrid method.
- In India this dish is a must at weddings and festivals, and is hailed as "India's national dish".
