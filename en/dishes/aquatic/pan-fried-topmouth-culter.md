# How to Make Pan-Fried Topmouth Culter

![Pan-fried topmouth culter](../../../dishes/aquatic/香煎翘嘴鱼/香煎翘嘴鱼.jpeg)

Pan-fried topmouth culter is a richly flavored home-style river fish dish. After salting and air-drying, the fish becomes dry, fragrant and firm; it's fried until the skin is golden and crisp, and the meat is delicate and savory, with a mild heat and sweet finish from bean paste and green chilies. Fish is rich in high-quality protein and unsaturated fatty acids, which are easily absorbed. It needs two to four days of salting and drying in advance, and about half an hour of cooking. Frying the fish requires patience with the heat, which is a bit of a challenge for beginners, but the finished dish smells amazing and goes especially well with rice.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 820 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Topmouth culter (a predatory fish with delicate meat and great texture)
- Ginger
- Green onion
- Garlic
- Green chili peppers
- Cilantro
- Dark soy sauce
- White sugar
- Doubanjiang (chili bean paste)
- Cooking wine
- Light soy sauce
- Salt

## Calculation

Note: these amounts serve about 2-3 people

Per portion:

- Topmouth culter: 1kg (2 jin) is best
- Minced ginger: 20g
- Green onion: half a stalk (50 grams)
- Garlic: 4 cloves
- Cilantro: to taste
- Dark soy sauce: 2ml (leave it out if you don't like strong flavors)
- White sugar: 10g
- Dried chili peppers: 4-6 (to taste)
- Cooking wine: 100ml
- Light soy sauce: 4ml
- Salt: about 50g for salting the fish
- Cooking oil: 100ml

## Steps

1. Have the fish killed and opened along the back (ask the fishmonger to do it; never cut it open from the belly—remember, open it along the back), then rinse clean
2. Rub salt evenly over the surface of the fish, pour on about 80ml of cooking wine and 20g of minced ginger, and put it in the refrigerator (not the freezer) to cure for 1-2 days
3. Take out the cured fish, hang it up with string and air-dry it until half dry (about 1-2 days; the exact time depends on temperature and sunlight)
4. Before cooking, rinse the fish with water and drain well (to prevent water spattering in the oil)
5. Heat the wok on high, then quickly turn to low heat. Add oil, keeping as much of the wok surface coated as possible, and slide the fish in along the side of the wok (fry the back side first)
6. After the fish goes in (and after flipping), don't rush to move it (the skin tears easily at this point). After frying for about 30 seconds, try shaking the wok
7. After frying the back side for about 1 minute, flip and fry for about 1-2 minutes, until both sides are golden
8. When both sides are done, push the fish toward the side of the wok a bit to leave space, add the doubanjiang and fry until fragrant, then add the ginger and garlic
9. Once the aromatics are fragrant, add the cooking wine, light soy sauce and dark soy sauce, then pour in hot water to the same level as the fish or a little less
10. Turn to medium-high heat and cook for 5-10 minutes, then add the green chili segments, white sugar, chicken bouillon powder, thirteen-spice powder and aged vinegar
11. Turn to low heat for 2-5 minutes, add the green onion and cilantro, and serve

## Additional Notes

- Remember to have the fish opened along the back. When curing, just rub salt evenly over the surface; 1-2 days each for curing and drying is enough
- Fry the fish over low heat the whole time, and don't move it right after it goes in, or the skin will tear
- After adding the green chili segments, keep an eye on how cooked they are; the chilies are delicious. Add the cilantro last
- Pay attention to switching the heat, to the flavor-boosting effect of the doubanjiang and white sugar, and to the aroma-boosting effect of the aged vinegar
