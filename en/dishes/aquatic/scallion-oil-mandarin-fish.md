# How to Make Scallion Oil Mandarin Fish

Scallion oil mandarin fish is a home-style steamed dish with a strong scallion aroma and fresh, tender fish. It has a light Chinese flavor that suits people from everywhere. Mandarin fish is rich in high-quality protein and unsaturated fatty acids, and careful removal of fishiness preserves the fish's flavor and nutrition. The method is very beginner-friendly and forgiving; follow the steps and it's basically foolproof. The whole process takes about 45 minutes from prep to plate.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 311 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Mandarin fish (Chinese perch)
- Green onion
- Bird's eye chili
- Ginger
- Cooking wine
- Vegetable oil
- Salt
- Seasoned soy sauce for fish
- Steamer basket (with steamer pot)
- Water
- Cutting board
- Iron wok
- Plastic plate or basin (for marinating the fish)
- Disposable gloves
- Paper towels
- Plate for steaming fish (just big enough to lay a fish flat)
- Kitchen knife
- Peeler
- Hot plate gripper (or heat-resistant gloves)

## Calculation

The recipe is written for 2 portions. In practice, since the texture and flavor of the fish vary with the size of the individual fish, it's recommended to cook 2 portions at a time for the best flavor.

- Mandarin fish = 500g (1 jin)
- Green onion = 1 stalk (30cm long)
- Bird's eye chili = 2
- Ginger = 50g
- Cooking wine = 25g
- Vegetable oil = 15g
- Salt = 8g
- Seasoned soy sauce for fish = 10g
- Water = 5L

## Steps

1. Buy a fish from the market that has already been cleaned (if you do it yourself, discard the innards). Scrape all the scales off the surface of the fish
2. Use paper towels to wipe away the blood along the bone and the black membrane inside the belly (the blood along the bone affects the taste, and the black membrane is the source of fishy smell)
3. Scrape the surface of the fish back and forth with a kitchen knife several times to remove the slime and further reduce fishiness, then rinse the fish inside and out with water
4. Lay the fish flat on the cutting board and pat it dry inside and out with paper towels. With the head to the left and tail to the right, starting from the gills, make a cut every 3cm across the fish, deep enough to reach the spine. Do the same on the other side
5. Lay the fish flat in the basin, making sure there's no excess water in it
6. Take a 50g piece of ginger (about the size of an egg), peel it with the peeler, rinse, and cut into 3mm thick slices
7. Wash the bird's eye chilies, remove the stems, and cut into 2mm thick rings (or 1mm wide shreds)
8. Wash the green onion, remove the roots, and cut into 3cm segments. Slightly thicker green onions can be split down the middle lengthwise
9. Add 8g of salt and 25g of cooking wine to the basin, put on disposable gloves, and massage the whole fish for 1 minute, making sure every part is evenly coated with salt and wine
10. After massaging, tuck a slice of ginger into each cut and put 3 slices of ginger inside the belly. Marinate for 10 minutes (don't marinate too long, or the fish loses freshness)
11. While the fish marinates, add 5L of water to the steamer pot, bring it to a boil, then place the steamer basket on top
12. After marinating, the fish will release liquid. Pour off the excess liquid along with the marinating wine and ginger slices, rinse the fish and belly with water, and pat them dry with paper towels
13. Lay the fish flat on the steaming plate, and tuck fresh ginger slices into the cuts on the body and belly
14. Put the steaming plate into the steamer basket, cover, and steam over medium heat for 20 minutes
15. During steaming, steam condenses all over the fish and plate and forms a fish broth. Never pour this broth away after steaming; it is the essence of the flavor
16. Lift the steaming plate out with the gripper, and drizzle 10g of seasoned soy sauce for fish over and around the fish
17. Then sprinkle the green onion segments and chilies evenly over and around the fish
18. Pour 15g of vegetable oil into the wok and heat slowly over medium-low heat for 5 minutes. Don't use high heat, or the oil will evaporate quickly
19. Slowly and evenly pour the hot oil over the fish, and your mouthwateringly fresh scallion oil mandarin fish is ready!

## Additional Notes

After several tests, the success rate is 100%. This dish is very forgiving and suits people from any province with any taste. Also, although this tutorial is called scallion oil mandarin fish, the main ingredient isn't limited to mandarin fish; you can substitute sea fish such as sea bass or turbot (freshwater fish carry somewhat more parasites than sea fish, so freshwater fish aren't recommended; for freshwater fish see tutorials such as "Red-Braised Fish"). Low coupling, high extensibility.
