# How to Make Sweet and Sour Carp

Sweet and sour carp is a classic Shandong dish: crispy outside and tender inside, pleasantly sweet and sour, with the fish fried golden and covered in a glossy red sweet and sour sauce. Fish is rich in high-quality protein and various minerals and is easy to digest. This dish isn't very beginner-friendly; it is moderately hard and requires mastering scoring the fish, battering and frying temperature. It takes about 1 hour from prep to finished dish.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 1011 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Carp
- Ketchup
- White sugar
- White vinegar
- Starch
- Salt
- Scallion
- Ginger
- Cooking wine
- Cilantro, 1 stalk
- Basins (two)
- Kitchen knife, one
- Spider strainer, one; spatula, one

## Calculation

Note: this dish only comes in a version for 3 or more people (fish that are too large or too small don't work), so no formula is needed. It's especially good for family gatherings, such as the Lunar New Year's Eve dinner.

- Carp = about 1.5kg (3 jin)
- Water = 50g
- Ketchup = 40g
- White sugar = 20g
- White vinegar = 10g
- Starch = 10g
- Salt = 30g
- Scallion = 30g (about half a stalk)
- Ginger = 30g
- Cooking wine = 25g

## Steps

1. Clean the fish thoroughly, making sure there are no scales or other debris left
2. Place the fish with its head to the left and belly down, knife in your right hand. Cut straight down 1cm, then hold the fish firmly and slice 3-4cm toward the left, then make one light cut in the middle of that flap
3. Put the fish in a basin. Slice the ginger and cut the scallion into segments (cut however you like; it's mainly for removing the fishy smell)
4. Squeeze the juice from the scallion and ginger into the basin with all your might
5. Add 20g of salt and 25g of cooking wine, then give the carp a good scrub, rubbing it all over evenly
6. ![Marinating](../../../dishes/aquatic/糖醋鲤鱼/腌制.jpg)

    (The longer the fish marinates, the less fishy it tastes. Marinating for over 30 minutes is recommended)

7. In a clean basin, add 100g of flour, 200g of starch, 180g of water and 5g of salt, and mix evenly by hand. The batter should now be thick enough to form strands when pulled. Then crack in an egg and mix again
8. Wait 30 minutes
9. Put the fish on the cutting board and dry it with a dry towel (this helps the batter stick better)
10. Rinse the basin clean and dry it with a dry towel
11. Heat the wok, add about 1L of oil, and heat it to 7/10 heat, about 200-240 degrees
12. Hold the fish by the tail and lower the head into the bottom of the wok. Ladle hot oil over the body with a spoon. Once the batter has set, slowly lower the whole fish into the wok, gently lift the head with the spatula and slide the spider strainer under it, to keep the bottom from burning.
13. Prepare a plate for the fish and put it next to the wok.
14. Gently slide the spatula in under the body, and use both tools together to flip the fish. Fry for another two minutes, then use the same method (spider strainer under the head, spatula under the body) to transfer the fish to the plate
15. Pour the oil from the wok into the dried basin, set it aside, and scrub the wok clean
16. Put 50g of water, 40g of ketchup, 20g of white sugar and 10g of white vinegar in a small bowl and stir evenly
17. In another small bowl, mix 10g of starch with 10g of water to make a starch slurry
18. Heat the wok on high, then pour in the sauce mixture you prepared. Bring to a boil on high heat, then turn to low heat
19. Add the starch slurry, stirring as you pour, then turn off the heat after 20 seconds
20. Spoon the finished sweet and sour sauce evenly over the fish. You can garnish with a little cilantro or chopped green onion. Your sweet and sour carp is done
