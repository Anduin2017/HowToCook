# How to Make Spicy Crayfish

![Spicy crayfish - preview image 1](../../../dishes/aquatic/小龙虾/成品.jpg)

This dish is numbing, spicy, savory and fragrant, with tender, juicy crayfish meat. It is a classic Sichuan-Hunan style dish to go with drinks, and making it at home is clean and affordable. Crayfish is rich in high-quality protein and various trace elements, but the sauce is high in oil and salt, so it is best enjoyed occasionally. Handling live crayfish and many spices is a bit of a challenge for beginners, though skipping deveining makes it much easier. It takes about 45 minutes from prep to plate.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★★

Estimated calories: 571 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Crayfish
- Oil
- Bay leaves
- Star anise
- Cassia bark
- Green Sichuan peppercorns
- Sichuan peppercorns
- Bullet chilies (zidantou)
- Scallion, ginger, garlic
- Pixian doubanjiang (chili bean paste)
- Soybean paste
- Beer
- Light soy sauce
- Salt

## Calculation

The amounts below are for 1kg of crayfish; scale proportionally.

- Crayfish = 1kg (2 jin)
- Oil = 70 ml (3 times the usual amount for a stir-fry)
- Bay leaves = 2
- Star anise = 1
- Cassia bark = 3 g
- Green Sichuan peppercorns = 10 g
- Sichuan peppercorns = 10 g
- Bullet chilies = 5 g
- Scallion = 1 large scallion (Chinese leek-type)
- Ginger = 30 g
- Garlic = 7 large cloves
- Pixian doubanjiang = 30 g
- Soybean paste = 30 g
- Beer = 500 ml
- Light soy sauce = 30 ml
- Salt = 10 g

## Steps

1. Scrub the crayfish clean and remove the veins. Cut the scallion into 2cm segments and mince the ginger and garlic.
2. Heat the oil. When it is slightly hot, add the bay leaves, star anise, cassia bark, green Sichuan peppercorns, Sichuan peppercorns and bullet chilies.
3. Once the spices are fragrant, add the scallion, ginger and garlic
4. Once the scallion, ginger and garlic are fragrant, add the Pixian doubanjiang and soybean paste and stir-fry until the oil turns red.
5. Add the crayfish and stir-fry until they change color.
6. Add the beer. Once the beer boils, add the light soy sauce and salt.
7. Cook until the crayfish are fully cooked, then serve.

## Additional Notes

Restaurants usually deep-fry the crayfish first, but deep-frying at home wastes too much oil. So this recipe uses more oil than a normal stir-fry to pan-fry them instead, and in practice it tastes just as good.

Deveined crayfish meat has a slightly worse texture than un-deveined meat, and deveining crayfish is a challenge for beginners. If you don't mind the vein, you can skip deveining.
