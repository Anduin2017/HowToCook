# How to Make Tea Eggs

This traditional snack has a rich tea aroma and a savory, delicious flavor. It's rich in high-quality protein, making it a quick, nutritious breakfast choice. The steps are clear and the difficulty moderate; beginners should pay attention to the heat and spice ratios. It takes about 30 minutes and makes enough for 2-3 people in one batch, good to prepare at a leisurely pace on the weekend.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 193 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Eggs
- Star anise
- Bay leaves
- Cassia bark
- Fennel seeds
- Rock sugar
- Black tea
- Light soy sauce
- Dark soy sauce
- Salt

## Calculation

- Eggs 400g (about 8)
- Star anise 4g (about 2)
- Bay leaves 0.5-1g (about 2)
- Cassia bark 3g (1 small piece)
- Fennel seeds 5g
- Rock sugar 15g
- Black tea 20g
- Light soy sauce 15g
- Dark soy sauce 25g
- Salt 3g

Use the above to calculate the proportions of ingredients you plan to use.

## Steps

1. Put the eggs in cold water and boil until cooked, about 8 minutes on high heat (depending on your cookware)
2. Remove the eggs and rinse them in cold water
3. Knock the eggs against each other so each one has cracks
4. Put the eggs back in the pot and add the star anise, bay leaves, cassia bark, fennel seeds, rock sugar, black tea, light soy sauce, dark soy sauce and salt
5. Add water until the eggs are covered
6. Bring to a boil on high heat, then turn to medium-low and simmer for 15 minutes

> After simmering for 15 minutes, remove the spices and tea leaves; letting the eggs soak a while longer improves the flavor

## Additional Notes

- Rinsing the eggs in cold water creates a gap between the egg and the shell
- You can leave out the salt depending on your taste
- If you don't have all of star anise, bay leaves, cassia bark, fennel and rock sugar, you can buy a ready-made braising spice packet
- With the usual method, the braising liquid soaks in slowly and the inside tends to taste bland. For faster flavor and a nicer cross-section: **peel the eggs** → **make 3–11 cuts** in the sides of the whites (in a cross pattern top to bottom, close to the yolk but not too deep so the yolk doesn't spill out) → put them in a small container with the braising liquid **covering the eggs** → soak for **at least 2 days**
