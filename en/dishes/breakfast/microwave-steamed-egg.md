# How to Make Microwave Steamed Egg

A tender, smooth steamed egg made quickly in the microwave, as silky as pudding. It's a home-style quick dish. Rich in high-quality protein and easy to digest, it's great for breakfast or a light meal. It's very simple; beginners can handle it easily, and it takes about 10 minutes in total.

Estimated difficulty: ★

Estimated calories: 151 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

- Eggs
- Warm water or stock
- Salt
- Light soy sauce (optional)
- Sesame oil
- Heat-resistant bowl
- Plastic wrap or a microwave cover

## Calculation

- Eggs 2 (about 100g of beaten egg)
- Warm water or stock 100-120ml (1.0-1.2 times the volume of the egg)
- Salt 1g
- Light soy sauce 2ml (optional)
- Sesame oil, a few drops (for finishing)

Use the above to calculate the proportions of ingredients you plan to use.

## Steps

1. Beat the eggs, add the warm water/stock, salt and light soy sauce, and stir gently, trying not to create bubbles.
2. Strain the egg mixture into the heat-resistant bowl. If there are bubbles on the surface, gently pop them with a toothpick.
3. Cover with plastic wrap and poke 8-10 small holes, or use a microwave cover (leaving a gap).
4. Heat in the microwave:

  - 700W: 1 minute 30 seconds → depending on how it looks, heat another 20-30 seconds until the surface has just set.
  - 600W: about 1 minute 40 seconds – 2 minutes 10 seconds.
  - 800W: about 1 minute 10 seconds – 1 minute 40 seconds.

5. When done, take it out and let it rest for 1 minute so the residual heat fully cooks the center.
6. Drizzle with sesame oil, sprinkle with chopped green onion, and serve.

> Different power levels and containers affect the time. On your first try, heat in short bursts several times to find the best time for your microwave.

## Additional Notes

- The water should be 40–50℃, not too hot.
- An egg-to-water ratio between 1:1 and 1:1.2 gives the best texture.
- Covering with plastic wrap and poking holes prevents the surface from bursting or getting honeycombed.
- Straining significantly improves smoothness.
- If the surface puffs up or water seeps out, it was overheated; just shorten the time next time.
