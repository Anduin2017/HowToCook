# How to Make Onsen Tamago

Onsen tamago (hot spring egg) comes from Japan. The white is a semi-liquid curd, while the yolk is warm and set, giving a silky, delicate texture—exactly the opposite of a soft-boiled egg. Eggs are rich in high-quality protein, lecithin and various vitamins, making them very nutritious. You need a thermometer to keep the water at 70℃; although the temperature matters, the steps are simple, and with about half an hour of patient waiting it's done. Served with kombu soy sauce and chopped green onion, it's fresh and savory, an easy little dish to make.

Estimated difficulty: ★★★

Estimated calories: 72 kcal

## Ingredients and Tools

### Main Ingredient

- Eggs

### Required Tools

- Kitchen thermometer (preferably one with a clip)
- A cup (any cup will do)

### Additional Ingredients

Extra ingredients for garnish, optional

- Kombu soy sauce (a Japanese low-sodium soy sauce used to season onsen tamago)
- Green onion

## Calculation

- Use 1 egg, scaled to your appetite and the size of your pot.

## Steps

### Cooking the Eggs

1. Fill a pot with tap water so that it covers the eggs by about 3cm, and put the thermometer in the water
2. Turn on the stove or induction cooker and gradually adjust the power or flame to keep the water at **70 degrees Celsius**
3. Put the eggs in the pot. The eggs must not be stacked; they should all sit on the bottom with room to move
4. Hold the current temperature for **25 minutes**
5. Prepare a cup of ice water
6. Take out the eggs and immediately put them in the ice water, **waiting 3 minutes**
7. Crack the eggs into a small bowl, and you're done

### Notes

- If using a mechanical thermometer (no power needed), the probe must be submerged at least 6cm in the water. Electronic thermometers have no special requirements
- A slightly thicker pot makes it easier to control the temperature
- Although 70 degrees Celsius for 25 minutes is enough to kill salmonella, still eat these in moderation, or make them with eggs certified safe for raw consumption
